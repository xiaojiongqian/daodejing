# 第014-1章：赞玄——从作用辨认运行

## 原文

### 第1页

视之不见名曰夷，听之不闻名曰希，搏之不得名曰微。

此三者，不可致诘，故混而为一。

其上不皦，其下不昧。绳绳不可名，复归于无物。

白话：看它而见不到形色，称为“夷”；听它而闻不到声音，称为“希”；伸手而握不到实体，称为“微”。三者都超出逐项追问，合起来指向同一个运行。

### 第2页

是谓无状之状，无物之象，是谓惚恍。

迎之不见其首，随之不见其后。

执古之道，以御今之有。能知古始，是谓道纪。

白话：它呈现无形的形态、无物的迹象，来时见不到开端，跟随时也见不到终点。把握古已有之的道，可以处理今天发生的事；由此认识万物的起始线索，称为“道纪”。

## 释义

### 第3页

第十四章从感官边界出发。眼睛辨认形色，耳朵接收声音，手掌确认质地。道作为万物生成与变化的根本运行，超出了单一感官所能框定的对象。

“夷、希、微”因此标记三种抵达边界的经验。人仍可观察事物怎样生长、转化和返回，再从这些作用中辨认更深的秩序。

### 第4页

王弼说，道“无状无象，无声无响”，所以能够通往万物。他随即补上一层：万物由此成形。道超出形体，同时经由形体留下作用。

“无状之状，无物之象”保留了这份张力。读者面对的既是一种感官边界，也是一种推理方法：从结果回看条件，从变化辨认运行。

### 第5页

“执古之道，以御今之有”把章节带入时间。王弼强调，时代和风俗会变化，万物生成的根本仍可为今天提供线索。“古”指向起始与根本，也要求读者检验古老原则如何进入当下事实。

这样的用古包含两项工作：保存长期经验，核对眼前条件。古道提供方向，当下之“有”提供材料，判断在两者接触时形成。

## 术语与考据简注

### 第6页

**赞玄：** 河上公为第十四章所加的章题。“玄”指幽深而难以穷尽的运行，章题赞叹它超出形色，同时成就万物。

**夷、希、微：** 河上公分别以无色、无声、无形解释。三个词从视觉、听觉与触觉指出感官所达的边界。

**不可致诘：** “致诘”含有追问到底之意。经文提醒人，逐项拆解感官属性仍会遗漏整体运行。

### 第7页

**惚恍：** 指难以固定、若隐若现的状态。王弼说“不可得而定”，强调它无法停在一个确定形状中。

**道纪：** “纪”可以指纲纪与线索。河上公将其解释为道的纲纪，王弼则把它连到万物之宗与古今相承。

“畏旧”与“守古”都偏离这里的重点。执古之道要求人带着根本原则处理今天已经发生的事。

## 正文

### 第8页

以下是一则原创框架（fiction）。

一场暴雨过后，沿海城市的旧街区连续积水。市政档案里留着一张百年前的排水图，纸面已经泛黄，几条墨线穿过今天的商场、地铁口与住宅楼。

工程师林汐把旧图铺在会议桌上。屏幕里的新模型显示道路和管网，两个时代的线条只在少数地方重合。

### 第9页

团队先沿街查看。路面已经排干，墙脚仍留着水痕；一处地下室散出潮气，三个井盖旁堆着被水推来的细叶。地面看上去恢复平常，积水经过的路线仍留在物件上。

林汐逐一记录水痕高度、井盖位置和泵站启动时间。她寻找的是流动留下的关系。

### 第10页

旧图上有一条沟渠曾经通向海湾。城市扩建以后，沟渠被覆盖，名称也从地图上消失。地势仍沿原来的方向下降，几条新管道则在它上方交叉。

林汐让测量人员重新核对高程。雨水从新街道汇入低处，又在一段收窄的管口前停住。百年前的墨线提供线索，今天的数据说明阻塞发生在哪里。

### 第11页

会议上有人主张照旧图恢复整条沟渠。林汐把现状图放到旁边：地铁基础已经占据一段旧线，住宅楼也改变了汇水范围。原样恢复会把新的风险带给现有居民。

她提出另一套方案：保留旧沟渠的排水方向，在可施工地段增加调蓄空间，并把狭窄管口移到便于维护的位置。

### 第12页

“执古之道”在这张桌上成为一种工作次序。先问旧系统依照什么条件运行，再问这些条件今天以何种形式存在。墨线提供经验，测量提供校正。

古老原则因此获得当代形态。水仍顺地势汇流，城市则用新的材料、法规与人口条件回应它。

### 第13页

第十四章所说的“视之不见”也有现实对应。人看见井盖，眼睛却收纳不了整张地下管网；人听见泵声，耳朵也只能接收某个地点的运行。

工程判断把这些局部证据接起来。水痕、坡度、流量和时间共同指向一个肉眼难以同时看到的系统。

### 第14页

现代科学常从可见作用推知隐藏条件。医生依据指标判断身体过程，天文学家依据光线研究远方天体，工程师依据振动检查结构。工具扩展感官，推理连接证据。

这种方法与老子的道各有语境。它们在一个认识问题上相遇：直接观看只提供部分事实，运行还要从关系与变化中辨认。

### 第15页

暴雨敲上控制室的窗，屏幕上的水位线逐段升起。数值越过预设位置，调蓄空间随即启用，水位曲线开始回落。监测人员把结果写进记录，林汐接过记录夹，带着旧图走到街面。

雨水沿路缘流向井口，商店门前保持通行。林汐在檐下展开记录夹，将街面的水流与屏幕数据逐项核对。旧图仍夹在其中，今天的雨又为它添了一层记录。

## 回扣

### 第16页

第十四章把认识分成两个层次。感官接触形色、声音与质地；理解则追踪事物怎样生成、连接和变化。夷、希、微标出前一层的边界，道纪引向后一层的线索。

“执古之道”也属于这种追踪。人从长期经验中辨认稳定关系，再用当前事实修正行动。

### 第17页

雨水退入井口，林汐带着记录夹回到桌前，把新水位标在旧图的复印件上。百年前的墨线与今天的蓝线交叉，纸面因此多了一层时间。

水流已经退去，墙脚的痕迹、管网的记录和保持畅通的街道共同说明它曾怎样经过。

道的线索也常以这种方式抵达：形体各自出现，运行贯穿其中。

# Chapter 014-1: Praising the Mysterious—Recognizing Process Through Effects

## Original Text

### Page 1

We look at it, and we do not see it, and we name it “the Equable.” We listen to it, and we do not hear it, and we name it “the Inaudible.” We try to grasp it, and do not get hold of it, and we name it “the Subtle.” With these three qualities, it cannot be made the subject of description; hence we blend them together and obtain the One.

Its upper part is not bright, and its lower part is not obscure. Ceaseless in its action, it cannot be named, and then it again returns and becomes nothing.

### Page 2

This is called the Form of the Formless, and the Semblance of the Invisible; this is called the Fleeting and Indeterminable.

We meet it and do not see its front; we follow it and do not see its back. When we can lay hold of the Dao of old to direct the things of the present day, and know it as it was in the beginning, this is called unwinding the clue of Dao.

## Interpretation

### Page 3

Chapter Fourteen begins at the boundary of the senses. Eyes distinguish shape and color, ears receive sound, and hands confirm texture. Dao, as the generative process through which things arise and change, exceeds any single sensory object.

The Equable, the Inaudible, and the Subtle mark three encounters with that boundary. We can still observe how things grow, transform, and return, then recognize a deeper order through their effects.

### Page 4

Wang Bi says Dao has neither shape nor sound and therefore passes through all things. He immediately adds that things take form through it. Dao exceeds form while leaving effects through forms.

“The form of the formless” preserves this tension. It offers both an account of sensory limits and a method of inquiry: follow results toward conditions and changes toward process.

### Page 5

“Hold to the Dao of old in order to govern what exists today” brings time into the chapter. Wang Bi notes that ages and customs change while the root of generation still offers guidance. The ancient here points toward beginnings and underlying relations.

Using the old requires two forms of attention: preserve long experience and test present conditions. Ancient guidance meets current evidence in the act of judgment.

## Key Terms and Notes

### Page 6

**Praising the Mysterious:** Heshang Gong's title for Chapter Fourteen. The mysterious names a depth that exceeds complete description while giving rise to things.

**Equable, Inaudible, Subtle:** Heshang Gong links them with the absence of visible color, audible sound, and graspable form. Together they mark sensory limits.

**Beyond interrogation:** Ordinary questions can separate qualities one by one. The whole process exceeds that procedure.

### Page 7

**Fleeting and indeterminable:** Wang Bi says it cannot be fixed. Its activity continues through changing forms.

**Clue of Dao:** The word *ji* can mean a governing thread. Heshang Gong reads it as the organizing line of Dao, while Wang Bi connects it with the source of things across past and present.

The line asks readers to bring a root principle into contact with present events.

## Main Text

### Page 8

The following is an original fictional framework.

After a storm, an old coastal district floods across several streets. The municipal archive holds a century-old drainage map. Faded ink lines now pass beneath a shopping center, a subway entrance, and blocks of housing.

An engineer named Lin Xi spreads the old map across a table. A new model glows on the wall. The two systems meet at only a few points.

### Page 9

The team walks the district. The pavement has drained, while tide marks remain at the base of walls. Damp air rises from a basement, and fine leaves gather beside three drain covers. The surface has returned to ordinary use; objects still hold the route of the water.

Lin Xi records mark height, drain location, and pump timing. She is looking for relations left by movement.

### Page 10

The old map shows a channel once leading toward the bay. Later construction covered it and removed its name from maps. The ground still descends along the former direction, while newer pipes cross above it.

Lin Xi asks the survey team to check the elevations again. Rain converges in the low area and gathers before a narrow pipe. The old line supplies a clue; present data locates the constraint.

### Page 11

At the meeting, one proposal calls for restoring the entire historic channel. Lin Xi places the current map beside it. Subway foundations occupy one section, and housing has changed the catchment.

Her alternative preserves the channel's drainage direction, adds storage where construction is feasible, and relocates the narrow connection to a place that crews can maintain.

### Page 12

“Holding to the ancient Dao” becomes an order of work at this table. First ask what conditions allowed the old system to function. Then identify the form those conditions take today. Ink carries experience; measurement supplies correction.

An old principle receives a present form. Water still follows terrain, while a city responds through current materials, law, and population.

### Page 13

The chapter's sensory limits also appear in ordinary engineering. A person sees one drain cover, while the buried network extends across the district. A pump can be heard from one corner, while the system acts across many locations.

Judgment connects partial evidence. Tide marks, slope, flow, and time point toward a system that the eye cannot hold all at once.

### Page 14

Modern science often infers hidden conditions from visible effects. Physicians use measurements to study bodily processes, astronomers use light to study distant objects, and engineers use vibration to inspect structures. Instruments extend the senses, and reasoning connects evidence.

These practices and Laozi's Dao belong to different contexts. They meet at one question of knowledge: direct perception supplies part of the fact, while relations and changes disclose process.

### Page 15

Rain strikes the control-room windows, and water levels rise section by section across the screen. A value crosses its set point, the storage area opens, and the curves begin to recede. Staff enter the result in the log. Lin Xi takes the folder, places the old map inside, and walks above ground.

Rain moves along the curb and enters the drains. Shop entrances remain passable. Under an awning, Lin Xi opens the folder and checks the street flow against the screen data. The old map remains inside, now joined by another record from today’s rain.

## Return to the Original

### Page 16

Chapter Fourteen distinguishes two levels of knowing. The senses meet color, sound, and texture. Understanding follows the way things arise, connect, and change. The three terms mark the boundary of the first level; the clue of Dao opens the second.

Holding to the ancient Dao belongs to the same pursuit. Long experience reveals durable relations, and current facts correct action.

### Page 17

As the last water enters the drains, Lin Xi carries the folder back to the table and marks the new levels on a copy of the old plan. Century-old ink crosses today’s blue line, adding another layer of time to the page.

The water has withdrawn. Marks on walls, records in the network, and open streets together show how it passed.

The clue of Dao often arrives this way: forms appear separately, while process runs through them.
