# 第010-3章：不知——不要把空白读成答案

## 原文

### 第1页

爱国治民，能无知乎？

白话：爱护国家、治理人民，能够不倚仗自以为无所不知的聪明，不用智巧替所有人预先决定一切吗？

这里的“无知”不是昏聩，也不是把知识从治理中赶走。它首先提出一道限制：一个人即使肩负责任、掌握资料，也不可把有限的所知冒充成没有边界的全知。

### 第2页

“知”与“不知”在《道德经》中反复出现，却不是一道可以一次算完的题。

老子赞许知常、自知、知足、知止，也警惕急于炫示、分类、操控的智巧；他有时说“吾不知”，让语言停在证据的边界。不同章句各有语境，需要分别理解。

我们在这里先抓住其中一条线：真正的知，为什么必须在自己内部保留“不知”的位置？

## 深入释义

### 第3页

理解“不知”有两种相反的误区。

第一种把它读成拒绝学习，仿佛资料越少、判断越含混，便越接近“道”。但没有事实的谦逊，很容易只是无能；不肯核验的从容，也可能让别人承担代价。

第二种误区则认为，只要知识足够多，所有未知终会被消灭。于是现实像模型一样确定，真理像职位一样可靠，经验丰富的人也渐渐失去说“我还不知道”的能力。

老子所警惕的，更接近第二种危险，却绝不因此赞美第一种。

### 第4页

“知”首先有朴素而坚实的一面：看见事实，辨认规律，记住经验，并让判断接受结果的检验。

一位医生需要知道症状与风险，一位工程师需要知道材料和水位，一位治理者需要知道人的生活并不等同于报表。知识使行动免于盲目，也使责任不必只靠善意。

但真知还有一道不那么显眼的工作：分清我亲眼或可靠地知道了什么，我根据迹象推测了什么，又有什么仍没有得到回答。

### 第5页

因此，“不知”是知识画出的边界线，它标明判断可以抵达的位置。

边界以内，判断可以有根据；边界以外，名称、模型和经验仍可暂时指路，却不能假装已经取代了事物本身。地图上的空白不是一块已经探明的平地，仪表上的横线也不是数值零。

承认“不知”，并没有提供答案。它做的是更早也更重要的一件事：不让一个尚未得到的答案，偷偷穿上事实的衣服。

### 第6页

这就是“不知”在第十章进入治理问题时的重要性。

个人判断错误，代价也许只落在自己身上；权力把推测说成事实，错误便会沿着命令、预算和制度传到许多人那里。行动范围越大，越需要知道自己的知识在哪一处中断。

这并不要求治理者永远等待。洪水、疾病和危险不会等到信息完美才发生。它要求的是：在必须行动时，把未知也列入行动条件，使决策能够核验、修正，并给现实留下返回的通道。

### 第7页

**“无知”与“不知”：** 第十章王弼本作“爱国治民，能无知乎”；河上公本把相关文字次序置于别处。这里用“不知”作为较宽的讨论入口，但不把所有含“知”的章句视为同一个固定术语。

**知与智巧：** 《道德经》并不一概否定认识、经验和分辨。它反复限制的，是知识变成自炫的身份、控制的技术，或不再接受现实修正的结论。

**暂留的三扇门：** 后文还会出现“不知其名”“为学日益，为道日损”与“知不知”。它们将把命名、学习和认知边界推得更深。本篇只把门标出来，不提前走完门后的道路。

## 画面描述

### 第8页

以下是一则原创画面叙事（fiction）。

台风掠过山地的深夜，一座流域防汛控制室仍亮着白光。大屏幕上，雨云正向下游移动，河道被画成一条条发亮的细线。多数水位数字不断跳动，唯独一条上游支流旁，只剩一枚短横线。

年轻值班员看了一眼：“那里是零，可以把巡查车调去下游，也不必封闭沿河通道。”调度员的手已经移向确认键。

总工程师说：“停一下。横线不是零。”

“那它是什么？”

“它只说明传感器没有回答。”

她拿起白板笔，把屏幕旁的空白分成三栏：**事实、推论、未知。**

## 正文

### 第9页

事实栏写着：上游连续强降雨；下游水位仍在上升；该测站十七分钟前失联。

推论栏写着：洪峰可能尚未通过；雷电可能损坏了通信；也可能只是局部停电。

未知栏只有一句：该支流此刻的实际水位。

总工程师没有把“不知”当成停工的理由。她要求联系附近村镇，派出离测站最近的巡查人员，并调用不依赖同一通信线路的备用观测；在新消息到达以前，沿河通道暂时关闭，巡查车不撤，关键泄洪余量继续保留。

半小时后，现场回报：水已经接近警戒线。屏幕上的短横线没有预言洪水，却阻止了控制室把沉默误报成平安。

### 第10页

现代系统善于制造一种平整的确信。地图有颜色，模型有小数，仪表有刻度，报告有结论。整齐的界面很容易让人忘记：每一个数字都有来源、时间和误差，每一处空白也都有原因。

“没有数据”与“数据为零”只差一个界面符号，在行动中却可能相隔甚远。没有投诉，不等于没有伤害；没有被记录的病人，不等于没有疾病；没有反对意见，也不等于所有人赞同。

不知的重要，常常就在这个小小的停顿里显现：先问沉默意味着什么，再决定它能够证明什么。

### 第11页

把信息分成“事实、推论、未知”，可以让偏见显出所在，也使句子接受证据检查。

事实是已有可靠依据的部分；推论是根据事实作出的暂时解释；未知则是当前证据尚不能承载的空位。三者都会变化：新的测量可以把未知变成事实，也可以推翻昨日最合理的推论。

成熟的判断不只在结论旁写下信心，还写下条件：我为什么这样认为？什么新事实会使我改变决定？如果判断错了，能否及时回转？

### 第12页

在不确定中行动，不等于把每一步都拖延到万无一失。那同样可能是一种逃避。

防汛人员仍要关路，医生仍要处理紧急风险，管理者仍要在资料不全时分配资源。区别在于，他们是否把推测坦白为推测，是否优先寻找最接近现场的信息，是否为不同意见留门，并尽量避免一开始就作出无法挽回的选择。

“不知”把风险留在仪表盘上，提醒行动者前方仍有待查部分。速度、余量与复查方式也要据此调整。

### 第13页

专业的人会准确说出知识抵达何处，也会说明仍待查证的部分。

初学者可能看不见风险；半懂的人容易用一个熟悉模式解释所有现象；经验深的人则知道，同样的表面可以由不同原因造成，某项检查能够排除什么，又不能排除什么。

因此，专业知识与“不知”彼此校正。知识越可靠，边界通常越清楚。脱离条件的确定性值得警惕：它要求别人服从，同时拒绝说明误差可能出现的位置。

### 第14页

同样，“治民无知”不能被用来替愚民、隐瞒或反智辩护。

治理者若把教育和信息留给少数人，知识便会变成权力。机构若长期隐去依据，并压住受影响者的事实陈述，依赖便会增长。

第十章的问题首先朝向掌权者：“你能不能不以自己的知来宰制？”责任落在掌权者身上：他需要承认，知识分散在现场、社区和不同专业中，自己的座位只能接住其中一部分。

### 第15页

“不知”之所以在《道德经》中重要，还因为“道”从来不是一件可以被最后一张标签完全装下的物品。

老子仍然使用语言，仍然辨认、比较、劝诫，也仍然给出“道”这个字。可他让某些句子停在“吾不知”旁边，仿佛在名称的边缘留下一小块没有涂满的纸。

那块空白究竟意味着什么？它与学习中不断增加的知识是什么关系？为什么承认不知，后来会被说成一种更高的知？这些问题将在后文逐一回来。此刻可以先看见：老子的思想从答案停止冒充整体的地方开始。

## 回扣

### 第16页

所以，“知”与“不知”不是要求我们二选一。

知，使人尊重事实、经验与规律；不知，使人不把局部事实扩张成全部现实。知给行动以根据；不知给行动以分寸。知可以破除蒙昧；不知可以阻止知识变成傲慢、智巧和无边界的控制。

真正的困难出现在责任压来、众人等待、屏幕要求立即确认之时：一个人仍把未知如实留在判断中。

### 第17页

防汛控制室的屏幕重新亮起时，那枚短横线终于变成了一个数字。

总工程师却没有擦掉白板上的“未知”。她只在那一栏旁边写下时间，又留出下一行。因为每一个答案都有抵达的时刻，也有失效的时刻；诚实的判断从不假装自己已经结束。

不要把空白读成零，也不要急着把空白填满。

有时，正是被清楚保留下来的空处，使知识仍能呼吸，使权力仍可回转，也使我们尚有可能看见下一件来到眼前的事。

# Chapter 010-3: Not Knowing—Do Not Read a Blank as an Answer

## Original Text

### Page 1

In caring for the state and governing the people, can you do without knowing?

In plain language: can those entrusted with public responsibility refrain from treating their own intelligence as omniscience, or from using cleverness to decide everything in advance for everyone else?

“Without knowing” does not praise incompetence or drive knowledge out of government. It first places a limit upon power: even a responsible person with substantial information must not disguise partial knowledge as knowledge without boundaries.

### Page 2

Knowing and not knowing recur throughout the *Dao De Jing*, but they do not form a problem that can be solved once and closed.

Laozi praises knowing constancy, oneself, sufficiency, and when to stop. He also distrusts cleverness used for display, classification, and control. At times he says, “I do not know,” not to abandon inquiry, but to stop language from outrunning what it can support. These passages arise in different contexts and should not be compressed into one rigid definition.

Here we follow one thread only: why must genuine knowledge preserve a place for not knowing within itself?

## Deeper Interpretation

### Page 3

Two opposite mistakes surround “not knowing.”

The first treats it as a refusal to learn, as if fewer facts and blurrier judgments brought us nearer to the Dao. Yet humility without evidence may be mere incapacity. Serenity that refuses verification may leave other people to bear the cost.

The second assumes that enough information will eventually abolish the unknown. A model begins to look as certain as reality, official authority begins to resemble truth, and experienced people lose the ability to say, “I do not yet know.”

Laozi's warning is closer to the second danger, but he does not therefore celebrate the first.

### Page 4

Knowledge has a plain and durable work: to see facts, recognize patterns, remember experience, and allow judgment to be tested by results.

A physician needs knowledge of symptoms and risk. An engineer needs knowledge of materials and water levels. A public official needs to know that human lives are not identical with the reports written about them. Knowledge keeps action from blindness and prevents responsibility from relying upon good intentions alone.

Genuine knowledge has another, quieter task: to distinguish what I know through sound evidence, what I infer from signs, and what remains unanswered.

### Page 5

Not knowing is therefore not darkness outside knowledge. It is the boundary knowledge draws around itself.

Within that boundary, judgment may have grounds. Beyond it, names, models, and experience can still guide provisionally, but they cannot pretend to have replaced the thing itself. A blank on a map is not a plain already surveyed. A dash on an instrument is not the number zero.

Admitting uncertainty does not supply an answer. It performs an earlier and more important task: it prevents an answer we do not possess from quietly dressing itself as fact.

### Page 6

This is why not knowing becomes so important when Chapter Ten turns toward government.

An individual's error may fall mainly upon that individual. When power presents inference as fact, error travels through commands, budgets, and institutions into many lives. The greater the reach of action, the more important it becomes to know where knowledge stops.

This does not require government to wait forever. Floods, illness, and danger do not postpone themselves until information is complete. It requires uncertainty to become part of the conditions of action, so that decisions can be checked, revised, and given a path of return.

### Page 7

**“Without knowing” and “not knowing”:** In Wang Bi's text, the phrase follows the question about caring for the state and governing the people. Heshang Gong's version places the related wording differently. This essay uses “not knowing” as a broader entrance without treating every passage containing “know” as one fixed technical term.

**Knowledge and cleverness:** The *Dao De Jing* does not reject recognition, experience, or discernment as a whole. It repeatedly limits knowledge when it becomes an identity performed for admiration, a technology of control, or a conclusion no longer answerable to reality.

**Three doors left open:** Later chapters speak of not knowing the name, of learning that increases and the Dao that decreases, and of knowing that one does not know. They will take naming, learning, and cognitive limits further. Here we mark the doors without walking every road beyond them.

## Visual Description

### Page 8

The following is an original visual narrative (fiction).

Late at night after a typhoon has crossed the mountains, the flood-control room for an entire watershed remains filled with white light. On the main display, rain clouds move downstream and rivers appear as luminous threads. Most water-level figures continue to change. Beside one upstream tributary, however, there is only a dash.

A young operator glances at it. “That station is at zero. We can send the inspection vehicle downstream and leave the riverside passage open.” The dispatcher's hand moves toward the confirmation key.

The chief engineer says, “Wait. A dash is not zero.”

“Then what is it?”

“It tells us only that the sensor has not answered.”

She lifts a marker and divides the whiteboard beside the display into three columns: **FACT, INFERENCE, UNKNOWN.**

## Main Text

### Page 9

Under FACT she writes: heavy rain upstream; downstream water still rising; station offline for seventeen minutes.

Under INFERENCE: the crest may not have passed; lightning may have damaged communications; a local power failure is also possible.

Under UNKNOWN she writes one sentence: the tributary's actual water level now.

The engineer does not use uncertainty as a reason to stop working. She asks nearby communities for reports, sends the closest available inspection team, and requests a backup observation that does not depend on the same communications link. Until new information arrives, the riverside passage remains closed, the vehicle stays in place, and critical flood capacity is held in reserve.

Half an hour later, the field report arrives: the water is near the warning level. The dash did not predict the flood. It prevented the room from reporting silence as safety.

### Page 10

Modern systems are skilled at producing a smooth appearance of certainty. Maps have colours, models have decimals, instruments have scales, and reports have conclusions. An orderly interface makes it easy to forget that every number has a source, a time, and an error range—and every blank has a cause.

“No data” and “a value of zero” may differ by one symbol on a screen, yet lead to entirely different actions. The absence of complaints does not prove the absence of harm. The absence of recorded patients does not prove the absence of illness. The absence of voiced opposition does not prove universal agreement.

The importance of not knowing often appears inside this small pause: ask first what the silence means, then decide what it can prove.

### Page 11

Dividing information into fact, inference, and unknown does not eliminate bias, but it makes bias harder to hide inside a sentence.

Facts are the parts supported by reliable evidence. Inferences are provisional explanations drawn from those facts. Unknowns are the spaces the present evidence cannot yet carry. All three may change: a new measurement can turn an unknown into a fact, or overturn yesterday's most reasonable inference.

Mature judgment writes conditions beside confidence: why do I believe this? What new fact would change the decision? If I am wrong, can the action be reversed in time?

### Page 12

Acting under uncertainty does not mean delaying every step until nothing can go wrong. That too may become an evasion.

Flood workers must still close roads. Physicians must still respond to urgent risk. Managers must still allocate resources before every report is complete. The difference lies in whether they state inference as inference, seek information closest to the situation, leave a door open for dissent, and avoid making irreversible choices at the beginning whenever possible.

Not knowing is not itself a brake. It is more like a warning light on the instrument panel: part of the road remains unseen, so speed, margin, and methods of review must change.

### Page 13

Real expertise is often not the ability to say “I don't know” less frequently than others, but the ability to say more precisely what is not known.

A beginner may not see the risk. Someone with partial understanding may force every event into one familiar pattern. A deeply experienced person knows that the same appearance may arise from different causes, what a test can exclude, and what it cannot.

Expert knowledge and not knowing are therefore not enemies. The more reliable the knowledge, the clearer its boundary tends to become. What deserves suspicion is not certainty itself, but certainty that will not disclose its conditions—certainty that demands obedience while refusing to say where it could be wrong.

### Page 14

For the same reason, “governing without knowing” cannot justify keeping people ignorant, withholding information, or treating learning as a threat.

A ruler who deliberately deprives people of education and evidence has not relinquished cleverness. Knowledge has merely been turned into the property of a few. An institution that never reveals its grounds and never allows affected people to correct its facts does not produce simplicity. It produces dependence.

Chapter Ten directs its question first toward the holder of power: can you refrain from ruling through the claim of superior knowledge? The task is not to close everyone's eyes. It is to recognize that knowledge dispersed across communities, workplaces, and fields of expertise does not automatically gather beneath one official chair.

### Page 15

Not knowing matters throughout the *Dao De Jing* for another reason: the Dao is never an object that can be contained by one final label.

Laozi still uses language. He distinguishes, compares, advises, and gives us the word “Dao.” Yet some sentences pause beside “I do not know,” as if leaving a small portion of the page unpainted at the edge of a name.

What does that blank mean? How does it relate to the knowledge that learning continually adds? Why will acknowledging not knowing later be called a higher kind of knowledge? These questions will return. For now, it is enough to see that Laozi's thought does not begin with the number of answers, but at the place where an answer ceases to impersonate the whole.

## Return

### Page 16

Knowing and not knowing are not alternatives from which we must choose only one.

Knowledge honours fact, experience, and pattern. Not knowing prevents a partial fact from expanding into the whole of reality. Knowledge gives action grounds; not knowing gives action proportion. Knowledge can dispel confusion; not knowing can prevent knowledge from hardening into pride, manipulation, and control without limits.

The difficult act is not saying “I don't know.” It is keeping the unknown honestly present when responsibility presses, people wait, and the screen demands immediate confirmation.

### Page 17

When the display in the flood-control room comes back online, the dash finally becomes a number.

The chief engineer does not erase UNKNOWN from the whiteboard. She writes the time beside the entry and leaves the next line open. Every answer has a moment when it arrives and a moment when it expires. Honest judgment never pretends that it has finished forever.

Do not read a blank as zero. Do not rush to fill every blank.

Sometimes it is the space clearly preserved that allows knowledge to breathe, power to turn back, and the next thing before us to become visible.
