# 第016-1章：归根——在循环中辨认长久

## 原文

### 第1页

致虚极，守静笃。

万物并作，吾以观复。

夫物芸芸，各复归其根。

归根曰静，是谓复命。

白话：让心中空出充分的位置，安定地守住观察。万物一同生长，我从中观看它们往复运行。草木繁盛以后，各自回到根部。回归根本称为静，也称为回到生命的常序。

### 第2页

复命曰常，知常曰明。

不知常，妄作凶。

知常容，容乃公，公乃王，王乃天，天乃道，道乃久，没身不殆。

白话：懂得生命往复的常序，便能获得明察。忽略常序而轻率行动，容易招致灾害。懂得常序，心量便能包容；包容带来公正，公正形成统摄，统摄合于天地，天地合于道，道使事物获得长久。如此终身可以远离危险。

## 释义

### 第3页

第十六章先安排一个观察位置。“致虚”清理成见，“守静”稳定注意。观察者暂缓干预，让变化走完一段路程，万物的往复才会显出轮廓。

这里的静承担认识功能。它让人看见萌发、繁盛、收束与再生之间的联系，也让一次得失回到较长的时间中接受判断。

### 第4页

“复”写返回，也写更新。叶片落下，养分进入土壤；种子休眠，来年重新萌发。回到根部属于生命运行的一环。

“常”因此指可反复辨认的秩序。它存在于变化之中。懂得常的人，会为生长留时，也会为休整留时。

### 第5页

经文从“知常”走向“容、公、王”。当一个人看见各阶段都有位置，眼前的旺盛便失去独占尺度的权力。衰退、停顿与恢复也进入判断。

这种眼界形成包容。公共决策由此照顾多种生命、多段时间和多方需要。“明”最终落实为一种分配尺度。

## 术语与考据简注

### 第6页

**归根：** 河上公为第十六章所加的章题。草木枝叶繁盛，岁时到来以后回到根部，根又保存下一轮生机。

**致虚极，守静笃：** “致”有达到之意，“笃”有深厚、坚定之意。河上公把它联系到清理情欲与守持清静。王弼说“有起于虚，动起于静”，借虚静说明生发的根基。

**观复：** 河上公释作观看万物归本。观察的对象包含整个过程，视线也从枝叶回到根部。

### 第7页

**复命曰常：** “命”在这里指生命所禀受的运行。往复持续发生，常序由此可知。

**知常容：** 王弼把“容”解释为包通万物。认识常序以后，观察者能够容纳差异，也能给各物安排合宜位置。

**公乃王：** 河上公本与通行本作“公乃王，王乃天”。王弼本有“公乃全，全乃天”的异文。两条文本路径都把“公”继续推向完整的统摄，并接入天、道与久。

## 正文

### 第8页

以下是一则原创框架（fiction）。

深秋，城北植物园的草甸完成一季花期。茎秆转为褐色，种荚挂在风里。游客中心收到许多留言，希望园方尽快清理，再种一片冬季花卉。

养护负责人林澄带着记录板走进草甸。她在每块样地前停下，记下种子成熟度、土壤湿度和昆虫活动。

### 第9页

管理会议上，运营主管打开明年的宣传图。图中的草甸四季开花，色彩始终饱满。

林澄把一只纸袋放在桌上，里面装着当天收集的种子。

“这片地正在完成今年的工作。”她说，“种子落下以后，我们再割第一批茎秆。”

### 第10页

团队把草甸分成六个区域。两块保留种荚，两块延后修剪，一块覆盖落叶，一块作为来年对照。每次养护都记录日期，土壤样本也按月归档。

冬季来临，地表逐渐安静。林澄用窄铲取出一小块土。根系仍贴着土粒，芽点藏在近地处。

### 第11页

一位志愿者问：“游客看见这片颜色，会觉得园方忘了照料吗？”

林澄把样本递给他：“请先看根。”

他们蹲在步道旁，逐一辨认根茎、种壳和新芽。志愿者把辨认结果整理成说明牌，写明结籽、休眠与修剪日期，也邀请游客记录每周变化。

### 第12页

冬至以后，园丁分区修剪。割下的茎秆在场地边缘粉碎，铺回土面。种子留在原地，鸟类也继续取食。

这套安排让维护跟随草甸的节律。工作人员分批进入，土壤获得覆盖，来年的萌发记录也保留了对照。

### 第13页

春季第一场暖雨过后，样地陆续出现新芽。不同植物在不同日期醒来。林澄每天清晨走一遍，把萌发位置画在方格纸上。

运营主管也来到草甸。他看完记录，把下一年的宣传语改成一句朴素说明：“请跟随一片草地，走完四季。”

### 第14页

《周易》的复卦以“一阳来复”写周期回转。白居易写“野火烧不尽，春风吹又生”，也把视线从地表转向下一轮生机。这些表达共同提醒人们，眼前形态只占过程的一段。

现代生态养护同样依靠长期记录。花期、结籽、休眠和更新都进入计划，管理者据此决定修剪时点与保护范围。

### 第15页

这种判断也适用于工作。一个项目经历启动、推进、交付、复盘与维护。团队为每一段配置时间，经验才能回到流程，人员也能恢复精力。

“知常”让负责人看见整个周期。预算由此包含维护，排期容纳复盘，评价也承认基础工作。公共尺度从时间中形成。

## 回扣

### 第16页

第十六章从一颗安静的心走向一套广阔的秩序。虚让观察拥有空间，静让变化显出轨迹，复让生命回到循环，常让人获得判断。

“知常”生出“容”，“容”又通向“公、王”，个人明察由此进入共同生活。一个人看得越长，越能承认多种阶段的价值，也越能承担分配与治理。

### 第17页

春末，草甸重新开花。林澄沿步道收起旧说明牌，在背面写下今年第一批种子成熟的日期。

花开属于循环，记录也属于循环。人站在其中，守住片刻安静，便能看见来处与去向。

所谓长久，正由一次次归根获得形状。

# Chapter 016-1: Returning to the Root—Recognizing Continuity in Cycles

## Original Text

### Page 1

Bring emptiness to its utmost degree; hold firmly to stillness.

The myriad things arise together, and I watch their return.

Things flourish in profusion, and each returns to its root.

Returning to the root is called stillness; this is called returning to life's course.

### Page 2

Returning to life's course is called constancy; knowing constancy is called illumination.

Ignoring constancy and acting recklessly brings harm.

Knowing constancy, one becomes capacious. Being capacious, one becomes impartial. Being impartial, one can govern. Governing accords with Heaven; Heaven accords with Dao; Dao endures. Through the end of life, one remains free from peril.

## Interpretation

### Page 3

Chapter Sixteen first establishes a place from which to observe. Emptiness clears away preconception; stillness steadies attention. The observer delays intervention long enough for change to complete part of its course and reveal the shape of return.

Stillness serves knowledge here. It allows us to see the relation among emergence, flourishing, contraction, and renewal. It also places a single gain or loss within a longer span of time.

### Page 4

“Return” describes both going back and beginning again. Leaves fall and feed the soil. Seeds enter dormancy and germinate in another season. The return to the root belongs to the work of life.

“Constancy” therefore means an order that can be recognized through repetition. It lives inside change. A person who knows it gives growth its season and recovery its season.

### Page 5

The text moves from knowing constancy to capacity, impartiality, and governance. Once every phase has a place, visible flourishing loses its claim to be the sole measure. Decline, pause, and recovery also enter judgment.

This wider view produces capacity. Public decisions can then account for different lives, different times, and different needs. Illumination becomes a standard for allocation.

## Key Terms and Notes

### Page 6

**Returning to the Root:** Heshang Gong's title for Chapter Sixteen. Plants reach abundance in their branches and leaves, then return with the season to the root, which carries life into another cycle.

**Bring emptiness to the utmost; hold firmly to stillness:** The first verb means to reach or bring about, while the final word suggests depth and firmness. Heshang Gong links the line with clearing desire and preserving quiet. Wang Bi writes that being arises from emptiness and movement arises from stillness.

**Watching the return:** Heshang Gong reads this as watching the myriad things return to their basis. Observation embraces the whole process and follows the eye from branch to root.

### Page 7

**Returning to life's course is constancy:** “Life's course” refers to the movement allotted to living things. Its recurrence makes a lasting pattern intelligible.

**Knowing constancy, one becomes capacious:** Wang Bi explains capacity as the ability to comprehend and include the myriad things. Recognizing recurrent order makes room for difference and gives each thing an appropriate place.

**Being impartial, one can govern:** Heshang Gong's text and many received editions continue with governance and Heaven. Wang Bi's edition has a variant meaning wholeness before Heaven. Both textual paths carry impartiality toward comprehensive order, Heaven, Dao, and duration.

## Main Text

### Page 8

The following is an original fictional framework.

In late autumn, a meadow at the northern botanical garden finishes its flowering season. Stems turn brown and seed heads hang in the wind. The visitor center receives many requests for an immediate clearing and a new display of winter flowers.

Lin Cheng, the head of maintenance, enters with a clipboard. At each plot, she records seed maturity, soil moisture, and insect activity.

### Page 9

At the management meeting, the operations director opens next year's promotional design. Its meadow flowers in every season, with color held at a constant peak.

Lin Cheng places a paper bag of seeds on the table.

“This ground is completing this year's work,” she says. “After the seeds fall, we will cut the first section.”

### Page 10

The team divides the meadow into six areas. Two retain their seed heads, two receive later cutting, one receives a leaf cover, and one becomes a control plot for the following year. Every intervention gets a date, and soil samples enter a monthly archive.

Winter arrives and the surface grows quiet. Lin Cheng lifts a narrow core of soil. Roots still hold its grains, and buds rest close to the ground.

### Page 11

A volunteer asks, “Will visitors see these colors and think the garden has forgotten its work?”

Lin Cheng hands him the sample. “Look at the roots first.”

They crouch beside the path and identify rhizomes, seed cases, and new buds one by one. The volunteer turns what they identify into a sign listing the dates for seeding, dormancy, and cutting, with an invitation for visitors to record weekly changes.

### Page 12

After the winter solstice, gardeners cut the meadow section by section. They shred the stems at the edge of the site and spread them back over the soil. Seeds remain in place, and birds continue to feed.

The arrangement makes maintenance follow the meadow's rhythm. Staff enter in stages, soil keeps its cover, and the record preserves a comparison for spring germination.

### Page 13

After the first warm spring rain, shoots appear across the plots. Different plants wake on different dates. Lin Cheng walks the meadow each morning and maps every emergence on graph paper.

The operations director comes as well. After reading the record, he revises next year's line of promotion: “Follow one meadow through all four seasons.”

### Page 14

The Return hexagram in the *Book of Changes* speaks of the first yang line coming back. Bai Juyi's line, “Wildfire cannot consume them; spring wind brings them forth again,” also directs the eye from the surface toward another cycle of life. Both place the present form within a longer process.

Contemporary ecological care likewise depends on records over time. Flowering, seeding, dormancy, and renewal enter the plan, guiding the timing of cutting and the boundaries of protection.

### Page 15

The same judgment applies to work. A project moves through initiation, effort, delivery, review, and maintenance. When a team gives time to each phase, experience returns to the process and people recover their strength.

Knowing constancy lets a leader see the entire cycle. Budgets then include maintenance, schedules include review, and evaluation recognizes foundational labor. A public standard takes shape through time.

## Return to the Original

### Page 16

Chapter Sixteen travels from a quiet mind into a spacious order. Emptiness gives observation room, stillness reveals a trajectory, return places life within a cycle, and constancy supplies judgment.

Knowing constancy produces capacity, and capacity opens toward impartiality and governance. Personal insight thereby enters shared life. Longer vision admits the value of many phases and supports the work of allocation and care.

### Page 17

By late spring, the meadow flowers again. Lin Cheng walks the path and removes the old sign. On its back, she writes the date when this year's first seeds will mature.

Flowering belongs to the cycle, and so does the record. Standing within it, a person keeps still for a moment and sees both origin and direction.

Continuity takes form through each return to the root.
