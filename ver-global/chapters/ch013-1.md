# 第013-1章：厌耻——宠辱来到身体时

## 原文

### 第1页

宠辱若惊，贵大患若身。

何谓宠辱若惊？

宠为下，得之若惊，失之若惊，是谓宠辱若惊。

白话：受到宠爱与遭遇屈辱，都会使人惊动。宠爱把价值交到别人手里，得到时担忧失去，失去时又感到恐惧。

### 第2页

何谓贵大患若身？

吾所以有大患者，为吾有身，及吾无身，吾有何患？

故贵以身为天下，若可寄天下；爱以身为天下，若可托天下。

白话：大的忧患经由身体发生。能够珍重自身，也能以同样分量对待天下生命的人，才适合接受天下的托付。

## 释义

### 第3页

宠与辱看似位于两端，却共享同一条绳索。宠来自上位者的给予，辱来自上位者的贬抑。一个人把自身价值系在这条绳索上，心便会随着外界升降。

“宠为下”点出其中的权力关系。受宠者需要持续符合给予者的期待，所得越显眼，对失去的警觉也越强。掌声由此进入身体，变成失眠、紧张和反复确认。

### 第4页

“吾所以有大患者，为吾有身”历来容纳多种解释。河上公把身体的饥寒劳苦与逐欲之患放在一起；王弼则强调荣宠会损易其身。两家都让抽象得失落回生命承担的代价。

身体在这里提供一条尺度。名位可以更换，评价可以转向，饥饿、疼痛与疲惫却要求真实照料。一个人愿意听取这条尺度，才会知道哪些荣誉值得接受，哪些代价已经过重。

### 第5页

本章结尾从个人安顿转向天下托付。珍重生命的人会谨慎使用权力，因为每项命令最终都落到具体身体上：有人因此劳作，有人因此迁移，也有人因此承受危险。

“贵身”因此带有公共含义。治理者珍惜自身生命，也承认他人的生命具有同等重量。天下可以托付给这样的人，因为他理解一项决定究竟由谁付账。

## 术语与考据简注

### 第6页

**厌耻：** 河上公为第十三章所加的章题。“厌”在此可联系到压伏、克服，“耻”则与辱相连。章题把注意力放在荣辱对心身的扰动上。

**宠辱若惊：** “若惊”写受到惊动的状态。王弼把宠、辱与荣、患并举，指出它们都可能扰乱人对自身分量的判断。

**宠为下：** 宠爱由他人授予，受宠者因此处在依附位置。这里讨论权力结构，也讨论人如何把判断权交给外界。

### 第7页

**有身：** 经文首先指人的身体与生命，也可延伸到对身份的执取。解释时仍应保留身体层面的实际重量。

**寄天下、托天下：** 都指把天下交付给某人治理。王弼强调，一个人先守住自身免受荣宠损易，才具备承担天下的条件。

“贵身”与纵容欲望分属两件事。前者照料生命，后者让欲望不断索取。第十二章的“为腹”与本章的“贵身”由此形成连续。

## 正文

### 第8页

《史记·廉颇蔺相如列传》记载了一个简短场面。廉颇失去将军职位以后，原先聚在门下的宾客相继离开。后来他重新被任用，那些人又回到门前。

门客的脚步随着职位来去。院门、坐席和名帖仍在，交情的温度却由朝廷任命决定。

### 第9页

廉颇准备赶走这些归来的宾客。其中一位门客当面解释：天下人按“市道”交往，权势在时便追随，权势离开便散去。这套往来像市场交换，双方都在衡量所得。

这番话带着冷意，也揭开受宠的结构。门庭兴盛时，廉颇容易把拥戴看成对自己的认可；门庭冷落时，他才发现许多人看重的是将军职位。

### 第10页

“宠辱若惊”照见的正是这种升降。重新任将带来欢喜，也重新带来失去职位的可能。宾客回归可以满足尊荣，同时提醒尊荣依赖外部条件。

老子把宠与辱放在同一句中，迫使人看见两者的共同来源。只要身份由别人随时授予或收回，处在高位也会保持戒惧。

### 第11页

廉颇的故事还留下一个现实问题：怎样分辨一个人尊重的是我，还是我的位置？答案需要经过时间与处境。职位变化以后仍愿意承担关系的人，才让交情获得独立于权势的分量。

这种分辨也要求当事人放松对热闹的依赖。门客满堂时照常做事，门庭冷落时仍能吃饭、睡觉、训练和判断，宠辱才会回到事件本身。

### 第12页

现代组织也有自己的门庭。升职邮件一经发出，祝贺与邀约便挤满日历；项目失败写进通报，会议名单随即删去几个名字。通讯录中的增减常比正式评价更早显示权力流向。

一个人可以记录这些变化，却把决定放在稳定尺度上：职责是否完成，事实是否说清，身体是否承受得住，关系是否经得起职位变化。

### 第13页

领导者面对宠辱时，还要承担额外责任。团队的称赞容易把普通判断包装成天赋，公开批评又容易把一次错误扩大成人格判决。两种声音都会改变决策者接收事实的能力。

可靠的做法很具体：让反对意见进入会议，让坏消息绕过层级，让提出异议的人保有职位安全。评价仍然存在，事实获得了自己的通道。

### 第14页

“贵以身为天下”也提醒照料者保存自身。长期透支的医生、父母或管理者，常把疲惫解释成责任感。失眠拖慢判断，疼痛缩短耐心，急躁也由此进入每次决定。

珍重自身会改变照料方式：轮班得到落实，边界得到说明，任务可以交接。一个人保存了继续照料的能力，身边的人也免于依赖一次性的牺牲。

### 第15页

宠辱的力量来自反复进入日常。一句赞扬留在睡前，一条批评占据早餐，人的注意力便逐渐围绕别人眼中的自己运转。

身体提供返回现实的路径。脚仍要走路，饭仍要入口，工作仍要一项项完成。评价可以被听见，行动则依据事实继续。

## 回扣

### 第16页

第十三章把三层关系接在一起：宠辱扰动身份，忧患进入身体，贵身通向天下托付。人的稳定因此带有公共后果。

能够守住自身的人，较少用权力追逐赞美，也较少让他人承担自己的恐惧。他先以生命衡量自身承受，再把同一尺度用于天下。

### 第17页

一次会议结束后，名牌被工作人员从主位收走，桌上留下一圈浅痕。刚才受到称赞的人提起外套，和同事一起走向电梯。

第二天，桌上会放另一个名字。需要完成的工作仍在，人的身体也仍要走进晨光、吃完早餐、承担当天的决定。

宠辱各有来去。生命以自己的重量留在原处。

# Chapter 013-1: Weariness of Disgrace—When Favor Reaches the Body

## Original Text

### Page 1

Favour and disgrace would seem equally to be feared; honour and great calamity, to be regarded as personal conditions of the same kind.

What is meant by speaking thus of favour and disgrace? Disgrace is being in a low position after the enjoyment of favour. The getting of favour leads to apprehension, and its loss leads to fear. This is what is meant by saying that favour and disgrace would seem equally to be feared.

### Page 2

And what is meant by saying that honour and great calamity are to be regarded as personal conditions? What makes me liable to great calamity is my having the body which I call myself; if I had not the body, what great calamity could come to me?

Therefore, one who would administer the kingdom, honouring it as he honours his own person, may be employed to govern it. One who would administer it with the love he bears to his own person may be entrusted with it.

## Interpretation

### Page 3

Favor and disgrace appear at opposite ends of status, yet they hang from the same cord. Favor comes through another person's grant, and disgrace through another person's demotion. Once self-worth is tied to that cord, the heart rises and falls with external judgment.

“Favor is low” names a relation of power. The favored person must keep meeting the giver's expectations. Prominence can therefore enter the body as lost sleep, tension, and continual checking.

### Page 4

“My great trouble comes from having a body” has supported several readings. Heshang Gong connects the body with hunger, cold, labor, and desire. Wang Bi emphasizes the way honor can damage life. Both bring abstract gain and loss back to a cost carried by the living person.

The body supplies a measure. Rank changes and judgment turns, while hunger, pain, and fatigue still ask for care. Listening to that measure clarifies which honors deserve acceptance and which costs have become excessive.

### Page 5

The closing lines move from personal steadiness to public trust. A person who values life uses power carefully, because every command eventually reaches bodies: someone works, moves, waits, or enters danger.

Valuing the body therefore has a public meaning. A ruler who cherishes life can recognize equal weight in other lives. Such a person understands who pays for a decision.

## Key Terms and Notes

### Page 6

**Weariness of disgrace:** Heshang Gong's title for Chapter Thirteen. It directs attention toward the disturbance produced by honor and shame.

**Favor and disgrace startle alike:** Wang Bi places favor, disgrace, honor, and calamity together. Each can unsettle a person's sense of proportion.

**Favor is low:** Favor is granted by another. The recipient therefore occupies a dependent position and may hand judgment over to the outside world.

### Page 7

**Having a body:** The line begins with embodied life and may also extend toward attachment to identity. Interpretation should preserve the concrete weight of the body.

**Entrusting the world:** Both phrases concern giving the care of the world to someone. Wang Bi emphasizes the capacity to preserve life from the damage of honor.

Valuing life and indulging desire follow different directions. The first cares for life; the second keeps demanding from it.

## Main Text

### Page 8

The *Records of the Grand Historian*, in the biography of Lian Po and Lin Xiangru, preserves a brief scene. When Lian Po lost his command, the retainers around him departed. When he returned to office, they came back to his gate.

Their footsteps followed rank. The courtyard, mats, and calling cards remained, while the warmth of association changed with an appointment from court.

### Page 9

Lian Po prepared to dismiss the returning retainers. One of them answered that people dealt with one another according to the way of the marketplace: power drew followers, and its departure dispersed them. Each side measured what it could gain.

The answer is cold, yet it exposes the structure of favor. A crowded house can look like affection until office disappears.

### Page 10

“Favor and disgrace startle alike” describes this rise and fall. Reappointment brings pleasure and revives the possibility of another dismissal. The return of retainers confirms honor while revealing its dependence on external conditions.

Laozi places favor and disgrace in one sentence so that their common source becomes visible. A role granted and withdrawn by others carries anxiety even at its height.

### Page 11

Lian Po's story leaves a practical question: does someone respect the person or the position? Time and circumstance answer. Those who continue carrying a relationship after rank changes give friendship a weight independent of power.

The person at the center must also loosen dependence on a crowded house. Work, food, sleep, training, and judgment can continue through both acclaim and neglect.

### Page 12

Modern organizations have their own courtyards. A promotion announcement fills a calendar with congratulations and invitations; a failed project enters the record, and several names disappear from meeting lists. Such additions and removals can reveal the movement of power before a formal review does.

A person may observe those movements while deciding by steadier measures: whether the duty was completed, facts were stated, the body can bear the cost, and the relationship can survive a change of title.

### Page 13

Leaders carry an added responsibility. Praise can dress an ordinary judgment as genius; public criticism can enlarge one mistake into a verdict on character. Both sounds alter a leader's ability to receive facts.

Practical safeguards help: dissent enters the meeting, bad news travels around rank, and the person who raises a concern retains safety at work. Evaluation remains, while facts receive a path of their own.

### Page 14

“Value the body as the world” also asks caregivers to preserve themselves. Doctors, parents, and managers may translate depletion into devotion. Lost sleep slows judgment, pain shortens patience, and impatience thereby enters each decision.

Care for the self changes the arrangement: shifts are honored, boundaries are stated, and tasks can be handed over. The ability to keep caring survives.

### Page 15

Favor and disgrace gain power by entering ordinary hours. A compliment remains at bedtime; a criticism occupies breakfast. Attention gradually begins to revolve around the self seen by others.

The body provides a return to reality. Feet still walk, food still enters the mouth, and work still proceeds one task at a time. Judgment may be heard while action continues according to fact.

## Return to the Original

### Page 16

Chapter Thirteen joins three relations: favor disturbs identity, trouble enters the body, and valuing life opens toward public trust. Personal steadiness therefore has public consequences.

A person who can preserve life has less need to use power for praise or make others carry private fear. Life becomes the measure later applied to the world.

### Page 17

After a meeting, a staff member removes the nameplate from the principal seat. A pale ring remains on the table. The person praised a moment earlier lifts a coat and walks toward the elevator with colleagues.

Tomorrow another name will stand there. Work remains to be done, and a body must still enter morning light, finish breakfast, and carry the day's decisions.

Favor and disgrace come and go. Life keeps its own weight.
