# 第004-1章：道冲而用之

## 原文

### 第1页

道冲而用之或不盈，渊乎似万物之宗。

挫其锐，解其纷，和其光，同其尘，湛兮似若存。

吾不知谁之子，象帝之先。

### 第2页

道以空处容纳万物，作用由此绵延。它磨去锋芒，理开纷扰，调和光辉，与尘土相处，幽隐而常存。它先于“帝”的观念，也越过任何确定名称的边界。

## 释义

### 第3页

“道冲而用之”写道以虚静保持流通，生成、容纳并成全万物。“或不盈”写它始终留有余地，新事物因而能够进入，原有事物也得以变化。

### 第4页

“挫其锐，解其纷，和其光，同其尘”依次写出四个动作：锋芒收住，乱结松开，光线变得柔和，生命回到日常尘土之中。冲突失去扩大的力量，万物便有机会恢复平衡。“湛兮似若存”写道的静默常在；“象帝之先”则把道安置在“帝”的观念之前，指向命令形成以前的根源。

## 术语与考据简注

### 第5页

**道冲**：道的本体是空，非死寂之空，而是能容纳、生成、流通万物的动态空性。

**或不盈**：道不自满，不停留于固定形态，故能持续发挥作用。

**和光同尘**：让光辉不炫耀，让存在不显赫，回归平凡与共生。

## 正文

### 第6页

这一章把道的存在方式写进一组动作。人收住一句逞强的话，争执便少添一层；他理清纠缠的事务，再把注意力交还给共同的问题。光芒从个人身上移开，落到事情本身，“挫锐”“解纷”“和光”“同尘”便进入日常。

## 回扣

### 第7页

本章以空处说明道的作用。余地容纳变化，柔和缓解冲突，日常尘土承接万物。道持续敞开自身，因而能够成为万物之宗。

# Chapter 004-1: The Way as Empty Yet Effective

## Original Text

### Page 1

The Tao is (like) the emptiness of a vessel; and in our employment of it we must be on our guard against all fulness. How deep and unfathomable it is, as if it were the Honoured Ancestor of all things!

We should blunt our sharp points, and unravel the complications of things; we should attemper our brightness, and bring ourselves into agreement with the obscurity of others. How pure and still the Tao is, as if it would ever so continue!

I do not know whose son it is. It might appear to have been before God.

### Page 2

Dao receives things through openness, and its use therefore continues. It blunts sharpness, untangles disorder, tempers brightness, and joins the dust of ordinary life. Hidden yet enduring, it precedes even the idea of a sovereign Lord and exceeds the boundary of any fixed name.

## Interpretation

### Page 3

This chapter presents Dao as openness in active use, a source through which becoming can continue. The phrase “not full” describes the room it keeps for arrival and change.

### Page 4

“Blunt sharpness, untangle disorder, temper brightness, and join the dust” unfolds as four actions. An edge is softened, a knot loosens, light grows mild, and life returns to ordinary dust. Conflict loses the force that enlarges it, giving things room to regain balance. “Deep and still, as if it were there” points to Dao’s quiet persistence. “Seeming to precede the Lord” places Dao before the very idea of command.

## Key Terms and Notes

### Page 5

**The Tao as empty vessel**: The Tao is not a solid substance but a mode of emptiness that can contain everything.

**Not full**: The Tao does not become stagnant or self-satisfied; its power lies in remaining open and receptive.

**Blend with the dust**: The Tao does not seek special distinction; it meets the world in humility and harmony.

## Main Text

### Page 6

This chapter places the source of reality in an openness that lets things appear. A person holds back a cutting remark, and the quarrel loses one source of heat; attention then returns to the shared problem. Brilliance moves away from the self and falls upon the work itself. In such actions, sharpness softens and light joins the dust.

## Return to the Original

### Page 7

This chapter uses openness to explain Dao’s effectiveness. Room receives change, gentleness eases conflict, and ordinary dust carries living things. Dao remains available in this way and becomes the source of the ten thousand things.
