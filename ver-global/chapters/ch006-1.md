# 第006-1章：谷神不死

## 原文

### 第1页

谷神不死，是谓玄牝。玄牝之门，是谓天地根。绵绵若存，用之不勤。

### 第2页

此句以“谷神”写道的本源。山谷承接水流，水流滋养草木，万物的生长由此连回内在根基。

### 第3页

“玄牝”既是幽深之母，也是万物生成之门。它藏在生长发生的地方，持续提供养分，使微小的开端逐渐长成完整生命。

## 释义

### 第4页

“谷神不死”说明世界的根本力量在隐微处持续发生。雨水沿坡面汇入山谷，再渗进土层，草木便从根部获得滋养。

### 第5页

“玄牝”写道阴柔而深邃的一面。它以承载为万物提供生成条件：土壤接住种子，身体孕育生命，空处容纳新的形态。“玄”指幽深，“牝”指生育与承载。

### 第6页

“玄牝之门”指万物生成的起点。天地由此获得持续生发的根基。“绵绵若存”写力量一线相续，“用之不勤”写它依循节律，始终保有余力。

## 术语与考据简注

### 第7页

**谷神**：指道之深层本源，常被解释为持续生成的生命之根。它存在于万物的生发之中。

**玄牝**：玄，深奥；牝，阴柔、孕养。此处用以形容道之母性的、生成性的根基。

**玄牝之门**：万物生发的入口，亦可理解为天地生成的根本关窍。

**天地根**：天地持续存在的根本，指向内在生发的源头。

**用之不勤**：使用它时并不费力，也不需要刻意耗损；它自然而然地持续运作。

## 正文

### 第8页

这一章把目光从树冠移向根部。风折断高枝，断口在雨中变暗；雨水又沿树干进入土层，被根系一点点吸收。来年能否抽芽，取决于地下仍在进行的工作。

### 第9页

人们从枝叶判断一棵树的盛衰，水的来路却藏在山势之中。雨落上坡面，顺着低处汇入山谷，再沿谷地进入草根和土壤。空处由此成为滋养发生的地方。

### 第10页

老子借“谷神”说明长久所需的条件。树木不断抽出枝叶，根部也要同时得到水分；水土得到守护，眼前的繁茂才有来年的延续。

### 第11页

“玄牝”代表一种生成方式。土壤承接落下的种子，水分渗入种皮，嫩芽便沿着自身节律生长。柔和的承载让生命获得展开的条件。

### 第12页

这也解释了道家对“柔弱胜刚强”的重视。强行推动会持续消耗施力者，承载与调节则把力量分散在漫长过程里。能让秩序维持下去的力量，常藏在日复一日的照料中。

### 第13页

“绵绵若存”写细微的连续：呼吸一进一出，泉水从石缝渗出，根须在土中伸展。每次变化都很小，生命却由这些变化持续积累。

### 第14页

“用之不勤”把用力放回节律之中。人先辨认事情自身的条件，再在需要之处施力，行动便能保有余地。无为由此成为一种准确的参与：清除阻塞，补足所缺，让万物依其纹理完成生长。

### 第15页

因此，这一章既谈生成，也谈人的用力方式。一个人把注意力从持续张扬移回呼吸、睡眠、工作与关系，日常节奏便逐渐稳定，也为漫长生活里的变化留下应对余地。

## 回扣

### 第16页

世界和人生都依赖力量的来处。泥沙堵住泉眼，水流便一天天变窄，河床随之露出；源头得到疏通，水又沿旧河道向前。眼前的盛大，要由持续供给它的根基承托。

### 第17页

“谷神不死”提醒我们把力量连回源头。一次行动留下余力，休息补回消耗，下一次行动便能从完整的节奏中开始。长久由这条细微的源流维系。

# Chapter 006-1: The Valley Spirit Never Dies

## Original Text

### Page 1

The valley spirit never dies; this is called the mysterious female. The gateway of the mysterious female is called the root of heaven and earth. It is continuous and seems to remain; use it without effort.

### Page 2

This passage uses the valley spirit to describe the source of Dao. A valley receives water, water nourishes plants, and growth thus remains connected to an inner foundation.

### Page 3

The “mysterious female” is both the profound mother and the gate of generation. It dwells wherever growth begins, continually supplying what allows a small beginning to become a complete life.

## Interpretation

### Page 4

“The valley spirit never dies” describes the world’s sustaining power at work in hidden places. Rain runs down the slope into a valley, enters the soil, and nourishes plants from their roots.

### Page 5

The “mysterious female” represents the receptive and generative side of Dao. Soil receives a seed, a body carries new life, and an open space admits a new form. Through such receptivity, beings gain the conditions in which to arise.

### Page 6

The “gateway of the mysterious female” names the point from which things are generated. Heaven and earth endure through this foundation. “Continuous and seems to remain” traces a fine continuity; “use it without effort” describes action that follows its rhythm and retains reserve.

## Key Terms and Notes

### Page 7

**The valley spirit**: The deep source of the Dao, often understood as an immortal and invisible root of life. It is not an external deity, but the inner source of all generation.

**The mysterious female**: “Mysterious” means profound; “female” means receptive, nurturing, and generative. It refers to the motherly aspect of the Dao.

**The gateway of the mysterious female**: The opening through which all things are born, or the fundamental passage of generation.

**The root of heaven and earth**: The fundamental basis of the existence of heaven and earth, not a separate created object but an unbroken source.

**Use it without effort**: One can draw upon it without strain or exhaustion; it operates naturally and continuously.

## Main Text

### Page 8

This chapter turns our attention from the crown to the roots. Wind breaks a high branch and rain darkens the exposed wood; the same rain travels down the trunk into the soil, where roots absorb it. Next year’s shoots depend on that underground work.

### Page 9

We judge a tree’s condition by its branches, while the water’s path remains hidden in the land. Rain falls on a slope, gathers in low ground, and enters grass roots and soil along the valley. Open space becomes the place where nourishment collects.

### Page 10

Laozi uses the valley spirit to identify the conditions of endurance. As a tree puts out leaves, its roots must continue receiving water. Preserving water and soil gives present abundance a path into the next season.

### Page 11

The “mysterious female” names a way of generating life. Soil receives a fallen seed, moisture enters its coat, and the shoot follows its own rhythm upward. Receptivity gives life room to unfold.

### Page 12

This also explains the Daoist praise of softness. Forceful pressure continually spends the strength of the one who applies it; receptivity and adjustment distribute effort across a longer process. The power that sustains order often resides in daily care.

### Page 13

“Continuous and seems to remain” describes a fine continuity: breath entering and leaving, a spring seeping through stone, roots lengthening underground. Each change is small, while life accumulates through their continuation.

### Page 14

“Use it without effort” returns exertion to rhythm. A person first reads the conditions of a task, then applies strength where it is needed, preserving room for what follows. Non-action becomes accurate participation: clear an obstruction, supply what is lacking, and let things develop according to their grain.

### Page 15

The chapter therefore concerns both generation and the human use of effort. When attention returns from display to breath, sleep, work, and relationship, a steady rhythm forms and leaves room for the changes of a long life.

## Return to the Original

### Page 16

The world and a human life both depend on the source of their strength. Silt blocks a spring, the current narrows by the day, and the riverbed appears. Clear the source, and water follows the old channel again. Present abundance needs a foundation that continues to supply it.

### Page 17

“The valley spirit never dies” asks us to reconnect effort with its source. One action leaves a reserve, rest restores what was spent, and the next begins within a complete rhythm. Endurance is maintained by this fine current.
