# 第004-3章：回归

## 原文

### 第1页

回归

和光同尘与厚黑术之别

在庸俗的官场解读中，“光”被视作“显赫的才能或功绩”，“尘”被视作“庸俗的环境或同僚”。“和光同尘”因此沦为一种策略：收敛光芒，以免刺眼；混入尘土，以免被排斥。

### 第2页

在老子的宇宙观里，“光”与“尘”呈现“道”在世间的两种质地：

“光”是照见事物的能力：辨明处境，也看见彼此的关联。日光落在山河上，使路径和边界一一显形；智慧也可如此，把注意力引向事物，自己退到一旁。

“尘”是衣食、劳作、衰老这些寻常生活，也是万物终会归返的物质。双手每天触到它，身体也在其中经历生长、磨损与死亡。

### 第3页

“和光同尘”让觉性与物质相遇，也松动高贵与低贱的分别。人放下对光的追逐，也接纳尘世生活，由此接近老子所说的道。

### 第4页

**体与用：“道冲而用之或不盈。”** 留有空处，事物便能持续进入。这是道的自足性：它以不盈的状态发挥作用，外在名目只需退居其后。对生命而言，丰盛也来自一份可供经验进入的余地。

**功：“挫锐解纷。”** 这句话写道对锐气的收敛和对纷争的化解。它强调过程中的调整，使冲突逐步失去继续扩大的条件。

### 第5页

**相：“和光同尘。”** 这句话写道的遍在。光与尘共同出现在日常生活中，神圣也以平凡形态显现。

**源：“象帝之先。”** 老子把道放在“帝”的观念之前。他把目光移到第一道命令形成以前，追问万物何以发生。这一层的“回归”，通向命名与支配尚待形成的状态。

### 第6页

“和光同尘”也可以成为一种生命理想：让光保持谦卑，让尘获得尊严。

这是许多传统文人向往的生命状态。苏轼在赤壁之下写道：“惟江上之清风，与山间之明月，耳得之而为声，目遇之而成色”，这可以理解为与光同游；他躬耕东坡、与农人往来，则近于与尘同息。他未必真正做到“玄同”，却一生都在向此道回归。

这个理想给了“光”以谦卑：智慧收敛自己的声势，帮助人辨清手边事务，也把注意力留给事情本身。

### 第7页

它给了“尘”以尊严：柴米油盐、生老病死都承载着真实生活，道也从这些日用之事中显形。慧能说“佛法在世间，不离世间觉”，正是“同尘”的禅宗回响。

### 第8页

禅宗的一则公案，让光与尘在眼前相遇。

禅宗有一则公案，可以把“光与尘”拉回手边：

有人问洞山守初禅师：“如何是佛？”

洞山答：“麻三斤。”

麻是三斤重的一团麻线，是尘中之尘，物中之物。当学人满怀对“佛光”的崇敬去追问时，禅师却以最粗粝、最平凡之物截断其妄想。

禅师把问题压回眼前这三斤麻。学人先称它的重量，再用手把纤维捻成线；手上的工作重新接住了刚才飘远的追问。

“和光同尘”可以落实为这种“归家稳坐”的踏实感。人低头面对手中的事，辨明分寸，再完成该做的动作，光与尘便在其中相遇。

### 第9页

光落在尘埃上，尘埃显出光的路径；尘埃沿着气流移动，房间里的明暗也随之显形。

“和光同尘”让才智与日常生活回到同一间屋子。人看得明白，也肯把手放到眼前具体的事情上。

# Chapter 004-3: Return

## Original Text

### Page 1

Return

Blending Light with Dust and the Art of Corruption

In vulgar interpretations of official life, “light” is seen as prominent talent or achievement, and “dust” is seen as vulgar surroundings or colleagues. Thus “conceal light and blend with dust” becomes a strategy: dampen your brilliance so it does not glare; mix into the dust so you are not rejected.

### Page 2

In Laozi’s cosmology, “light” and “dust” are two modes through which Dao appears in the world:

“Light” is the capacity to see: to distinguish a situation and perceive its relations. Daylight falls across rivers and mountains, bringing paths and boundaries into view. Wisdom can act in the same way, drawing attention toward things while receding from notice itself.

“Dust” is food, work, aging, and the ordinary matter to which living things return. Our hands touch it each day, and our bodies undergo growth, wear, and death within it.

### Page 3

To “conceal light and blend with dust” is not to disguise a supposedly noble self in a lowly environment. It loosens the distinction between noble and lowly by bringing awareness (light) and material existence (dust) together. When light is no longer assumed to be superior to dust, and one no longer seeks only to shine or fears being soiled, one draws closer to what Laozi calls Dao.

### Page 4

**Substance and function: “Dao is empty, yet in use it may never be filled.”** Because it leaves room, Dao can continue to receive and respond. It remains self-sufficient through this unfilled state, while external titles recede in importance. Human abundance can likewise grow from room left open to experience.

**Action: “Blunt sharpness and dissolve conflict.”** The phrase follows a process of adjustment. A cutting edge grows mild, tangled strands separate, and conflict loses the conditions that kept it expanding.

### Page 5

**Appearance: “Conceal light and blend with dust.”** This is Dao’s hiddenness and ubiquity. Light and dust are two sides of one whole. Dao is not confined to temples or clouds; it is also in the dust we breathe and the ordinary light before our eyes. The sacred appears in common forms because Dao is not suspended outside daily life.

**Origin: “Seeming to precede the Lord.”** Laozi places Dao before the idea of a supreme ruler. He turns toward the conditions from which things arise before command and intention take shape. Here, return reaches toward a state in which naming and domination have yet to begin.

### Page 6

“Conceal light and blend with dust” can also become an ideal of life: light retains humility, while dust is granted dignity.

Many traditional literati longed for such a way of life. At Red Cliff, Su Shi wrote that the river breeze became sound to the ear and the mountain moon color to the eye; this can be read as traveling with light. Farming at Dongpo and living among villagers brought him closer to breathing with dust. He may never have fully attained profound unity, but he spent his life returning toward it.

This ideal gives humility to light. Wisdom lowers its own profile, helps people discern the matter at hand, and leaves attention with the work itself.

### Page 7

It gives dignity to dust. Firewood, rice, oil, salt, birth, aging, sickness, and death all carry actual life, and Dao appears through these daily matters. Huineng said, “Buddha Dharma is in the world, not separate from worldly awareness.” This is the Chan echo of blending with dust.

### Page 8

A Zen case brings light and dust into an encounter before our eyes.

A Zen case brings light and dust back within reach.

Someone asked Dongshan Shouchu, “What is Buddha?”

Dongshan answered: “Three catties of hemp.”

Hemp is a bundle of hemp weighing three catties. It is dust within dust, thing within thing. When a student asks in full reverence for Buddha light, the master stops the delusion with the roughest, most ordinary object.

The master presses the question back into the three catties of hemp at hand. The student weighs it and begins twisting its fibers into cord. Work done by the hands receives the question that had drifted beyond reach.

“Conceal light and blend with dust” can be felt as the grounded ease of “returning home and sitting steadily.” Attention returns to the task at hand, measures what it requires, and completes the next action. Light and dust meet within that work.

### Page 9

Light falls on dust, and dust makes its path visible. As the particles move with the air, the room’s light and shade take shape.

Blending light with dust returns intelligence and ordinary life to the same room: see clearly, and place your hands on the particular work before you.
