# 第011-1章：无用——给作用留下位置

## 原文

### 第1页

三十辐共一毂，当其无，有车之用。

埏埴以为器，当其无，有器之用。

白话：许多辐条汇集到一个轮毂，正因为轮毂中央留有空处，车轴才能穿过，车轮才可以转动。揉和黏土做成器皿，正因为器皿内部留有空处，它才可以盛放东西。

### 第2页

凿户牖以为室，当其无，有室之用。

故有之以为利，无之以为用。

白话：开出门窗、围成房室，正因为其中留有可进入、可居住的空间，房屋才发生作用。所以，有形的材料和结构带来条件与便利，无形的空处使这些条件真正成为可用之物。

老子没有在“有”和“无”之间选出一个胜者。他问的是：一件东西的作用，究竟发生在看得见的形体上，还是发生在形体为世界让出的位置里？

## 释义

### 第3页

第十一章只用了三个日常物件：车轮、陶器和房屋。它们由木材、泥土、墙壁构成，却都不能只凭材料完成自身。

辐条若没有中心孔，不能与车轴配合；泥土若没有围出内腔，只是一团成形的土；墙壁若没有围出可居之室，也没有门窗供人、光和空气出入，便更接近封闭的障碍。

“无”在这里指被形体围出、被关系需要、可以让事情发生的空处。

### 第4页

空处也不是任意的缺口。

轮毂的孔必须在合适的位置，并与车轴相应；陶器的内腔需要由完整的器壁托住；门窗若开错地方，可能损伤结构，也未必带来光和通行。并不是材料减少得越多，作用便越大。

因此，“无之以为用”不等于崇拜残缺。真正发生作用的空，是被尺度、边界和目的认真塑造的空。它不与“有”敌对，而由“有”保存，也使“有”不至于成为封死自己的实体。

### 第5页

我们习惯把价值归给可见之物：用了多少材料，增加了多少功能，占满了多少面积，完成了多少日程。空白则容易被叫作浪费、闲置或尚未完成。

老子的三个譬喻让这种计算暂时反转。器物以自身材料承力，也以内部空处允许事物进入、通过、停留和变化。边界与开放彼此配合，作用便由此出现。

这也是第十一章的重要转折：道家的“无”不再只是难以命名的源头，开始进入一只轮、一只碗和一间屋子的具体生活。

## 术语与考据简注

### 第6页

**无用：** 河上公为第十一章所加的章题。这里不宜理解成“毫无价值”，更接近看似没有固定内容、却能使作用发生的空处。

**三十辐、毂：** “辐”是连接轮圈与中心的构件，“毂”是车轮中心与车轴相接的部分。古注以三十辐解释古车制度，也有人由此引申历日之数；理解本章的关键仍在毂中之空与车轴、众辐的配合。

**埏埴：** “埏”有揉和、调治之意，“埴”指黏土；合起来是和泥制陶。

**户牖：** “户”是门，“牖”是窗。经文既指门窗的开口，也借整个室内空间说明“无”如何成就居住。

### 第7页

**利与用：** “有之以为利，无之以为用”不必被拆成两条彼此绝对排斥的定义。“利”偏向有形条件带来的便利，“用”偏向作用的实际发生；句子的力量在于二者互相依赖。

王弼强调，有形的木、土与墙之所以成为车、器、室，都要依赖其中的“无”才能发生作用。河上公则更具体地说，毂中空才能转行，器中空才能盛受，室中空才能出入居处。两家都没有叫人抛弃形体。

用今天的话，可以把这种“无”理解成被保留的容量、通道和可变性；这只是现代说明，不是老子的技术术语。空若没有边界与照料，可能只是荒废；有若不肯留空，也会把自己的作用堵住。

## 正文

### 第8页

以下是一则原创框架（fiction）。

一座停用多年的城市电车库将被改造成社区中心。设计评审会上，大屏幕里的平面图几乎涂满了颜色：教室是蓝色，厨房是黄色，诊室是绿色，储藏室是灰色。只有建筑中央，留着一大片没有名称的白。

财务负责人用笔圈住它：“这一千平方米做什么？”

年轻建筑师回答：“暂时不指定一种用途。”

“我们花钱修复一块不知道拿来做什么的地方？”

“我们知道它要让许多事情发生，”她说，“只是还不知道那些事情会以什么次序到来。”

### 第9页

会议室安静下来。没有名称的空间，在预算表上很难辩护。办公室可以计算座位，店铺可以估算租金，仓库可以写出容量；一片空地只能显示为尚未被分配的面积。

建筑师翻到下一张图。她画了一只轮毂、一只陶碗和一间屋子的剖面。

“木头让轮子承力，空孔让车轴通过；泥壁给水边界，空腔让水留下；墙和屋顶遮蔽风雨，室内的空才让人居住。我们并不是不建这一部分。我们要建的，正是它保持可进入、可改变的能力。”

财务负责人问：“能力能画进施工图吗？”

“能。只是它看起来不像一堵新墙。”

### 第10页

为了守住中央大厅的空，设计反而必须更加具体。

承重加固尽量留在边缘，地面按多种荷载加强；四面设置宽门，电源、给排水和网络接口藏在可开启的地板带里；吸音帘与活动隔板收进侧墙，桌椅放在邻近库房，灯光既能照亮一张工作台，也能覆盖整片地面。

空白不是少画几笔。它需要计算人怎样进来、物怎样移走、声音怎样停下、下一种使用怎样不必拆掉上一种使用留下的硬壳。

评审委员会最终保留了大厅，却要求一年后重新检查：“如果它只是空着，就把它分隔掉。”

### 第11页

社区中心开放后，大厅并没有立即证明自己。

清晨，有人在靠窗处练习太极；下午，维修志愿者摆开几张长桌；周末，一场旧书交换只占了大厅的一角。更多时候，白色地面显得过于宽阔，脚步声从一端传到另一端。

有人提议在中央加建固定舞台，有人想摆上永久展柜，也有人不断把暂时不用的纸箱推进来。每一种建议都有真实需要，也都试图把共同的空处变成某一种需要的长期领地。

管理人只在墙上挂了一条规则：使用之后，让地面重新出现。

### 第12页

入冬后的一个夜晚，管理人接到电话：附近三栋公寓的供暖管道已经破裂，维修至少需要两天。他打开社区中心的侧门，工作人员推入折叠床，老人、孩子和需要协助的人也沿着这条通道进入大厅。

原本有名称的房间很快用完：诊室处理慢性病药物，厨房烧水，教室安置需要安静的家庭。中央大厅则在几个小时内改变了形状。

折叠床沿地面标线排开，活动隔板围出更衣处，充电桌靠近接口，热饮车从宽门直接推入。翻译志愿者、维修人员和寻找家人的居民不断穿过其间，却没有谁必须先拆掉一堵为别种用途而建的墙。

那一夜，平面图上的白色有了许多颜色。

### 第13页

大厅并没有预先知道灾情，也不是因为“什么都不做”才有用。

它的作用来自一种经过准备的未决定：边界已经建好，通道保持畅通，资源可以接入，而中心没有被单一方案永久占据。正因为设计者放弃了预测每一件未来事件，建筑才获得回应未被预测之事的能力。

这与第十章保留“未知”形成一处呼应。上一章为尚待证据的部分保留空白；这一章为尚未到来的生活保留用途。两种留白都给未来留下修正眼前判断的空间。

### 第14页

热饮车第二次推入大厅时，旧车库的高窗已经透进晨光。财务负责人跟着车走过床铺之间的通道，在窗边放下一只装着热水的陶杯，又回头看向仍可通行的地面。

他问建筑师：“所以，空白本身就是功能？”

“不是空白独自完成了这些。”她说，“没有屋顶，人会受冻；没有电和水，这里撑不过一夜；没有工作人员，宽阔只会变成混乱。是这些有形的东西把一片空托住，而这片空又让它们能够重新组合。”

他看着人们从门口进出：“有给它边界，无给它变化。”

### 第15页

供暖恢复后，折叠床、隔板和电线被一件件收回。捐来的物资还剩很多，有人建议索性留在大厅中央，免得下次再搬。

管理人仍把它们送入侧库，因为每一件“暂时放一下”的东西都会占用大厅。空处的维护与墙壁、设备一样需要劳动，也需要共同约束。

日程、组织和谈话也有类似的中心。如果每一分钟都提前占满，意外便无处安放；如果每个成员只能执行既定角色，新能力便难以出现；如果一句话刚停下就立刻被下一句话盖住，理解也没有进入的门。

留空允许计划之外的生活发生，也给后来者留下安排的权利。

## 回扣

### 第16页

车轮、陶器和房屋分别让运动、盛受与居住成为可能。

辐条、泥壁和墙体是“有”：它们承力、围合、遮蔽，给事物以形。毂孔、器腹和室内是“无”：它们让车轴通过，让水与粮食进入，让身体、光和空气获得位置。没有“有”，“无”无法被保存；没有“无”，“有”也难以完成自己的作用。

所以，“有之以为利，无之以为用”不是把世界分成有价值与无价值的两半。它让我们看见，真正的功能常诞生于实体愿意在哪里止住。

### 第17页

几天后，大厅恢复了空旷。地面上还留着胶带揭去后的浅色方框：这里曾放过床，那里曾有人等候消息，靠窗处曾摆着一只盛满热水的杯子。

空处保存着发生过的事，也继续等待下一次使用。

器皿以泥土围出可盛放的腹，房间以墙壁围出可生活的内部。边界认真完成，空处便能接住尚未来到的人与事；一扇门则把这些可能带进来。

# Chapter 011-1: No Fixed Use—Leaving Room for Use to Happen

## Original Text

### Page 1

The thirty spokes unite in the one nave; but it is on the empty space for the axle that the use of the wheel depends.

Clay is fashioned into vessels; but it is on their empty hollowness that their use depends.

In plain language: many spokes converge upon one hub, but the wheel can turn because the centre leaves an opening for the axle. Clay forms the walls of a vessel, but the vessel can receive because it has room within.

### Page 2

The door and windows are cut out from the walls to form an apartment; but it is on the empty space within that its use depends.

Therefore, what has a positive existence serves for profitable adaptation, and what has not serves for actual usefulness.

In plain language: materials and structures provide form, support, and protection. The open places they preserve allow entry, movement, receiving, and dwelling.

Laozi does not choose a winner between being and non-being. He asks where the work of a thing truly happens: in the form we can see, or in the place that form leaves open to the world?

## Interpretation

### Page 3

Chapter Eleven uses only three ordinary objects: a wheel, a vessel, and a room. Each is made from visible matter—wood, clay, walls—yet matter alone cannot complete it.

Without the opening at the hub, the spokes cannot work with an axle. Without an interior hollow, shaped clay remains a mass rather than a vessel. Without habitable space, and without doors and windows for bodies, light, and air, walls become closer to an enclosure than a dwelling.

Here, “what is not” does not mean an abstract nothingness. It is an opening shaped by form, required by relationship, and capable of allowing something to happen.

### Page 4

An empty place is not an arbitrary gap.

The opening in a hub must be correctly placed and fitted to the axle. A vessel's hollow must be held by an intact wall. A door or window cut carelessly may weaken a structure without admitting useful light or passage. Less material does not automatically create more use.

“What has not serves for usefulness” is therefore no praise of damage. Effective emptiness is shaped by scale, boundary, and purpose. It does not oppose what exists. Form preserves the opening, and the opening prevents form from sealing itself shut.

### Page 5

We tend to assign value to what can be seen and counted: material added, features installed, floor area occupied, hours scheduled. A blank is easily named waste, vacancy, or unfinished work.

Laozi's three images briefly reverse the calculation. A thing is useful not only because of what it is, but because of what it permits to enter, pass through, remain, and change. Use does not hide inside matter or emptiness alone. It appears when boundary and openness work together.

This is an important turn in Chapter Eleven. The Daoist “not-being” is no longer only a source beyond naming. It enters the concrete life of a wheel, a bowl, and a room.

## Key Terms and Notes

### Page 6

**No Fixed Use:** Heshang Gong titles Chapter Eleven “No Use.” The phrase should not be read as “without value.” It points toward a space that seems to contain no fixed function yet allows functions to occur.

**Thirty spokes and the nave:** The spokes join the rim to the centre; the nave is the hub where wheel and axle meet. Early commentators discuss the structure of ancient wheels and sometimes connect the number thirty with the days of a month. The chapter's central image remains the relationship among the spokes, the hub's opening, and the axle.

**Fashioning clay:** The Chinese expression describes working and shaping clay into a vessel.

**Door and window:** The text includes both the openings cut through the enclosure and the interior space that makes dwelling possible.

### Page 7

**Benefit and use:** The final line need not be divided into two mutually exclusive definitions. “Benefit” leans toward the advantage provided by visible form; “use” toward the moment function actually occurs. The force of the sentence lies in their dependence upon each other.

Wang Bi observes that wood, clay, and walls become wheel, vessel, and room only because emptiness allows them to function. Heshang Gong is more concrete: the open hub permits turning, the hollow vessel permits receiving, and the open room permits entry and dwelling. Neither asks us to discard form.

In modern language, this emptiness may resemble preserved capacity, passage, and adaptability. That is an explanatory bridge, not Laozi's technical vocabulary. Emptiness without boundaries and care may be neglect; form that refuses all emptiness can obstruct its own use.

## Main Text

### Page 8

The following is an original framework (fiction).

A city tram depot, unused for years, is being converted into a community centre. At the design review, almost every part of the plan is coloured: classrooms in blue, a kitchen in yellow, a clinic in green, storage in grey. Only the centre of the building remains a large unnamed field of white.

The finance director circles it with a pen. “What will these thousand square metres be used for?”

The young architect answers, “For now, they will not be assigned to one use.”

“We are paying to restore a place whose purpose you cannot name?”

“We know it must allow many things to happen,” she says. “We do not yet know the order in which they will arrive.”

### Page 9

The room grows quiet. Unnamed space is difficult to defend in a budget. An office can count desks, a shop can estimate rent, and a storehouse can state its capacity. Open floor appears only as area not yet allocated.

The architect turns to the next drawing. It shows a wheel hub, a clay bowl, and the section of a room.

“Wood allows the wheel to bear weight; the opening allows the axle to pass. Clay gives water a boundary; the hollow lets water remain. Walls and roof keep out weather; the room within allows people to dwell. We are not refusing to build this part. We are building its ability to remain enterable and changeable.”

“Can ability be drawn on a construction plan?” the finance director asks.

“Yes. It simply does not look like another wall.”

### Page 10

Preserving the empty hall requires unusually specific design.

Load-bearing reinforcement is kept toward the edges, and the floor is strengthened for different loads. Wide doors open on four sides. Power, water, drainage, and data connections lie beneath accessible floor strips. Acoustic curtains and movable partitions fold into the side walls. Tables and chairs wait in adjacent storage. Lighting can illuminate one workbench or the entire floor.

The blank is not made by drawing less. It requires thought about how people enter, how objects leave, how sound settles, and how the next use can arrive without demolishing the hard shell of the previous one.

The review committee preserves the hall, but requires another assessment after one year. “If it merely stands empty, we will divide it.”

### Page 11

After the community centre opens, the hall does not immediately prove itself.

At dawn, a few people practise tai chi by the windows. In the afternoon, repair volunteers unfold several long tables. On weekends, a book exchange occupies one corner. Much of the time, however, the white floor appears excessively wide, and footsteps travel from one end to the other.

Some propose a permanent stage. Others want fixed display cases. Unused boxes steadily migrate toward the centre. Every proposal answers a real need, and each attempts to turn shared openness into the permanent territory of one need.

The manager hangs one rule on the wall: after use, let the floor appear again.

### Page 12

One winter night, the manager receives a call: heating pipes have failed in three nearby apartment buildings, and repairs will take at least two days. He unlocks a side door, staff wheel in folding beds, and older residents, children, and people needing assistance enter through the same passage.

The rooms with names fill quickly. The clinic manages medication for chronic conditions; the kitchen boils water; classrooms shelter families who need quiet. Within hours, the central hall changes shape.

Folding beds follow the floor markings. Movable partitions create changing areas. Charging tables stand near the connections, and hot-drink carts roll directly through the wide doors. Interpreters, repair workers, and residents looking for family move among them without first tearing down a wall built for some other purpose.

That night, the white field on the plan acquires many colours.

### Page 13

The hall did not predict the emergency, nor was it useful because “nothing was done” to it.

Its usefulness came from prepared indeterminacy. Boundaries had been built, routes kept clear, and resources made connectable, while the centre had not been permanently occupied by one scheme. Because the designers gave up predicting every future event, the building could respond to an event they had not predicted.

This is not the same kind of emptiness preserved as the “unknown” in Chapter Ten, but the restraint is related. The previous chapter refused to disguise a lack of evidence as an answer. This chapter refuses to fill the life not yet arrived with one authorised use. Both forms of openness keep a present judgment from occupying the whole future.

### Page 14

When the hot-drink cart enters the hall for a second round, daylight has reached the high windows of the former depot. The finance director follows the cart between the beds, sets a clay cup of hot water by the window, and turns to inspect the routes still open across the floor.

“So the blank itself is the function?” he asks the architect.

“The blank did not accomplish this alone,” she replies. “Without a roof, people would be cold. Without power and water, the hall would fail within a night. Without workers, width would become disorder. The visible things hold an opening in place, and the opening lets them be rearranged.”

He watches people pass through the doors. “Form gives it a boundary. Emptiness gives it change.”

### Page 15

When heating is restored, the beds, partitions, and cables are put away one by one. Many donated supplies remain, and someone suggests leaving them in the middle of the hall to save moving them next time.

The manager returns them to side storage. Not because the hall must preserve an immaculate appearance, but because useful openness is easily consumed by everything placed there “for now.” Maintaining space requires labour and shared discipline, just as walls and equipment do.

Calendars, organisations, and conversations have similar centres. If every minute is assigned in advance, surprise has nowhere to land. If every member can perform only a fixed role, new ability struggles to emerge. If each pause is immediately covered by another sentence, understanding finds no door through which to enter.

Leaving room does not reject life. It refuses to allow life only the shape already planned for it.

## Return to the Original

### Page 16

The wheel, vessel, and room allow movement, receiving, and dwelling.

Spokes, clay walls, and built walls are what exists: they bear, enclose, and protect, giving each thing form. The hub opening, vessel hollow, and interior room are what does not: they let an axle pass, water and grain enter, and bodies, light, and air find a place. Without form, openness cannot be preserved. Without openness, form cannot complete its work.

“What exists provides benefit; what does not provides use” does not divide the world into valuable and valueless halves. It shows that function often begins where solid form agrees to stop.

### Page 17

Several days later, the hall is open again. Pale rectangles remain where tape has been lifted from the floor: a bed stood here, someone waited for news there, and a cup filled with hot water rested near the window.

The open space keeps what happened and remains available for its next use.

A vessel uses clay to enclose a hollow that can receive; a room uses walls to enclose a place where life can occur. When the boundary is completed with care, the opening can receive people and events still to come, and a door gives those possibilities an entrance.
