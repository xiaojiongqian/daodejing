# 第005-1章：天地不仁

## 原文

### 第1页

天地不仁，以万物为刍狗；圣人不仁，以百姓为刍狗。天地之间，其犹橐籥乎？虚而不屈，动而愈出。多言数穷，不如守中。

### 第2页

“天地不仁”写天地依共同法则运行，万物都在这套条件中生长和衰败；“圣人不仁”则要求治理者把私人情感放在公共尺度之后。刍狗是古代祭礼中的草扎狗，礼前安置，礼毕撤去，其待遇随仪式的阶段改变。天地之间如橐籥，腹中留空，拉动便送出气流。言语耗尽判断时，人应回到内在的中道。

## 释义

### 第3页

“天地不仁”说天地依照共同条件容纳万物，人的偏爱与恩怨都在这套尺度之外。草木鸟兽各自生长、成形、衰败，每一物都要经历变化。

### 第4页

“圣人不仁”要求治理保持共同尺度。一己之爱退居公共判断之后，亲近者与疏远者都受同一秩序约束。

## 术语与考据简注

### 第5页

**刍狗**：古代祭礼中常用的草扎狗形，祭祀完毕便弃置，象征祭礼的功用是暂时性的，不应执著于物。

**橐籥**：古代风箱，空而不满，鼓动时气息愈出，意在说明虚实相生、动静相成。

**守中**：保持内心的平衡与稳住，不为外在多言、争辩、欲求所耗。

## 正文

### 第6页

这一章把“不仁”落在共同尺度上。祭礼开始，执事把刍狗安置在席前；礼仪结束，他又将它撤下。两次动作都服从仪式阶段，人的眷恋留在公共程序之外。

### 第7页

天地给万物同一片风雨，草木便依各自根性生长。圣人治理天下，也让私人情感退到共同尺度之后：先核实事实，再依规则处置，亲疏因此受到同样约束。

### 第8页

橐籥把“虚而不屈”变成一组手上的动作。人拉开风箱，空腔吸入空气；他再压下木柄，气流便送进炉膛。谈话也需要这样的余地：先听清问题，稳住判断，再把必要的话说出。

## 回扣

### 第9页

本章把力量放在空明与中道之中。人收敛偏爱、施恩与喧哗，让万物各行其道。

### 第10页

“天地不仁”与“圣人不仁”，其核心都在于“不以私心为中心”。这与《道德经》一贯强调的“无为而治”“虚而不盈”是一脉相承的。

# Chapter 005-1: Heaven and Earth Are Not Benevolent

## Original Text

### Page 1

Heaven and earth do not act from (the impulse of) any wish to be benevolent; they deal with all things as the dogs of grass are dealt with. The sages do not act from (any wish to be) benevolent; they deal with the people as the dogs of grass are dealt with.

### Page 2

May not the space between heaven and earth be compared to a bellows?

'Tis emptied, yet it loses not its power;
'Tis moved again, and sends forth air the more.
Much speech to swift exhaustion lead we see;
Your inner being guard, and keep it free.

## Interpretation

### Page 3

This chapter places all things within conditions shared under heaven and earth. Plants and animals grow, take form, and pass away under the same seasons; human affection and resentment do not alter that measure.

### Page 4

The sage likewise places private affection after the public measure. Facts are established, rules are applied, and those near and far remain subject to the same order.

## Key Terms and Notes

### Page 5

**Straw dogs**: In ancient ritual usage, straw dogs were temporary objects used in sacrifice and then discarded. The point is not attachment but function.

**Bellows**: An instrument that remains empty yet produces ever more air when worked. The image suggests that emptiness and movement are the source of power.

**Guard the inner being**: Keep the mind centered and undistracted, avoiding excessive speech and agitation.

## Main Text

### Page 6

The straw dog makes the point tangible. An attendant arranges it before the rite, performs the ceremony, and removes it when the rite is complete. Each action follows the stage of the ritual, leaving personal attachment outside the public procedure.

### Page 7

Heaven and earth give all things the same wind and rain, and each grows according to its roots. The sage places private attachment behind a common measure: establish the facts, apply the rule, and let closeness and distance receive equal treatment.

### Page 8

The bellows turns openness into a sequence of actions. A hand pulls the chamber open and air enters; the handle presses down and air moves toward the fire. Speech needs similar room: hear the question, steady judgment, and then say what the matter requires.

## Return to the Original

### Page 9

Thus the chapter teaches governance and cultivation through a common measure. Private attachment recedes, while the needs of the whole remain present in judgment.

### Page 10

The chapter teaches disciplined non-attachment: act without clinging, govern by a common measure, and preserve the inner center so that speech remains answerable to the matter at hand.
