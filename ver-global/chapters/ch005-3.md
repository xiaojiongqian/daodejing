# 第005-3章：不仁与无我

## 原文

### 第1页

天地不仁，以万物为刍狗；圣人不仁，以百姓为刍狗。天地之间，其犹橐籥乎？虚而不屈，动而愈出。多言数穷，不如守中。

### 第2页

天地以共同尺度对待万物；圣人则把私爱留在公共秩序之外。无为由此为情感划出制度边界。

## 释义

### 第3页

这一章把“仁”从私人情感放到公共秩序中重新审视。人可以怀有怜爱，治理者则需警惕借仁爱之名施舍、偏袒，使公共事务围绕个人意志运转。

### 第4页

“天地不仁”所指的是超越偏私。万物各自生长，天地以共同条件容纳它们。圣人效法这一点，治理时让公共规则先于私人好恶。

## 术语与考据简注

### 第5页

“刍狗”是古代祭礼中供祭的草扎狗，祭毕即弃，象征物件在仪式中的作用是暂时的，不应执著。

“橐籥”指古代风箱，空而不屈，鼓动后气息愈出，说明虚能生动。

“守中”是保持内心的平衡，不被外物和多言牵引。

## 正文

### 第6页

我们可以把“天地不仁”分成四个层次来理解。首先，它从万物共同经历的条件出发。雨水落向田野，季节推动草木生长，天地在这些运行中容纳万物。

### 第7页

其次，“不仁”限制偏私。统治者若以仁爱之名偏袒一方，私人情感便会进入公共尺度，受宠者得到例外，疏远者承担代价。

### 第8页

再次，“不仁”使人放松对自我的执著。“无我”让个人好恶离开判断中心，个体性因此得到保存，他人的判断也获得位置。

### 第9页

最后，“橐籥”与“守中”把力量放在空处。欲望挤满内心，执念便催促人反复开口，判断也在争辩中耗损。人守住中道，先让话语停下，再看清需要回应的事情，力量便有了回返之处。

## 回扣

### 第10页

这一章要求我们分清怜爱与支配。爱进入人与人的关系，可以安慰、照料并陪伴；公共尺度则依据事实和规则处理事务。个人保存情感，社会也免受某个人亲疏好恶的支配。

# Chapter 005-3: Non-Benevolence and Selflessness

## Original Text

### Page 1

Heaven and earth do not act from (the impulse of) any wish to be benevolent; they deal with all things as the dogs of grass are dealt with. The sages do not act from (any wish to be) benevolent; they deal with the people as the dogs of grass are dealt with.

### Page 2

May not the space between heaven and earth be compared to a bellows?

'Tis emptied, yet it loses not its power;
'Tis moved again, and sends forth air the more.
Much speech to swift exhaustion lead we see;
Your inner being guard, and keep it free.

## Interpretation

### Page 3

This chapter reconsiders benevolence by moving from private feeling to public order. Human affection retains its place, while rulers must guard against using its language to dispense favors, show partiality, and make public affairs revolve around personal will.

### Page 4

When Laozi says that heaven and earth are “not benevolent,” he means that they are not ruled by partiality. All things grow and complete themselves in their own way; heaven and earth do not favor one thing over another. The sage governs by following this example and refusing to replace public order with private preference.

## Key Terms and Notes

### Page 5

“Straw dogs” were ritual objects used in sacrifice and then discarded, suggesting that their function is temporary rather than something to be clung to.

“A bellows” is an image of emptiness and movement, showing that power comes from vacancy and openness rather than fullness.

“Guard the inner being” means keeping the mind centered and not being pulled off course by external noise and excess speech.

## Main Text

### Page 6

We can understand “heaven and earth are not benevolent” in four layers. First, the phrase begins from conditions shared by living things. Rain falls across fields, seasons move plants through growth, and heaven and earth receive all things through these processes.

### Page 7

Second, “non-benevolence” restrains partiality. If a ruler uses the language of benevolence to favor one group, private feeling enters the public measure: favorites receive exceptions and distant people bear the cost.

### Page 8

Third, “non-benevolence” loosens attachment to the self. Personal likes and dislikes leave the center of judgment; individuality remains, and the judgment of others gains room.

### Page 9

Finally, the bellows and the inner center place power in open space. Desire crowds the mind, fixation drives repeated speech, and judgment wears down in argument. Keeping the middle allows speech to pause, the matter to come into view, and strength to return.

## Return to the Original

### Page 10

The chapter asks us to distinguish care from control. Affection enters human relationships through comfort, attention, and companionship; the public measure handles affairs through facts and rules. People retain feeling, while society gains protection from one person’s preferences.
