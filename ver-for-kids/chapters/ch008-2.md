# 儿童道德经第八章：其二 王座前的潮水

## 第1页

有一次，孔子站在河边，看水日夜不停地流。

他说，逝去的时间就像这条河，不分白天黑夜，一直往前。

## 第2页

孔子看水，看见的是“不断”：不要白白放过日子，要向前，要珍惜。

老子看水，又看见另一面：水不争高处，不急着显眼，却能滋养万物、待在低处。

## 第3页

很远很远的北方，也流传着一个关于水的传奇。

很久以前，欧洲有一段时候叫中世纪。那时有一位国王，名字常译作克努特。他管着英格兰、丹麦等地方，权力很大，身边也有许多爱说好听话的人。

## 第4页

那些人常夸他：“陛下，您的命令谁敢不听？陆地听您的，城堡听您的，军队听您的，连大海也该听您的。”

这样的夸奖听多了，会让人忘记自己只是人，不是天地的主人。

## 第5页

传说有一天，克努特让人把王座搬到海边。

那时正是涨潮的时候。天空低低的，海面一层一层推来，白色浪花像一队沉默的士兵。

## 第6页

国王坐在王座上，对海潮说：“到此为止，不许再往前，不许弄湿我的脚和袍子。”

侍臣们站在后面，屏住呼吸。谁也不知道海会不会给国王让路。

## 第7页

海没有发怒，也没有回答。

它按自己的时间来，一直往上涨。先湿了沙子，再碰到王座的脚，最后漫过国王的鞋，沾上他的袍边。

## 第8页

克努特站起来，退到干处。传说里，他说：“你们看，国王的权力也是有限的，海潮不听我的命令。”

更可信的说法是，他借大海提醒那些只会说好听话的人。海水一寸一寸上来，比一百句劝告更清楚。

## 第9页

海潮没有和国王争吵，也没有为了讨好王冠停下。到了时候，它来；到了时候，它退。

这正好照见“动善时”：掌声和命令，都拉不偏水的时间。

## 第10页

也照见“不争”。不争，是不把力气花在吵赢上，也不是谁的命令都听。

水不和王座辩论，却让所有人看见：王座命令不了潮水。

## 第11页

再看一句古话：“水唯能下方成海，山不矜高自及天。”水肯往低处走，才汇成大海；山不夸自己高，才一直通到天上。

海在低处，河流才有地方汇入。

## 第12页

如果每条小河都争着爬山顶，就没有大海。山泉、溪流、江河肯往下走，才有共同的归处。

山稳稳立在那里，经着风雨霜雪，也不用天天说“我最高”。

## 第13页

“居善地”，海在低处，众水才有地方汇入；王座被人抬高，潮水却不听命令。

“心善渊”，深水不急着解释。人在岸上把权力喊得越大，潮水越安静地显出边界。

## 第14页

“言善信”，是潮水用湿鞋给出的事实：它不说谎，也不讨好，大自然不会因好听的话改变时间。

“夫唯不争，故无尤”，潮水没有争吵，只照自己的时间来去，让人自己明白。

## 第15页

阿林想起后山的小溪，又想起海边的王座。小溪不抢石阶，潮水不听王座命令；水知道该往哪里去、什么时候动、能滋养什么。

做水一样的人，脚下低，心里深，该动时动得正好。别人夸你、命令你、催你，你仍可稳住脚步，像潮水按自己的道来去。

# Chapter 008-2: The Tide Before the Throne

## Page 1

Once, Confucius stood beside a river and watched the water flow day and night.

He said that passing time was like this river, moving forward without stopping by day or night.

## Page 2

Confucius looked at water and saw how it keeps going. He thought of days passing and the need to use them well.

Laozi looked at water and saw another side: water does not fight for the high place or hurry to be seen, yet it helps all things and stays low.

## Page 3

Far away in the north, there is also a legend about water.

Long ago, during the Middle Ages in Europe, there lived a king often called Cnut or Canute. He ruled England, Denmark, and other lands, and many people around him loved to praise him.

## Page 4

They said, "Your Majesty, who dares disobey your command? The land listens to you, the castles listen to you, the armies listen to you, and even the sea should listen to you."

After hearing such praise again and again, a person may forget that he is only human, not the master of heaven and earth.

## Page 5

The legend says that one day Cnut ordered his throne to be carried to the shore.

The tide was rising. The sky hung low, and the sea came in layer after layer, white foam like a silent line of soldiers.

## Page 6

The king sat on the throne and said to the tide, "Stop here. Come no farther. Do not wet my feet or my robe."

The courtiers stood behind him, holding their breath. No one knew whether the sea would make way for a king.

## Page 7

The sea did not rage, and it did not answer.

It kept coming at its own time. First it wet the sand, then touched the legs of the throne, and finally washed over the king's shoes and the edge of his robe.

## Page 8

Cnut stood up and stepped back to dry ground. In the legend, he said, "See, even a king's power has limits. The tide does not obey my command."

A more believable reading is that he used the sea to remind those who only said pleasing words. The water rising inch by inch was clearer than a hundred warnings.

## Page 9

The tide did not argue with the king or stop to please the crown. When the time came, it came; when the time passed, it withdrew.

This shows "good movement is timely": applause and orders cannot pull water away from its own time.

## Page 10

It also shows not striving. Not striving means spending no strength on winning an argument, but it does not mean obeying every order.

Water did not debate the throne, yet everyone saw that a throne cannot command the tide.

## Page 11

Now look at an old saying: water becomes the sea because it is willing to go low; a mountain reaches the sky without boasting of height.

The sea lies low, so rivers have a place to flow in.

## Page 12

If every little river tried to climb to the mountain top, there would be no sea. Springs, streams, and rivers find a shared home by going downhill.

A mountain stands through wind, rain, frost, and snow. It does not need to say every day, "I am the highest."

## Page 13

A good dwelling is in the fitting place. The sea lies low, so all waters have a place to enter; the throne is raised by people, yet the tide ignores its command.

A good mind is deep. Deep water does not hurry to explain itself. The louder people boast of their power, the more quietly the tide shows the limits of that power.

## Page 14

"Good words are trustworthy" appears in the tide's answer. The tide did not lie or flatter. Water over the king's shoes showed everyone that grand words cannot change nature's timing.

"Because it does not wrangle, no one finds fault." The tide did not quarrel. It came and went in its own time, letting people understand for themselves.

## Page 15

Lin thought of the stream behind the hill and the throne by the sea. The stream did not fight for the stone steps; the tide did not obey the throne. Water knows where to go, when to move, and what it can nourish.

To be like water is to stay low in posture, deep in heart, and move at the right time. When others praise, command, or hurry you, keep your footing and move quietly with the Way, as the tide does.
