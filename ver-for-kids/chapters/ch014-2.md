# 儿童道德经第十四章：其二 蜜蜂撞着亮窗纸

## 第1页

禅宗里有一个“蜂子投窗”的公案。公案，是禅宗里让人琢磨、参想的故事。

故事说，古灵神赞禅师悟道以后，回去看从前教他的老和尚。老和尚还在窗下读经，读得很用功。

## 第2页

那时的窗上糊着纸，不像今天许多透明玻璃窗。外面的光透进来，白白亮亮。

屋子旁边其实有门，门是开着的。

## 第3页

一只蜜蜂飞进屋里，想出去。

它看见窗纸很亮，以为那就是外面的天空。于是它直直撞过去，嗡的一声，又弹回来。

## 第4页

它不死心。

再撞一次，再弹回来。翅膀急急地振，身体一次次贴到纸上。亮光就在眼前，可就是出不去。

## 第5页

古灵神赞看见了，借蜜蜂提醒老和尚。

他的大意是：敞开的门就在旁边，蜜蜂却偏要钻旧窗纸；人如果只在旧纸堆里打转，不肯回头看看活路，也很难找到出路。

## 第6页

老和尚读的经书很宝贵，可眼睛只盯着纸上的亮，好字也会变成挡路的纸。

蜜蜂相信自己的眼睛：这里最亮，这里就是路。它一直撞的地方，却没有出口。

## 第7页

阿林听完，想到自己画“道”的事：“所以，看见的不一定是真路？”

老人说：“看见的也可能是真路，只是不能只靠眼睛。窗纸亮，门也开。还要问一问：这条路通不通？”

## 第8页

小石头问：“那蜜蜂为什么不回头？”

老人说：“急的时候，谁都容易这样。越撞越痛，越痛越觉得再撞一下就能出去。”

## 第9页

阿林想起，做题做不出时，他常盯着同一行字；找东西找不到时，他只翻同一个抽屉；和朋友吵架时，他只想证明自己没错。

这些时候，旁边也许就有一扇门。心里一急，眼睛就只看见那张亮亮的窗纸。

## 第10页

老人带孩子们去学堂，门边有一只小虫，也在窗纸上乱撞。

阿林没有用手去抓，只轻轻把门再推开一点，又把窗边的灯挪远。

## 第11页

小虫在纸上停了一会儿。

窗边暗了一些，门口的风清楚起来。它绕了一个小圈，飞向门外。

## 第12页

小石头低声说：“原来帮它，不是替它撞破纸。”

阿林说：“是让它看见门。”

## 第13页

第十四章说“视之不见名曰夷，听之不闻名曰希，搏之不得名曰微”。

蜜蜂的故事提醒我们：眼睛看见的亮，不一定就是最真实的路。耳边最大的嗡嗡声，也不一定告诉你出口在哪里。

## 第14页

“搏之不得”，也很像这只蜜蜂。

它拼命用身体去撞，好像只要更用力，就能抓到自由。可有些问题，越用力越紧；换一个方向，门就出现了。

## 第15页

“执古之道，以御今之有”，是把前人留下的线索拿在手里，往前走。古书、经文、经验，都是这样的线索。

读书要读，生活也要走进去；亮的地方要看，开着的门也要看。拿线索是为了走出去，若一直撞同一张窗纸，就算窗纸很亮，也不是道。

# Chapter 014-2: The Bee Striking the Bright Window Paper

## Page 1

In Chan tradition, there is a koan — a story meant for pondering — about a bee striking a paper window.

The story says that after the monk Guling Shenzan awakened, he returned to visit the old monk who had once taught him. The old monk was still reading scriptures by the window, working very hard.

## Page 2

Windows then were covered with paper, unlike the clear glass in many windows today. Outside light shone through in a pale brightness.

Beside the room, there was actually a door, and the door was open.

## Page 3

A bee flew into the room and wanted to get out.

It saw the bright window paper and thought that must be the sky outside. It flew straight at it, buzzed, struck, and bounced back.

## Page 4

It did not give up.

It struck again and bounced back again. Its wings beat urgently, and its body hit the paper again and again. The brightness was right before it, but it could not get out.

## Page 5

Guling Shenzan saw this and used the bee to warn the old monk.

His meaning was: an open door is right beside you, but the bee insists on drilling into old window paper. If people keep circling in old paper and never turn back to look at the living way, they cannot easily find a way out.

## Page 6

The scriptures the old monk read were precious. Yet if his eyes stayed fixed on brightness on paper, even good words could become a sheet blocking the road.

The bee trusted its eyes: this is brightest, so this must be the way. The place it kept striking had no exit.

## Page 7

Lin thought of his attempt to draw the Dao. "So what we see is not always the true road?"

The old man said, "What we see may be a true road, but we cannot rely only on our eyes. The window paper is bright, and the door is open. We must also ask: does this road go through?"

## Page 8

Little Stone asked, "Why did the bee not turn around?"

The old man said, "When we are anxious, we all become like that. The more it hurts, the more we feel that one more strike will get us out."

## Page 9

Lin remembered staring at the same line when he was stuck on a problem, searching the same drawer when something was lost, and trying only to prove himself right during an argument.

At such times, there may be an open door nearby. An anxious heart lets the eyes see only the bright paper.

## Page 10

The old man brought the children to the schoolroom. Near the door, a small insect was striking the paper window.

Lin did not catch it with his hand. He gently pushed the door wider and moved the lamp away from the window.

## Page 11

The insect paused on the paper.

With the window dimmer, the breeze at the doorway stood out. It circled once and flew outside.

## Page 12

Little Stone whispered, "So we helped it without breaking the paper."

Lin said, "We helped it see the door."

## Page 13

Chapter Fourteen says that we look and do not see, listen and do not hear, grasp and do not hold.

The bee story reminds us that the brightness seen by the eyes is not always the truest road. The loudest buzzing near the ear does not always tell us where the exit is.

## Page 14

"We try to grasp it and do not get hold of it" also sounds like this bee.

It struck with its whole body, as if greater force could seize freedom. But some problems only tighten with force. Turn, and a door appears.

## Page 15

"Holding the Tao of old to direct what exists today" means taking the clues left by those before us and walking on. Old books, scriptures, and experience are such clues.

Read books, and also enter life. Look at the bright place, and also at the open door. We hold a clue in order to walk out. If you keep striking the same window paper, even bright paper is not the Dao.
