# 儿童道德经第十三章：其二 两只手臂和一张契书

## 第1页

《庄子》里有一个故事：子华子去见韩国的国君。那时，韩国和魏国正为一块土地相争，国君满脸忧色。

子华子没有先责备他，只拿出一个比方，像把一把秤摆到国君面前。

## 第2页

他说：“假如有一张契书——就是古人写的字据——上面写着：你用左手拿它，右手就废掉；你用右手拿它，左手就废掉。”

“可是只要拿到它，你就能得到整个天下。你拿不拿？”

## 第3页

天下像所有城门、河流、田地和道路加在一起。一座房子、一车粮食，都装不下它。

这个问题很大，听起来，谁不心动？

## 第4页

国君立刻说：“不拿。”

没有手臂，怎么穿衣、吃饭、写字、扶人、抱孩子？天下再大，也不能替一只废掉的手拿起碗。

## 第5页

子华子这才往下说：“两只手臂，比天下还贵重。”

“人的身体，又比两只手臂更贵重。”

## 第6页

国君听着，脸上的忧色慢慢变了。

子华子继续说：“天下已经比你的身体轻，韩国又比天下轻。现在你所争的那块地，比韩国还轻。”

## 第7页

“为了一块更轻的地，愁坏更贵重的身，值得吗？”

国君叹了一口气。他还得治理国家，心里的秤却摆正了：身体、生命、国家、土地，各有轻重。

## 第8页

他原以为自己在为国家忧虑。

可忧虑若烧坏身体、搅乱判断，最后也未必帮得到国家。被小得失牵走的人，很难照看大的事情。

## 第9页

阿林听到这里，摸了摸自己的两只手。

一只手有一点墨迹，另一只手指甲里还有泥。它们很普通，却能翻书、种豆，还能扶小石头骑车。

## 第10页

小石头问：“那是不是为了身体，什么都不要做？”

老人说：“子华子是帮国君看清轻重。若为面子气坏身体，为争一点东西丢了睡眠、饭食和判断，就把轻重弄反了。”

## 第11页

阿林想起自己为红绸带吃不下饭。

那只是一场鼓舞，他的心却像个小国君，守着一块小地，愁得忘了身体。

## 第12页

老人让孩子们做选择。

“给你一箱金珠，但换走你今天所有的睡眠，你换不换？给你一面最大的小旗，但让你再也不能跑，你换不换？”

## 第13页

孩子们先笑，后来都摇头。有些东西听起来很亮，放到身体旁边，分量就轻了。

第十三章说“贵大患若身”。人有身体，所以会有忧患；也知道生命不能随便拿去换身外之物。

## 第14页

“贵以身为天下，若可寄天下”，不是自私。

真正珍惜身体和生命的人，才不会轻易让战争、面子、贪心把许多人拖进伤害里。

## 第15页

子华子把轻重摆在一张契书里：天下很大，两臂更贴身；国家很重，生命更根本。

手臂能抱人，心能清楚地判断。先不把这些弄丢，才照看得了更大的事。

# Chapter 013-2: Two Arms and a Written Deed

## Page 1

In the Zhuangzi, Zihuazi visits the ruler of Han. At that time, Han and Wei were contending over a piece of land, and the ruler's face was full of worry.

Zihuazi did not begin by scolding him. He offered a comparison, as if placing a scale before the ruler.

## Page 2

He said, "Suppose there is a written deed — a paper that makes a promise official — and it says: take it with your left hand, and your right arm will be ruined; take it with your right hand, and your left arm will be ruined."

"But if you take it, you will gain the whole world. Would you take it?"

## Page 3

The world is like all the gates, rivers, fields, and roads added together. Neither a house nor a cart of grain could hold it.

It was a huge question. Who would not be moved by such an offer?

## Page 4

The ruler immediately said, "I would not take it."

Without arms, how could one dress, eat, write, help someone, or hold a child? Even if you owned the world, a ruined hand still could not lift your bowl.

## Page 5

Only then did Zihuazi continue. "Two arms are more precious than the whole world."

"And a person's body is more precious than the two arms."

## Page 6

The ruler listened, and the worry on his face slowly changed.

Zihuazi went on. "If the world is lighter than your body, then Han is lighter than the world. And the land you are fighting over is lighter still."

## Page 7

"For a lighter piece of land, is it worth damaging the more precious body?"

The ruler sighed. He still had a state to govern, but the scale in his heart was level again: body, life, state, and land each had its weight.

## Page 8

He had thought he was worrying for the state.

Yet worry that burns the body and muddles judgment may not help the state at all. A person dragged along by small gain and loss cannot easily care for great matters.

## Page 9

When Lin heard this, he touched his two hands.

One had a little ink on it; the other had mud under the nails. They were ordinary hands, but they could turn pages, plant beans, and help Little Stone ride a bicycle.

## Page 10

Little Stone asked, "Then should we do nothing except protect the body?"

The old man said, "Zihuazi was helping the ruler see weight and measure. Ruining your body for face, or losing sleep, food, and judgment over a small thing, turns great and small upside down."

## Page 11

Lin remembered losing his appetite over the red drum band.

That was only a drum dance, yet his heart had become like a small ruler, guarding a small field and forgetting the body.

## Page 12

The old man asked the children to choose.

"If I give you a box of gold beads but take away all your sleep tonight, will you trade? If I give you the biggest flag but you can never run again, will you trade?"

## Page 13

At first the children laughed. Then they all shook their heads. Some things sound bright, but beside the body, their weight becomes light.

Chapter Thirteen speaks of great calamity as the body. Having bodies brings trouble, and it also teaches us that life should not be exchanged lightly for things outside us.

## Page 14

"Honoring the body as the world" is not selfishness.

One who truly cherishes body and life will not lightly let war, face, or greed drag many people into harm.

## Page 15

Zihuazi set out weight and measure in a written deed. The world is large, but two arms are closer. A state is weighty, but life is more basic.

Arms can hold people, and the heart can judge clearly. Keep these safe, and we can care for larger things.
