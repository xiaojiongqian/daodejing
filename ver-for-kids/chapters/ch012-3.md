# 儿童道德经第十二章：其三 塞壬的歌声

## 第1页

荷马史诗《奥德赛》里，特洛伊战争结束后，奥德修斯带着水手回家。他们已经离家很久，最想看见故乡的屋顶、港口和亲人的脸。

归家的海路上有一座危险的岛，岛上住着塞壬。传说她们的歌声美得让人忘记一切。船只一靠近，水手就会跟着歌声走，忘了礁石、船和回家的路。

## 第2页

奥德修斯想听那歌声，也知道危险。

他没有对自己说：“我意志很强，听一听也没事。”

## 第3页

他做了一件很奇怪、也很聪明的事。

他让水手们用蜡封住耳朵，这样他们听不见歌声。又让他们把自己绑在桅杆上，绑得结结实实。

## 第4页

他还提前吩咐：“等会儿不管我怎么喊、怎么求，你们都不要给我松绑。”

水手们点头。那时海面很平，风也正好。危险还没来，人的心比较清楚。

## 第5页

船慢慢靠近塞壬岛。远处先是一点声音，像银线落到水面；接着歌声越来越近，甜而亮。

歌声一进耳朵，奥德修斯刚才的计划就全忘了。他忘了船旁边可能有礁石，也忘了故乡还在前面。

## 第6页

他拼命挣扎，喊水手给他解开。

水手们耳朵里堵着蜡，看见船长在桅杆上发急。他们记得先前的约定，反倒把绳子绑得更紧。

## 第7页

歌声还在追。奥德修斯向海那边扑，绳子勒住他的胸口。

船继续往前，礁石一点点退到后面。等歌声远去，水手们取下耳中的蜡，海面又只剩桨声和风声。

## 第8页

他们这才解开奥德修斯。

他喘着气，低头看身上的绳痕。那些绳子刚才让他难受，却也把他留在了活着的船上。

## 第9页

小石头听到这里，皱起眉：“他不是英雄吗？英雄也会被歌声骗走？”

老人说：“所以他才聪明。他知道自己也会糊涂，就趁清醒的时候，先给糊涂的自己留下一条绳子。”

## 第10页

阿林想起集市上的鼓声和蓝珠子。

那天他的耳朵也被塞得满满的。小石头明明叫过他，可叫卖声太近、太亮，把朋友的声音挤到很远。

## 第11页

老人说：“有些声音，会把人从自己的路上叫走。”

“真正会听的人，会先挑一挑：哪些声音可以听，哪些声音要隔远一点。”

## 第12页

第十二章说“五音令人耳聋”。塞壬的歌声灌满奥德修斯的耳朵，让他忘了礁石、同伴和回家的方向。

“驰骋畋猎令人心发狂”，也可以这样看：人被刺激追着跑，把眼前的甜声当成全部，便忘了自己原本要去哪里。

## 第13页

“圣人为腹不为目”，在海上就是：先保住船，再保住身体，最后保住回家的路。

歌声再美，也不能拿全船人的性命去换。

## 第14页

“下次去集市，要是我光顾着看蓝珠子，忘了回家，你就拉我的袖子。”阿林对小石头说。

趁着清醒，先给容易被带走的自己留一条绳子。有些歌很好听，可回家的路，比那一刻的好听更重要。

# Chapter 012-3: The Sirens' Song

## Page 1

In Homer's Odyssey, Odysseus and his sailors were sailing home after the Trojan War. They had been away for a long time and longed to see the roofs of home, the harbor, and the faces of those waiting for them.

On the way lay a dangerous island, home to the Sirens. Their song was said to be so beautiful that people forgot everything. When ships came near, sailors followed the sound and forgot the rocks, their ship, and the road home.

## Page 2

Odysseus wanted to hear the song, and he knew its danger.

He did not say to himself, "My will is strong. I can listen and be fine."

## Page 3

He did something strange and wise.

He had the sailors seal their ears with wax so they could not hear the song. Then he ordered them to bind him firmly to the mast.

## Page 4

He told them beforehand, "No matter how I shout or beg later, do not untie me."

The sailors nodded. At that time, the sea was calm and the wind was good. Danger had not arrived yet, so the heart could still think clearly.

## Page 5

The ship slowly came near the Sirens' island. At first, the distant song was like a silver thread touching the water. Then it came closer, sweet and bright.

As soon as the song entered his ears, Odysseus forgot his own plan. He forgot the rocks beside the ship and forgot that home still lay ahead.

## Page 6

He struggled hard and shouted for the sailors to untie him.

The sailors' ears were sealed with wax, and they saw their captain straining against the mast. Remembering his earlier command, they tied the ropes tighter.

## Page 7

The song kept following. Odysseus threw himself toward the sea, and the ropes held his chest.

The ship kept moving, and the rocks slipped behind them. When the song faded, the sailors took the wax from their ears, and the sea held only the sounds of oars and wind.

## Page 8

Only then did they untie Odysseus.

He breathed hard and looked down at the marks of the rope. The ropes had hurt him, but they had kept him alive on the ship.

## Page 9

Little Stone frowned. "Wasn't he a hero? Can a hero be fooled by a song too?"

The old man said, "That is why he was wise. He knew he could become confused, so while he was clear, he left a rope for the confused self."

## Page 10

Lin remembered the market drums and the blue bead.

That day, his ears had been brimming too. Little Stone had called him, but the seller's voice was too near and too bright, and it pushed his friend's voice far away.

## Page 11

The old man said, "Some sounds call people away from their own road."

"A person who truly knows how to listen picks and chooses. He knows which sounds to hear, and which sounds to keep at a distance."

## Page 12

Chapter Twelve says that many notes can make the ears deaf. The Sirens' song filled Odysseus's ears until he forgot the rocks, his crew, and the way home.

The line about chasing excitement until the heart grows wild can also be read this way: when people are pulled by thrills, the sweet sound in front becomes everything, and they forget where they meant to go.

## Page 13

"For the belly, not for the eyes" at sea means: first protect the ship, then the body, and then the road home.

No song, however beautiful, is worth the lives of the whole crew.

## Page 14

“Next time we go to the market, if I get so busy looking at the blue beads that I forget to go home, tug my sleeve,” Lin told Little Stone.

While his mind was clear, he could leave a rope for the self that might be carried away. Some songs are beautiful, but the road home matters more than one moment of beauty.
