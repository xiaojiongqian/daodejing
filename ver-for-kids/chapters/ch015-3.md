# 儿童道德经第十五章：其三 浑水慢慢清

## 第1页

第十五章有一句话，适合放在一杯浑水旁边看：

“孰能浊以静之徐清？”谁能让浑水安静下来，慢慢变清？

## 第2页

暴雨后，空空村的小池塘变成了黄泥色。

水面漂着断草，泥沙在底下翻。平时爱照影子的白鹅站在岸边，不肯下去。

## 第3页

阿林想帮池水快点清，拿来长竿挑草叶。

竿子刚伸进水里，池底的泥又翻上来，水更浑了。

## 第4页

小石头拿来布袋：“我们把水滤一遍。”

两人舀起一桶水倒进去，细泥从布缝里渗出来，手上、鞋上、地上全成了黄的。忙了半天，池塘没有清，阿林有点恼：“为什么越帮越乱？”

## 第5页

老人舀起一碗浑水，放在石阶上：“先看这一碗。”

泥点像小小的云，草屑上下浮。阿林想伸手去碰，老人摇摇头：“别急。”

## 第6页

过了一会儿，重的泥慢慢沉下去。水面先清了一点，接着中间也亮了，碗底聚起一层细细的黄泥。

阿林小声说：“它自己会清。”

老人说：“先别乱管。浑的时候，最怕急手。”

## 第7页

他们清开池塘边的水口，让水重新慢慢流动，又捞走风吹来的断枝。

该做的事做完，长竿就靠在了树边，谁也没再往池底插。

## 第8页

第二天，浅处已经能看见石头。第三天，白鹅试着下水，脚掌划开一圈圈水纹，没再翻起多少泥。

第四天早晨，阿林在池边看见自己的眉毛和鼻子。影子还带点迷糊，像刚睡醒。

## 第9页

老人说：“心也是这样。吵架以后，受委屈以后，太兴奋以后，心里常常都是泥。”

“心里正乱，就急着判断、回嘴，或做大决定，就像拿长竿搅池底。”

## 第10页

小石头问：“那是不是只要坐着不动？”

老人说：“先静，是让泥沉下去；后来还要动，是让水重新流起来。”

## 第11页

他们把清一点的池水引向菜畦。水流急了会掀起苗根，大家便挖了浅沟，让水慢慢走。

阿林看着小苗喝水：先让泥沉下来，再让清一点的水流出去。心静下来以后，合适的动作再慢慢生出来，这就叫“徐生”。

## 第12页

傍晚，老人拿出一只旧木勺。勺柄有裂纹，已经用细铜丝缠过，舀水正稳。

小石头问：“为什么不换新的？”

老人说：“它还能用。爱护旧东西，心就不必天天求满、求新、求一下子变得了不起。”

## 第13页

“孰能浊以静之徐清”，阿林在池塘边看见了。风雨会搅浑水，事情太多、情绪太急会搅浑心；风雨过去，水又能照见眉毛和鼻子。

“孰能安以久动之徐生”，也在菜畦里。静下来以后，清水要流向需要它的地方，安下来的心也要去做该做的事。

## 第14页

“保此道者不欲盈”，像那只旧木勺。

人无须装成永远崭新、永远完美。裂过的地方能修好，旧东西也能有长久的用处。

## 第15页

浑的时候，先别急着证明自己清。

静一静，泥会沉；清一点，再慢慢动。水这样，人心也可以这样。

# Chapter 015-3: Muddy Water Slowly Clears

## Page 1

A line in Chapter Fifteen belongs beside a cup of muddy water:

"Who can make muddy water clear? Let it be still, and it will gradually become clear."

## Page 2

After a storm, the small pond in Kongkong Village turned yellow with mud.

Broken grass floated on the surface, and silt moved below. The white goose, who usually loved looking at her reflection, stood on the bank and refused to go in.

## Page 3

Lin wanted to help the pond clear faster, so he brought a long pole to lift out the grass.

As soon as he pushed it into the water, mud rose from the bottom, and the pond became even muddier.

## Page 4

Little Stone brought a cloth bag. "Let's filter the water."

They poured in a bucket of water, and fine mud leaked through the cloth, turning their hands, shoes, and the ground yellow. After half a day, the pond was still muddy. Lin grew annoyed. "Why does helping make it messier?"

## Page 5

The old man filled a bowl with muddy water and set it on the stone step. "Watch this bowl first."

Mud dots moved like tiny clouds, and bits of grass floated up and down. Lin put out his hand, but the old man shook his head. "Do not hurry."

## Page 6

After a while, the heavier mud slowly sank. The top cleared first, then the middle brightened, and a thin layer of yellow silt gathered at the bottom.

Lin whispered, "It can clear by itself."

The old man said, "First, do not manage it wildly. When things are muddy, hurried hands cause trouble."

## Page 7

They cleared the opening at the edge of the pond so the water could move slowly again, then removed the branches blown in by the wind.

With the needed work done, the long pole rested against a tree. No one pushed it into the pond bottom again.

## Page 8

The next day, stones could be seen in the shallows. On the third day, the white goose tried the water. Her feet made ripples but stirred up little mud.

On the fourth morning, Lin could see his eyebrows and nose in the pond. His reflection still looked hazy, as if it had just woken up.

## Page 9

The old man said, "The heart is like this too. After an argument, hurt feelings, or too much excitement, it is often muddy."

"Judging, answering back, or making a big decision while the heart is still stirred up is like stirring the pond bottom."

## Page 10

Little Stone asked, "Then should we just sit still?"

The old man said, "First, stillness lets the mud settle. Later, movement lets the water live again."

## Page 11

They guided some clearer pond water toward the vegetable patch. A strong flow would tear up the roots, so they dug shallow channels and let the water move slowly.

Lin watched the seedlings drink. First let the mud settle; then let the clearer water flow. When the heart is settled, the right action can slowly grow. This is gradual life.

## Page 12

At dusk, the old man took out an old wooden ladle. Its cracked handle had been bound with thin copper wire, and it scooped water steadily.

"Why not get a new one?" Little Stone asked.

"It still works," said the old man. "When we care for old things, the heart need not always seek fullness, newness, or sudden greatness."

## Page 13

Lin saw "let muddy water be still and it will gradually become clear" beside the pond. Wind and rain stir up water; too many matters and rushed feelings stir up the heart. When the storm has passed, the water can reflect eyebrows and a nose again.

"Let rest and movement gradually arise" was also in the vegetable patch. Once clearer, water should flow where it is needed. Once settled, the heart should do what needs doing.

## Page 14

"Not wishing to be full" is like the old wooden ladle.

We need not pretend to be new and perfect all the time. Cracked places can be repaired, and old things can remain useful for a long time.

## Page 15

When muddy, do not hurry to prove you are clear.

Be still, and silt will sink. When there is some clarity, move slowly. Water can do this, and so can the human heart.
