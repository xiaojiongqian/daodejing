# 儿童道德经第七章：其二 最后面的人，最前面的心

## 典故引入

### 第1页

有一家户外用品公司叫巴塔哥尼亚。创始人伊冯·乔伊纳德起初只想给攀岩朋友做耐用的工具和衣服。

公司越做越大，他也看见一个问题：生意如果只想着多卖、多赚，也会耗伤山、河和人的生活。

### 第2页

巴塔哥尼亚选择把“保护地球”放到赚钱前面。从一九八五年起，它长期把卖东西赚来的钱，拿出百分之一，捐给保护环境的人。

这看起来像先把钱让出去。可是顾客看见，这家公司在认真守护大家共同的家。

### 第3页

二零二二年，乔伊纳德一家做了一个决定：把公司交给一个专门为地球办事的机构。公司赚的钱，一部分留着继续做生意，其余的都拿去保护大自然。

它先问的是：“怎样让山、河、海和人还能继续生活？”钱不再坐在最中间，它像水一样，去浇更重要的地方。

### 第4页

这里的关键是：公司仍要卖衣服、付工资、改进产品，让生意站得住。

只是赚来的钱不再像王一样坐在王座上。它要回答一个更大的问题：我们共同的家，怎样一直被好好地用、好好地爱护？

### 第5页

所以顾客买到一件外套，也买到一种信任：这家公司不会把大家共同的家，当成挖完就丢的矿山。

信任一旦长出来，比广告还长久。

### 第6页

南非的曼德拉，被关在罗本岛监狱二十七年。白天，他在采石场工作，石灰岩的反光刺伤了眼睛。

如果只看他自己受的苦，他有太多理由把仇恨放在最前面。

### 第7页

可是出狱以后，他没有让国家跟着自己的伤口一起燃烧。他请从前看管他的狱卒来参加自己的就职典礼，也推动大家说出真相、彼此和解。

他没有忘记那些伤害，却把南非的未来放在自己的痛苦前面。这样留下来的，有他自己的生命，也有一个国家继续一起生活的可能。

### 第8页

他若把总统位置变成复仇的椅子，支持他的人也许会痛快一阵，国家却可能一直裂下去，合不起来。

他把自己的伤痛往后放，让共同未来坐到前面。于是，从前的敌人也有了一个台阶，可以放下武器。

### 第9页

中国的范仲淹写《岳阳楼记》，也把自己的心放到很远很大。

他写洞庭湖的阴晴风雨，写一座楼，也写一个人怎样看天下。

### 第10页

他留下“先天下之忧而忧，后天下之乐而乐”。忧的时候站在前面，享乐的时候退到后面。

这句话不是漂亮口号。一个人如果真的把天下人的忧愁和快乐放在自己前面，他的心就不会再困在自己的得失里。

### 第11页

范仲淹也有自己的苦和乐。他也被人说过坏话，也会觉得孤单。

可是他的心不把升官、降职当成人生的全部。所以一座楼、一片湖，能装下天下人的忧愁和快乐。

### 第12页

这三个故事有一条同样的线：越急着保住自己的钱、自己的伤痛和自己的得失，别人就越会防着你，力气也越花在互相争上。

如果先把大家共同的路守住，把自然、国家和天下放在前面，信任才有地方生根。

### 第13页

所以“后其身而身先”，讲的是人心的因果。

后退一步，是让众人看见：这里没有抢夺。人一放心，就愿意靠近，也愿意跟随。

## 回扣

### 第14页

“外其身而身存”也有因果。如果只想保住赚来的钱，巴塔哥尼亚可能失去大自然和顾客的信任；如果只想保住仇恨，曼德拉可能失去一个国家。

如果只想保住自己的得失，范仲淹也只是一位官员，不会成为后人记得的那颗宽大的心。

### 第15页

“不自生”，说的是不把孤零零的自己当成生命的中心。天地生养万物，却不把万物圈成自己的东西。

天地自己的生命，就活在万物一代一代的生长里。这就是“故能长生”。

### 第16页

所以老子说的是：一个人如果只为自己活着，就会一直和世界争；如果走进更大的生命里，就能和大家一起长久。

这就是为什么天地“不自生”，故能长生；圣人“后其身”，反而身先。

### 第17页

老子又说，外其身而身存；无私，故能成其私。“外”，说的是不让一点得失把自己围住。

一个人回到更大的生命里，成就的是要做的大事、大家一起的生活，和一颗宽广的心。

### 第18页

在孩子的生活里，也能练习：排队、分东西、承担责任时，先看大家怎样更好，再找自己站的位置。

阿禾写下：自己回到更大的生命里，像一滴水回到河里、海里，跟一直流动的生命一起长久。

# Chapter 007-2: The Last Person, the Foremost Heart

## Allusion

### Page 1

There is an outdoor company called Patagonia. Its founder, Yvon Chouinard, simply made durable tools and clothes for climbers.

As the company grew, he saw a problem: if business only tries to sell more and earn more, it can also wear down mountains, rivers, and people's lives.

### Page 2

Patagonia chose to put protecting the planet ahead of chasing money. Since nineteen eighty-five, it has taken one percent of the money it earns from selling things and given it to people who protect the environment.

That looked like giving money away first. But customers saw the company working to guard a shared home.

### Page 3

In twenty twenty-two, Chouinard's family made a decision: they handed the company to a special organization that works for the earth. Part of the money the company earns stays in the business; the rest goes to protect nature.

Its first question was, "How can mountains, rivers, seas, and people keep living?" Money no longer sits in the center. It flows like water to a more important place.

### Page 4

The point is that Patagonia still has to sell clothes, pay workers, improve products, and keep the business standing.

But the money it earns is no longer like a king on a throne. It has to answer a bigger question: how can our shared home keep being used well and loved well?

### Page 5

So customers buy a jacket, and they also buy a promise: this company will not treat our shared home as a mine to dig up and throw away.

When trust grows, it lasts longer than advertising.

### Page 6

Nelson Mandela in South Africa spent twenty-seven years in prison on Robben Island. By day, he worked in a stone quarry, where white limestone glare damaged his eyes.

If he had looked only at his own pain, he had many reasons to put hatred in front.

### Page 7

But after leaving prison, he did not make the country burn with his wound. He invited the guards who had watched over him to his inauguration, and he helped people tell the truth and make peace with one another.

He did not forget the harm, and still he put South Africa's future before his own hurt. What was kept was his own life, and also the chance for a country to live together.

### Page 8

If Mandela had turned the presidency into a chair of revenge, his supporters might have felt relief for a while, but the country could have kept splitting apart.

He put his own wounds behind and let the shared future sit in front. Then even former enemies had a step where they could put down their weapons.

### Page 9

In China, Fan Zhongyan wrote "The Record of Yueyang Tower" with a heart that looked far and wide.

He wrote of the wind, rain, brightness, and darkness over Dongting Lake. He wrote of a tower, and of how a person sees the world.

### Page 10

He left the thought: be first to worry for the world, and last to enjoy its joy. Stand in front for sorrow; step behind for pleasure.

This is not a pretty slogan. If a person truly puts the sorrow and joy of everyone under heaven before his own gain and loss, his heart is no longer trapped in his own small wins and losses.

### Page 11

Fan Zhongyan had his own sorrows and joys. People spoke badly of him too, and he knew loneliness.

But his heart did not make getting promoted or demoted the whole of life. So one tower and one lake could hold the sorrow and joy of everyone under heaven.

### Page 12

These three stories share one line: the more someone rushes to protect his money, his wounds, and his own gain and loss, the more others guard against him, and the more strength is spent on fighting.

When the shared road is kept first, and nature, country, and the world are put in front, trust has a place to take root.

### Page 13

"Putting oneself behind and being found in front" is a cause and effect in the human heart.

Stepping back lets others see: there is no grabbing here. When people feel safe, they are willing to come close and follow.

## Return to the Original

### Page 14

"Treating the self as outside and being preserved" has a cause and effect too. If Patagonia had only thought of keeping its money, it might have lost nature and its customers' trust; if Mandela had only kept his hatred, he might have lost a country.

If Fan Zhongyan had only kept his own gain and loss, he would have been only an official, not the wide heart that later generations remember.

### Page 15

"Not living for themselves" means not making the lonely little self the center of life. Heaven and earth give life to all things, yet do not turn all things into their own belongings.

Heaven and earth's own life lives on in the growing of all things, generation after generation. This is why they endure.

### Page 16

So Laozi is saying: a person who lives only for himself will always be fighting the world; a person who walks into a larger life can last together with everyone.

That is why heaven and earth "do not live for themselves," and so endure; that is why the sage puts himself behind, and is found in front.

### Page 17

Laozi also says the sage treats himself as outside, and so he is preserved; having no private ends, his ends are fulfilled. "Outside" means not letting a little gain or loss fence you in.

When a person walks back into a larger life, what is fulfilled is the great work, the life people live together, and a wide heart.

### Page 18

Children can practice this too. When standing in line, sharing things, or taking on a job, first ask how things can go better for everyone, then find where you stand.

Ahe wrote: The self returns to the larger life, like a drop returning to the river and the sea, lasting together with the life that keeps flowing.
