# 儿童道德经第二章：其二 西施的眉头和山谷的回声

## 第1页

庄子讲过一个故事：春秋时候，也就是两千多年前，有位女子叫西施，大家都说她很美。有一天她心口疼，轻轻按着胸口，眉头也皱了起来。

路人看见，还是觉得她好看。她按胸口的样子是真的，是身体难受时自己带出来的。

## 第2页

同村的东施看见了，以为“按胸口、皱眉头”就是美。她也照着做，还特意走到人多的地方。

人们看了，吓得躲开。后来，这个故事就叫“东施效颦”。

## 第3页

这个故事提醒我们：只学别人的样子，不知道那个样子是从哪里来的，就会越学越别扭。这个故事不是说谁长得不好。

这正好照见第二章开头：如果大家只认一种美，许多本来不一样的样子，就会被挤到“不美”那边去。

## 第4页

阿禾听完故事，兴冲冲办了一场“最美皱眉大会”。小兔先皱起眉，小鸟跟着皱，小石头把眉毛挤成一团。

没多久，大家的脸都酸了。院子里明明有阳光，却像飘来一小片闷闷的阴天。

## 第5页

这时，泥盆里的一株豆苗顶开土，露出一片嫩叶。小兔忘了皱眉，忽然咧嘴笑了。

阿禾看着那个笑，心里明白了：原来动人的样子是自己长出来的，练不出来。

## 第6页

老人说：“西施皱眉动人，是因为那一刻是真的。东施的错，是把别人的真样子，硬贴到自己脸上。”

阿禾看看大家酸酸的眉毛，赶紧把“最美皱眉大会”的牌子收了起来。

## 第7页

下午，他们走到山谷口。小石头拍了一下手，啪，声音飞进谷里。

孩子们站着等，山谷把声音送回来：啪。孩子们都笑了，原来声音也会有来有往。

## 第8页

小鸟吹一声高音，小兔敲一声低鼓。高音没有盖住低鼓，低鼓也没有抢走高音。

一个在上面亮一下，一个在下面托一下。听着听着，像有一座小桥在空气里搭起来。

## 第9页

古书《礼记·乐记》说，心被外面的事情碰到，就会发出声。一声应着另一声，慢慢有了节奏，就成了音乐。

阿禾听懂了一点：一个声音要听见别的声音，才知道自己该落在哪里。独自响着，还不算音乐。

## 第10页

他们试着只敲一只鼓，咚，咚，咚。声音很响，可听久了，就觉得少了点什么。

再加上竹笛、石片和拍手，声音开始互相让路。小曲子是从大家中间长出来的，不是谁一个人做出来的。

## 第11页

阿禾想抢着当领头的。老人由着他，把手放到耳边，做了一个“听”的动作。

阿禾停住，先听小鸟的长音，又听小兔的低鼓。等到中间空出一小格，他才轻轻敲了一下。

## 第12页

那一下不响，却刚好接住前一声。大家把声音放轻，曲子反而更好听。

风从山谷里出来，把最后一点回声送到孩子们脚边。

## 第13页

第二章说，“美”和“不美”是比出来的，也说“音声相和”。西施和东施的故事让阿禾看见：只认一种美，大家就只好照着学。

山谷里的声音又告诉他：不同的声音不用变成一样，也能一声应着另一声。

## 第14页

所以“无为”是不拿自己的标准去压别人。花按自己的样子开，鼓按自己的样子响，谁也不用学谁。

事情成了，也不急着说“都是我的功劳”。一首曲子能留下来，比谁站在最前面更重要。

## 第15页

阿禾在本子上写：不要把一种样子叫作全部的美。不要让一个声音盖住所有声音。

我愿意先听一听，再轻轻加入。这样，世界也许会自己唱起来。

# Chapter 002-2: Xi Shi's Brow and the Echo in the Valley

## Page 1

Zhuangzi tells a story about a woman named Xi Shi, who lived in ancient China more than two thousand years ago. Everyone said she was beautiful. One day her chest hurt, so she gently pressed her hand there and frowned.

People passing by still found her lovely. Her hand and her frown came straight out of the pain in her body.

## Page 2

Dong Shi, who lived in the same village, saw this and thought, "So pressing the chest and frowning is beauty." She copied the gesture and made sure people saw her.

People were startled and moved away. The story became known as "Dong Shi imitates the frown."

## Page 3

This story reminds us: if we copy only another person's outside and do not know where it comes from, we grow more and more awkward. The story was never about anyone's looks.

That fits the opening of Chapter Two: when everyone accepts only one kind of beauty, many other shapes are pushed into "not beautiful."

## Page 4

After hearing the story, Ahe cheerfully held a "Most Beautiful Frown Contest." Little Rabbit frowned first, Little Bird followed, and Little Stone squeezed his eyebrows into a knot.

Before long, everyone's face ached. The yard was sunny, but it felt as if a small gloomy cloud had drifted in.

## Page 5

Just then, a bean sprout pushed through the soil and opened one tender leaf. Little Rabbit forgot to frown and broke into a grin.

Ahe saw the smile and understood. The most moving expressions are the ones that grow on their own.

## Page 6

The old man said, "Xi Shi's frown moved people because it was true in that moment. Dong Shi's mistake was pasting another person's real look onto her own face."

Ahe looked at everyone's tired eyebrows and quickly took down the contest sign.

## Page 7

That afternoon, they walked to the mouth of the valley. Little Stone clapped once, and the sound flew inward.

The children stood and waited. The valley sent it back: clap. The children laughed. Even a sound could go out and return.

## Page 8

Little Bird blew a high note, and Little Rabbit tapped a low drum. The high note did not cover the drum, and the drum did not steal the high note.

One flashed above, one held below. As they listened, it was as if a small bridge were being built in the air.

## Page 9

The ancient *Record of Music* says that when outside things touch the heart, it sends out sound. When one sound answers another and a pattern slowly grows, the sounds become music.

Ahe understood a little: a sound has to hear other sounds before it knows where to land. Ringing alone, it is not yet music.

## Page 10

They tried only one drum: dong, dong, dong. It was loud, but after a while something felt missing.

Then they added bamboo pipe, stone chips, and clapping hands. The sounds began to make room for one another. The little tune grew among them; no single person made it.

## Page 11

Ahe wanted to rush in as leader. The old man let him try. He cupped a hand to his ear, the sign for "listen."

Ahe paused. He listened to Little Bird's long note, then to Little Rabbit's low drum. When a small space opened in the middle, he tapped softly once.

## Page 12

The tap was soft, and it caught the sound before it. Everyone kept their playing light, and the tune grew lovelier.

Wind came out of the valley and carried the last echo to the children's feet.

## Page 13

Chapter Two says beauty and not-beauty are made by comparing, and that notes and tones become harmonious together. Xi Shi and Dong Shi show Ahe: when only one beauty is allowed, people can only copy it.

The valley sounds show him something else: different voices do not have to become the same in order to answer one another.

## Page 14

So "without doing anything" means not pressing your own standard onto others. Let a flower be a flower and a drum be a drum. Nothing has to copy anything else.

When the work is done, do not hurry to say, "That was all mine." A song that remains matters more than the person standing in front.

## Page 15

Ahe wrote in his notebook: Do not call one shape the whole of beauty. Do not let one sound cover every other sound.

I will listen first, then join softly. Perhaps the world will begin to sing by itself.
