# 儿童道德经第十一章：其二 空竹才会唱

## 第1页

春末，麦子快熟了。空空村有个老规矩：傍晚收工时，要有人在田埂上吹一声竹笛。笛声一响，远处割草的人、放牛的人、捉虫的孩子，都知道该往家走了。

那天，老笛子忽然裂了，声音从裂缝里漏出去，像一只打喷嚏的鸭子。小石头笑得蹲在地上，阿林却很认真：“我来做一支新的。”

## 第2页

阿林跑到竹林，挑了一根最结实的竹子。“要声音大，就得用最实在的竹子。空空的东西，哪里有力气？”

他削平竹节，把外皮擦得发亮，却舍不得打通里面，也舍不得开孔。好好一根竹子，为什么要挖空它、戳伤它呢？

## 第3页

他把竹管放到嘴边，用力一吹，脸鼓起来，脖子也红了。竹子只发出一点闷闷的“噗”，像有人在袖子里叹气。

小石头问：“是不是还不够用力？”阿林吸了一大口气，又吹。笛子仍旧不出声，他的头越来越晕。

## 第4页

老人拿起那根竹子，轻轻敲了敲。

“它很结实。”老人说，“所以它只能当棍子。要做笛子，里面得有一条路，让气走过去。”

## 第5页

阿林不舍得：“挖空了，不就少了吗？”

老人把裂掉的旧笛递给他：“你听见过它唱歌，是因为它挖掉了一些竹子，多出一条空路。”

## 第6页

他们把竹节慢慢通开。

竹屑一点一点落下来，竹管里亮出一条长长的空。阿林从一头望进去，看见另一头有一点天光。

## 第7页

老人又量好位置，开了几个小孔。

阿林心疼地摸着孔边：“这些洞也是有用的？”

## 第8页

老人说：“别心疼。这些洞都是门。”

“孔开在哪里，空气走的路就有长短。路长一点，声音低一点；路短一点，声音高一点。手指按住、放开，曲子就变出来了。”

## 第9页

阿林再吹。

这一次，他没有把气全砸进去。气轻轻进竹管，先是一声细细的哨音，接着抖了一下，变成一段很短的调子。

## 第10页

田埂上的孩子都安静了。

声音不大，却走得很远。它从麦芒上掠过去，穿过桑树影子，落到放牛人耳边。

## 第11页

放牛人抬头笑了：“新笛子？”

阿林的脸红了。他低头看那根竹子，觉得它多了一条看不见的路。

## 第12页

第二天，他又试着多开一个孔。

这次孔开偏了，调子一下歪了。阿林才知道，空的地方也不能乱开。车轮的孔要在中心，窗要开在合适的地方，笛孔也有自己的位置。

## 第13页

老人说：“空，也要空在对的地方。”

“能让气走、让声成、让人听见远处，这样的空，才叫用。”

## 第14页

第十一章说“有之以为利，无之以为用”。竹子是“有”，没有竹身，气就散了；竹子若全是实心，气也无路可走。

笛子会唱，是因为竹身和孔里空着。那些空处不发光、不出头，却让一口气变成叫人回家的声音。

## 第15页

阿林想起车轮、陶罐和小屋：车轴穿过空孔，水住进空罐，人住进空屋，风也在空竹里变成歌。

空能给气让路，给光留门，也让别人进得来。心里留一条空路，话才吹得成好曲子。

# Chapter 011-2: Only Hollow Bamboo Can Sing

## Page 1

At the end of spring, the wheat was almost ripe. Kongkong Village had an old habit: at dusk, someone stood on the field ridge and played one note on a bamboo flute. People cutting grass, herding cattle, and catching insects knew it was time to go home.

That day, the old flute cracked, and sound leaked out like a duck sneezing. Little Stone laughed so hard he crouched down, but Lin was serious. "I will make a new one."

## Page 2

Lin ran to the bamboo grove and chose the strongest bamboo. "If the sound should be strong, the bamboo must be solid. How can an empty thing have strength?"

He smoothed the joints and polished the skin until it shone, but he could not bear to open the inside or cut holes. Why hollow out and pierce a perfectly good piece of bamboo?

## Page 3

He put the bamboo to his mouth and blew hard. His cheeks puffed and his neck reddened, but the bamboo made only a dull puff, like someone sighing inside a sleeve.

"Maybe you did not blow hard enough," Little Stone said. Lin took a huge breath and blew again. The flute stayed silent, and his head grew dizzier.

## Page 4

The old man picked up the bamboo and tapped it lightly.

"It is very solid," he said. "So it can only be a stick. To be a flute, it needs a path inside for breath to travel."

## Page 5

Lin did not want to let go. "If we hollow it out, won't there be less?"

The old man handed him the cracked old flute. "You heard it sing because some of the bamboo was carved away, and an empty road was added."

## Page 6

They slowly opened the bamboo joints.

Shavings fell little by little, and a long hollow space appeared inside the tube. Lin looked through one end and saw a point of sky at the other.

## Page 7

The old man measured carefully and cut several small holes.

Lin touched the edge of one hole. "Are these holes useful too?"

## Page 8

The old man said, "Don't feel sorry for it. Each hole is a door."

"Where a hole sits changes the length of the air's path. A longer path makes a lower note; a shorter path makes a higher note. Cover and uncover the holes with your fingers, and the notes become a tune."

## Page 9

Lin blew again.

This time, he did not throw all his breath into the bamboo. Air entered gently. First came a thin whistle; then it trembled and became a short melody.

## Page 10

The children on the field ridge grew quiet.

The sound was not loud, yet it traveled far. It skimmed over wheat tips, passed through mulberry shadows, and reached the ears of the cattle herder.

## Page 11

The herder looked up and smiled. "A new flute?"

Lin's face reddened. He looked down at the bamboo and felt that it had gained an invisible road.

## Page 12

The next day, he tried cutting one more hole.

This time the hole was in the wrong place, and the tune bent badly. Lin learned that empty places cannot be opened just anywhere. The wheel's opening belongs at the center, the window belongs in the right wall, and flute holes have their own places.

## Page 13

The old man said, "Emptiness, too, has to be in the right place."

"A useful empty place lets breath travel, lets sound form, and lets people hear from far away."

## Page 14

Chapter Eleven says that what exists gives benefit, and what is empty gives use. The bamboo is what exists: without its body, breath scatters; if it is solid all the way through, breath has no road.

The flute sings because the tube and holes are empty. Those empty places do not shine or show off, yet they turn one breath into a sound that calls people home.

## Page 15

Lin thought of the wheel, the jar, and the house: the axle passes through an empty hole, water rests in a hollow jar, people live in an open room, and wind becomes song inside hollow bamboo.

Emptiness makes a road for breath, leaves a door for light, and gives others a way in. The heart needs an open road too, so words can become a good tune.
