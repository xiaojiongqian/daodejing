# 儿童道德经第十二章：其二 四座城门之外

## 第1页

古印度有一个关于悉达多太子的故事。

这是佛教传统里非常重要的故事。给孩子讲时，不必急着讲完里面所有的道理，先看见一个问题就好：一个人如果只被美好的东西围住，会不会反而看不见真实？

## 第2页

悉达多出生在王宫里。

他的父亲希望他一生富贵快乐，便把宫殿修得极美：花园常开，乐声常在，衣服柔软，食物香甜。

## 第3页

父亲还吩咐，不让太子看见衰老、病痛和死亡。宫里的人藏起皱纹，压低哭声，把病人和送葬的人挡在远处。

这座宫殿像一个巨大的彩色盒子，五色、五音、五味都装得满满的，通向真实世界的缝却快没有了。

## 第4页

有一天，悉达多坚持要出城。马车走到东门外，他看见一位老人，背弯了，头发白了，脚步很慢，像每一步都要从地上借力。

太子问车夫：“人为什么会这样？”

车夫沉默了一会儿，说：“每个人都会老。太子也会。”

## 第5页

宫里的音乐好像忽然远了。

悉达多第一次知道，自己年轻的脸也会一年一年老去。

## 第6页

后来，他从南门出去，看见一个病人躺在路旁，脸色苍白，身体疼得发抖。太子问：“这也是别人家的事吗？”

车夫说：“不是。人有身体，就可能生病。强壮的人也会有脆弱的时候。”

这句话像一阵冷风，吹进太子从没开过的窗。

## 第7页

再后来，他从西门出去，看见一队送葬的人。人们低着头，脚步沉沉，悉达多知道生命也会走到尽头。

回到宫里，花还开着，甜果的味道也没有变。他却明白了：被藏起来的事情，不会因为看不见就消失。

## 第8页

最后，他从北门出去，看见一位修行人。

那人衣着朴素，手里没有珠宝，脸上却有一种安静。太子从他身上看见另一条路：不逃避真实，也不被真实吓倒。

## 第9页

四次出城，像四扇门一扇一扇打开。老人、病人、送葬队伍和修行人，把生命原本的样子摆在他面前。

宫里乐师还在弹琴，舞者还在旋转。悉达多知道，如果一生只躲在这些美好里，眼睛反而会被美好遮住。

## 第10页

后来，他离开王宫，去寻找关于生老病死的答案。

宫殿再华丽，也回答不了他心里那个真正的问题。

## 第11页

孩子们听到这里，有点安静。

小石头问：“那是不是快乐都是假的？”

## 第12页

老人摇头：“花是真的，音乐也是真的，快乐并不假。”

“可是如果有人只让你看花，不让你看根；只让你听歌，不让你听哭声，那你看到的就不完整。”

## 第13页

阿林想起集市上那颗蓝珠子。

珠子很好看。坏的是他让那一点亮光挡住了豆种、朋友的呼唤和自己答应过的事。

## 第14页

第十二章说“五色令人目盲”。宫殿五色虽美，若被用来挡住真实，也会变成一面亮墙。

“五音令人耳聋”，是只听见赞美和歌舞，便听不见世界的痛苦，也听不见自己心里真正的问题。

## 第15页

“圣人为腹不为目”，是回到生命的根本。好看、好听、好吃的都可以有，人却不能只靠这些活着。

真正的清醒，是看见美好时不忘真实，听见热闹时也听得见安静处的呼唤。美好本身不必推开。

# Chapter 012-2: Beyond the Four Gates

## Page 1

Ancient India has a story about Prince Siddhartha.

It is an important story in Buddhist tradition. For children, we need not rush through every teaching in it. We can first see one question: if a person is surrounded only by pleasant things, might he fail to see what is real?

## Page 2

Siddhartha was born in a palace.

His father wanted him to live in wealth and happiness, so he made the palace beautiful: gardens kept blooming, music kept playing, clothes were soft, and food was sweet.

## Page 3

The father also ordered that the prince should not see aging, sickness, or death. People in the palace hid wrinkles, lowered crying voices, and kept the sick and funeral processions far away.

The palace was like a great colored box, packed with colors, sounds, and flavors. Almost no crack remained through which the real world could enter.

## Page 4

One day, Siddhartha insisted on leaving the city. At the eastern gate, he saw an old man. The man's back was bent, his hair was white, and his steps were slow, as if each step had to borrow strength from the ground.

The prince asked the charioteer, "Why does a person become like this?"

The charioteer was silent for a moment. "Everyone grows old. Even the prince will."

## Page 5

The music of the palace suddenly seemed far away.

For the first time, Siddhartha understood that his own young face would grow older year by year.

## Page 6

Later, he went out through the southern gate and saw a sick man lying beside the road, pale and trembling with pain. The prince asked, "Is this only something that happens to other families?"

The charioteer said, "No. If a person has a body, sickness can come. Even the strong have fragile hours."

The words were like a cold wind entering a window that had never been opened.

## Page 7

Another time, he went out through the western gate and saw a funeral procession. People lowered their heads and walked heavily, and Siddhartha understood that life also comes to an end.

Back in the palace, the flowers still bloomed and the sweet fruit tasted the same. But he now knew that hidden things do not disappear simply because we cannot see them.

## Page 8

At last, he went out through the northern gate and saw a wandering seeker.

The man wore simple clothes and carried no jewels, yet his face was quiet. In him, the prince saw another road: one that did not flee from reality and did not panic before it.

## Page 9

These four outings were like four doors opening one by one. The old man, the sick man, the funeral, and the seeker set the true shape of life before him.

Back in the palace, musicians still played and dancers still turned. Siddhartha knew that if he hid all his life inside these pleasant things, beauty itself would cover his eyes.

## Page 10

Later, he left the palace to seek an answer to aging, sickness, and death.

Even the most beautiful palace could not answer the real question in his heart.

## Page 11

When the children heard this, they grew quiet.

Little Stone asked, "Does that mean happiness is false?"

## Page 12

The old man shook his head. "Flowers are real, music is real, and happiness is not false."

"But if someone lets you see only flowers and never roots, only songs and never crying, then what you see is not whole."

## Page 13

Lin remembered the blue bead from the market.

The bead itself was pretty. The harm came when its brightness hid the bean seeds, his friend's call, and the promise he had made.

## Page 14

Chapter Twelve says that many colors can blind. The palace's colors were beautiful, but when used to block reality, they became a shining wall.

"Many sounds can deafen" means hearing only praise and music until we miss the world's suffering and the true question in our own heart.

## Page 15

"For the belly, not for the eyes" means returning to what life truly needs. Beauty, music, and good food all have their place, but a person cannot live by these alone.

Being awake means seeing beauty without forgetting truth, and hearing liveliness while still hearing the call from quiet places. Beauty itself does not have to be pushed away.
