# 儿童道德经第七章：其一 把自己放在后面

## 第1页

天长地久。
天地所以能长且久者，以其不自生，故能长生。
是以圣人后其身而身先；
外其身而身存。
非以其无私邪，故能成其私。

## 第2页

第七章讲一条因果，不是教人拿吃亏换奖赏。只围着自己活，容易抢位置、怕失去；先看见整体，才能和大家一起长久。

王弼解释“不自生”：只为自己活，就会和万物争。天地不抢云、水、风、草木的位置，容得下万物，也显得长久。

## 第3页

河上公说，天地安静地给予万物，却不求回报，也不把每件事算成自己的功劳。

圣人学的正是这个因果。“后其身”，是先让事情成、让众人安，大家信他，才愿意把他放在前面。“外其身”，是不让一点得失管住自己，由此留住身体、信任、道路和更长久的生命。

## 第4页

“长久”的意思是很长很长地延续。这里指的是不靠争抢也能持续。

“不自生”的意思是不只为自己活，不把自己当作所有事情的中心。

“长生”在这里说的是长久。它靠的是不争，跟神仙故事里说的长生不老没有关系。

## 第5页

“后其身”的意思是把自己放在后面，先顾到事情和别人。

“身先”的意思是反而走到前面，得到大家的信任。

“外其身”的意思是不把自己那一点得失看得最大。

“无私”的意思是没有只为自己打算，所以能成全更大的自己。

## 第6页

夏天，空空村要给过路人搭一座凉棚。孩子们搬竹竿，老人们编草帘，地上堆着绳子和木桩。

阿林被推出来当小队长。他心里有点高兴，也有点想站到最前面，让大家都看见。

## 第7页

太阳很大，大家都想先喝酸梅汤。阿林刚伸手，老人把水勺递给他。

老人说：“你来分。”阿林接过水勺，手停了一下。他想到：队长要是先给自己倒，大家的眼睛就会盯着桶，没人再看凉棚。

## 第8页

他先给最小的孩子倒，又给搬竹竿的叔叔倒，再给额头冒汗的老人倒。

轮到自己时，桶底只剩一点点。一个小女孩把半杯推回来，小声说：“阿林哥哥也热。”

## 第9页

午后忽然起风，草帘被吹得翻起来。阿林本想躲到屋檐下，却看见棚角的竹绳快松了。

他知道自己一躲，别人也容易散开。于是他跑到棚边，踮脚拉住绳子。雨点打在脸上，手心也被磨红。

## 第10页

风停以后，凉棚没有倒。过路人坐在棚下，鞋边滴着雨水，脸上却有了笑。

大家商量怎么加固时，第一个转头看阿林。刚才需要有人站出来的时候，他在。

## 第11页

傍晚，阿林抬头看天。天空盖住每一间屋子，却没有在云上写自己的名字。

他又低头看地。土地托住每一双脚，也没有问谁先夸它。

## 第12页

第七章不是教人用退后来换掌声。若退后只是办法，私心还是站在最前面。

“后其身”让人看见谁可信任，“外其身”让害怕不再做主。把自己放进更大的事情里，才能和大家一起长久。

# Chapter 007-1: Putting Yourself Behind

## Page 1

Heaven is long-enduring and earth continues long. The reason why heaven and earth are able to endure and continue thus long is because they do not live of, or for, themselves. This is how they are able to continue and endure.

Therefore the sage puts his own person last, and yet it is found in the foremost place; he treats his person as if it were foreign to him, and yet that person is preserved. Is it not because he has no personal and private ends, that therefore such ends are realised?

## Page 2

Chapter Seven is about cause and effect, not trading loss for reward. When we make ourselves the center, we grab for places and fear loss. Seeing the larger whole lets us endure with others.

Wang Bi says that living only for themselves would make heaven and earth struggle against all things. They do not seize space from clouds, water, wind, or plants, so they can hold everything and endure.

## Page 3

Heshang Gong says heaven and earth give quietly without asking for anything back or claiming credit.

The sage learns the same cause and effect. He puts himself last by first letting the work succeed and keeping people safe; their trust then places him in front. He treats his person as foreign by refusing to let personal gain and loss rule him. He preserves his life, along with trust, a road to walk, and a longer future.

## Page 4

The phrase "long-enduring" means lasting for a very long time. Here it means lasting without fighting to possess.

The phrase “do not live of, or for, themselves” means they do not make themselves the center of everything.

The phrase "continue and endure" means to keep going and remain.

## Page 5

The phrase "puts his own person last" means putting himself behind the task and the people.

The phrase "foremost place" means the front place, where trust gathers.

The phrase "foreign to him" means that his own gain and loss are not treated as the biggest concern.

The phrase "private ends" means thinking only of oneself; with no such plan, a larger self can be fulfilled.

## Page 6

In summer, Kongkong Village built a shade shelter for travelers. The children carried bamboo poles, the elders wove grass mats, and ropes and stakes lay on the ground.

Lin was chosen as the small team leader. He felt pleased, and part of him wanted to stand in front where everyone could see him.

## Page 7

The sun was hot, and everyone wanted sour plum drink first. Just as Lin reached for a cup, the old man handed him the ladle.

"You serve," said the old man. Lin took the ladle, and his hand paused. He thought: if the leader poured for himself first, every eye would go to the bucket, and no one would be watching the shelter.

## Page 8

He poured first for the smallest children, then for the uncles carrying bamboo, then for the elders with sweat on their foreheads.

When his turn came, only a little remained. A small girl pushed back half her cup and whispered, "Brother Lin is hot too."

## Page 9

In the afternoon, wind rose and lifted the grass mats. Lin wanted to hide under the eaves, but he saw a bamboo rope loosening at the corner.

He knew that if he hid first, others might scatter too. So he ran to the shelter's edge and pulled the rope tight. Rain struck his face, and the rope reddened his palms.

## Page 10

When the wind stopped, the shelter still stood. Travelers sat beneath it with rain dripping from their shoes and smiles returning to their faces.

When everyone discussed how to strengthen the shelter, they first looked at Lin. When someone was needed, he had been there.

## Page 11

At dusk, Lin looked up at the sky. It covered every roof, yet wrote no name on the clouds.

Then he looked at the ground. It carried every pair of feet, yet did not ask who praised it first.

## Page 12

Chapter Seven does not teach stepping back as a way to earn praise. If stepping back is only a trick, selfishness is still standing in front.

Putting yourself last lets people see whom they can trust. Stepping outside your own gain and loss keeps fear from deciding for you. Taking part in something larger lets you endure together with everyone.
