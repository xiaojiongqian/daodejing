# 儿童道德经第六章：其一 不说话的山谷

## 原文

### 第1页

谷神不死，是谓玄牝。
玄牝之门，是谓天地根。
绵绵若存，用之不勤。

## 释义

### 第2页

“谷神”说的是山谷中间那片空处的作用。王弼说，那里空空、无形无影，风、水和声音却能从里面经过。它跟“山里住着会说话的神”没有关系。

它低低的，空空的，不抢东西，所以许多东西都能在里面来去。这种空着还能回应的作用，就是“不死”。

### 第3页

“玄牝”可以理解成深深的、生养生命的母亲，万物生长的深处。它不是指某个人，也不固定在某个地方。

老子用“门”和“根”来形容它：万物从那里显现，也靠那看不见的源头继续生长。

### 第4页

“绵绵若存”，是说它细细地、不断地在那里，一直在，却看不真切。

像山泉从石缝里流出，不轰隆，也不显摆，却一年一年养绿苔藓和树根。

### 第5页

河上公说，“用之不勤”讲的是用气要慢慢来，不要又急又累。它不是不用，也不是偷懒。

放到生活里，就是别把力气当成一只硬拧的水龙头。该用时用，该停时停，泉水才不浑。

## 难词注解

### 第6页

“谷神”指的是山谷空处的灵妙作用。空处能让声音回响，也能藏风、汇水；空是活的。

“玄”的意思是深远、看不清，却又真的存在。

“牝”的意思是“母”。这里指生命里能生养的那一面。

### 第7页

“门”是进出通过的地方。这里指万物出生的那个深深的开口。

“天地根”的意思是天地万物的根源，一个比树根更深的来处。

“不勤”的意思是不用得太急、太累。慢慢地用，才不容易用光。

## 正文

### 第8页

雨后，阿禾跟老人走进山谷。两边石壁高高站着，中间空出一条凉凉的路。

阿禾轻轻喊了一声，声音往里跑了一会儿，又从远处回来了。

### 第9页

小石头睁大眼：“山谷里有人回答吗？”

老人说：“不是有人藏着。是山谷空，声音才有地方走，也有地方回来。”

### 第10页

他们继续往里走，听见一线细水。水从石缝里出来，细得像银丝。

它的声音很轻，却把苔藓养绿，把树根润湿。小虫也沿着湿石头慢慢爬过去。

### 第11页

小石头拿起铲子，想把泉眼挖大。“这样水会更多吧？”

老人拦住他：“太用力，泥就浑了，泉眼也可能受伤。细细地流，反而流得久。”

### 第12页

阿禾蹲下看泉。水在石缝里一闪一闪，几乎看不见；可他用手一捧，凉意真的落到了掌心。

他忽然懂了“若存”：它一直都在，只是抓不住。

### 第13页

傍晚，山谷里起了雾。雾不急着跑，也不急着证明自己。

草尖挂着水珠，石壁慢慢变暗。山谷沉默，却正悄悄生养着草木。

## 回扣

### 第14页

第六章说的“谷神”，就是这种空着却能回应、安静却能生养的妙用。

它从不站出来说“我是根”。正因为它不占满自己，风和水从这里经过，草木在这里得到滋养。

### 第15页

阿禾写下：真正深的东西，往往安静；真正久的东西，用力很轻。

像山谷，低低地空着，所以能应；像泉水，细细地在，所以能生。

# Chapter 006-1: The Valley That Does Not Speak

## Original Text

### Page 1

The valley spirit dies not, aye the same;
The female mystery thus do we name.
Its gate, from which at first they issued forth,
Is called the root from which grew heaven and earth.
Long and unbroken does its power remain,
Used gently, and without the touch of pain.

## Interpretation

### Page 2

"Valley spirit" describes the empty responsiveness at the heart of a valley. Wang Bi says the center of a valley is empty and shapeless, yet wind, water, and sound can pass through it. It has nothing to do with a talking ghost living there.

It stays low and open, and does not grab at anything. So many things can come and go inside it. This empty, answering use is what "does not die."

### Page 3

"Female mystery" can mean the deep mother-source that gives life, the deep place where all things grow. It is not a person, and it does not sit in one fixed place.

Laozi uses "gate" and "root" for it: things appear through it and keep growing from an unseen source.

### Page 4

"Long and unbroken" means it is fine, continuous, and quietly there. It is always present, though we cannot see it clearly.

Like a spring coming through stone, it does not roar or show off, yet year after year it nourishes moss and roots.

### Page 5

Heshang Gong says that "used gently" means using your breath slowly, without hurry or strain. It is not "never used," and it is not laziness.

In daily life, this means not treating your strength like a faucet twisted hard. Use it when it is time, rest when it is time, and the spring will not muddy.

## Vocabulary Notes

### Page 6

The phrase "valley spirit" means the living usefulness of an empty valley, where sound echoes, wind moves, water gathers, and the valley responds.

The phrase "female mystery" means a deep, hidden source that gives life.

The word "gate" means a place where things pass in and out.

### Page 7

The word "root" means the deep source from which life grows.

The phrase "long and unbroken" means continuing softly without being cut off.

The phrase "used gently" means using without hurry or strain, so the source is not quickly used up.

## Main Text

### Page 8

After rain, Ahe walked with the old man into a valley. Tall stone walls stood on both sides, while a cool empty path opened in the middle.

Ahe called softly. His voice ran inward for a while, then came back from far away.

### Page 9

Little Stone's eyes widened. "Is someone answering inside the valley?"

"No one is hiding there," said the old man. "The valley is hollow, so sound has room to travel and return."

### Page 10

They walked farther in and heard a thread of water. It came through a crack in the stone, thin as silver thread.

Its voice was soft, yet it greened the moss and moistened the roots. A small insect crawled slowly along the wet stone.

### Page 11

Little Stone picked up a shovel and wanted to dig the spring wider. "Wouldn't that make more water?"

The old man stopped him. "Too much force muddies the water, and the spring itself may be hurt. A thin, steady flow lasts longer."

### Page 12

Ahe crouched to watch the spring. The water flickered in the crack, almost invisible; yet when he cupped his hands, the coolness truly landed in his palms.

He understood "as if present": always here, though never easy to grasp.

### Page 13

At dusk, mist rose in the valley. It did not rush away or try to prove itself.

Drops hung on the grass tips, and the stone walls slowly darkened. The valley was silent, yet quietly nourishing life.

## Return to the Original

### Page 14

The "valley spirit" in Chapter Six is this: empty, and still able to answer; quiet, and still able to give life.

It never announces, "I am the root." Because it does not fill itself, wind and water pass through, and plants are nourished here.

### Page 15

Ahe wrote: What is truly deep is often quiet. What lasts long uses a light hand.

Like a valley, it stays low and open, so it can respond. Like spring water, it continues softly, so it can give life.
