# 儿童道德经第六章：其一 不说话的山谷

## 第1页

谷神不死，是谓玄牝。
玄牝之门，是谓天地根。
绵绵若存，用之不勤。

## 第2页

“谷神”说的是山谷中间那片空处的作用。王弼说，那里空空、无形无影，风、水和声音却能从里面经过，跟“山里住着会说话的神”没有关系。

山谷低低的，空着，许多东西才能在里面来去。这种空着还能回应的作用，就是“不死”。

## 第3页

“玄牝”可以理解成生养万物的深深母源。它不指某个人，也不固定在某个地方。

老子用“门”和“根”来形容它：万物从那里显现，也靠那看不见的源头继续生长。

## 第4页

“绵绵若存”，是说它细细地、不断地在那里，一直在，却看不真切。

像山泉从石缝里流出，不轰隆，也不显摆，却一年一年养绿苔藓和树根。

## 第5页

河上公说，“用之不勤”讲的是用气要慢慢来，不要又急又累。它不是不用，也不是偷懒。

放到生活里，别急着把力气一下用光。该用时用，该停时停，才能像泉水一样流得久。

## 第6页

“谷神”是山谷空处的灵妙作用：能回声，也能藏风、汇水。

“玄”是深远难见；“牝”是母，指生养生命的一面。

“门”是万物出生的开口；“天地根”是天地万物的根源。

“绵绵若存”是细细不断、若有若无。“不勤”是用得不过急、不过累，才不容易用光。

## 第7页

雨后，阿林跟老人走进山谷。两边石壁高高站着，中间空出一条凉凉的路。

阿林轻轻喊了一声，声音往里跑了一会儿，又从远处回来了。

## 第8页

小石头睁大眼：“山谷里有人回答吗？”

老人说：“不是有人藏着。是山谷空，声音才有地方走，也有地方回来。”

## 第9页

他们继续往里走，听见一线细水。水从石缝里出来，细得像银丝。

它的声音很轻，却把苔藓养绿，把树根润湿。小虫也沿着湿石头慢慢爬过去。

## 第10页

小石头拿起铲子，想把泉眼挖大。“这样水会更多吧？”

老人拦住他：“太用力，泥就浑了，泉眼也可能受伤。细细地流，反而流得久。”

## 第11页

阿林蹲下捧起泉水，凉意落进掌心。他懂了“若存”：源头一直在，却藏身在石缝后，看不真切。

傍晚，雾漫进山谷，草尖挂上水珠。山谷沉默，正悄悄生养着草木。

## 第12页

第六章的山谷低低地空着，所以能回声、能藏风汇水；泉水细细地流，所以能养绿苔藓和树根。

真正深的东西常常安静，真正久的东西用力很轻。

# Chapter 006-1: The Valley That Does Not Speak

## Page 1

The valley spirit dies not, aye the same;
The female mystery thus do we name.
Its gate, from which at first they issued forth,
Is called the root from which grew heaven and earth.
Long and unbroken does its power remain,
Used gently, and without the touch of pain.

## Page 2

"Valley spirit" describes the empty responsiveness at the heart of a valley. Wang Bi says its center is empty and shapeless, yet wind, water, and sound can pass through it. It has nothing to do with a talking ghost living there.

A valley stays low and open, so many things can come and go. This empty, answering use is what "does not die."

## Page 3

"Female mystery" can mean the deep mother-source from which all things grow. It is not a person or a fixed place.

Laozi uses "gate" and "root" for it: things appear through it and keep growing from an unseen source.

## Page 4

"Long and unbroken" means it is fine, continuous, and quietly there. It is always present, though we cannot see it clearly.

Like a spring coming through stone, it does not roar or show off, yet year after year it nourishes moss and roots.

## Page 5

Heshang Gong says that "used gently" means using your breath slowly, without hurry or strain. It is not "never used," and it is not laziness.

In daily life, do not spend all your strength in a rush. Use it when it is time and rest when it is time, so it can last like a flowing spring.

## Page 6

The phrase "valley spirit" means the living usefulness of an empty valley, where sound echoes and wind and water gather.

The phrase "female mystery" means a deep, hidden source that gives life. The word "gate" means the deep opening through which things are born. The word "root" means the source of heaven, earth, and all things.

The phrase "long and unbroken" means continuing softly without being cut off. The phrase "used gently" means using without hurry or strain, so the source is not quickly used up.

## Page 7

After rain, Lin walked with the old man into a valley. Tall stone walls stood on both sides, while a cool empty path opened in the middle.

Lin called softly. His voice ran inward for a while, then came back from far away.

## Page 8

Little Stone's eyes widened. "Is someone answering inside the valley?"

"No one is hiding there," said the old man. "The valley is hollow, so sound has room to travel and return."

## Page 9

They walked farther in and heard a thread of water. It came through a crack in the stone, thin as silver thread.

Its voice was soft, yet it greened the moss and moistened the roots. A small insect crawled slowly along the wet stone.

## Page 10

Little Stone picked up a shovel and wanted to dig the spring wider. "Wouldn't that make more water?"

The old man stopped him. "Too much force muddies the water, and the spring itself may be hurt. A thin, steady flow lasts longer."

## Page 11

Lin crouched and cupped the spring water. Coolness settled into his palms. He understood "as if present": the source was always there, though hidden behind the stone and hard to see.

At dusk, mist drifted into the valley and beads of water gathered on the grass. Silent, the valley was quietly nourishing its plants.

## Page 12

The valley in Chapter Six stays low and open, so it can echo and hold wind and water. The spring flows softly, so it can green the moss and feed the roots.

What is truly deep is often quiet. What lasts long uses a light hand.
