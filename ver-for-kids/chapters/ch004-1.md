# 儿童道德经第四章：其一 不满的井

## 原文

### 第1页

道冲而用之或不盈，渊兮似万物之宗；

挫其锐，解其纷，和其光，同其尘，湛兮似或存。

吾不知谁之子，象帝之先。

## 释义

### 第2页

“冲”是空着、通着，所以能装、能过，也能生出用处。

老子说道像一个深深的器物，正因为没有被塞满，万物才有路从里面来来去去。

### 第3页

“渊兮似万物之宗”，是说道深得像不见底的水。万物好像都从那里来，又靠它继续生长。

它深深地待在那里，像井水在黑暗里不声不响，不站出来说“我是源头”。

### 第4页

“挫其锐”，是把尖锐的劲收柔一点；“解其纷”，是把缠在一起的结慢慢松开。

“和其光”，是让光柔一点，不刺眼；“同其尘”，是走进普通生活里，和普通人站在一起。

### 第5页

最后老子说：我不知道道是谁的孩子，它好像连天帝都还没出现的时候，就已经在了。

老子这样说，是在提醒我们：桌子、石头能抓在手里，道抓不住。可是花草、石头和人，都从它那里出来。

## 难词注解

### 第6页

“冲”的意思是空而通，不满不塞。像井口空着，水才能被打上来。

“不盈”的意思是不装得太满，不把自己塞得一点空处都没有。

“渊”指的是很深的水。这里用来形容道很深，看不到底。

### 第7页

“宗”的意思是根本、祖先或源头。

“挫锐”的意思是把尖尖的刺磨柔一点，让力量不伤人。

“解纷”的意思是解开缠在一起的乱线，就像慢慢松开打结的绳子。

### 第8页

“和光”的意思是让自己的光柔和一些，不用亮光逼人。

“同尘”的意思是和普通人一起生活，不把自己摆得很高。

“湛”的意思是清深而安静，好像一直在那里。

## 正文

### 第9页

空空村有一口老井。井沿不高，井口黑黑的，低头望进去，黑得看不见底。

小兔问：“里面空空的，为什么大家每天还来打水？”

### 第10页

老人把木桶慢慢放下去，绳子吱呀吱呀响。桶沉到底，停了一停，再提上来，清水在里面晃着光。

阿禾趴在井沿上看，忽然明白：井口看起来空，正因为空，水才能被打上来。

### 第11页

小石头搬来几块石头，想把井填满一点。“这样它看起来就有东西了。”

老人拦住他：“填满了，它就不再是井了。有些用处，正是靠留着空处，才留下来的。”

### 第12页

孩子们抢着拉水桶。你往左，他往右，绳子很快打成一个硬结。

老人把结放在膝上，一圈一圈挑开，手上不急。阿禾看着看着，发现越急，结越紧；手稳下来，线头才肯露出来。

### 第13页

太阳快落山时，井水照出一片柔柔的光。尘土在光里慢慢飘。

阿禾觉得，道也许就像这口井：深深地待在那里，安安静静把水送给村子。

## 回扣

### 第14页

回看第四章：道“冲而不盈”，不把自己塞死，所以用不尽。

“渊深如宗”，是说万物有看不见的根；“挫锐解纷”，是说真正的力量先不伤人，再慢慢解开乱结。

### 第15页

阿禾写下：井不满，水才能来；手不急，结才能松。光柔下来，连尘土也能被照见。

道像深处的空，让世界有路、有水、有光。

# Chapter 004-1: The Well That Is Not Full

## Original Text

### Page 1

The Tao is (like) the emptiness of a vessel; and in our employment of it we must be on our guard against all fulness. How deep and unfathomable it is, as if it were the Honoured Ancestor of all things!

### Page 2

We should blunt our sharp points, and unravel the complications of things; we should attemper our brightness, and bring ourselves into agreement with the obscurity of others. How pure and still the Tao is, as if it would ever so continue!

I do not know whose son it is. It might appear to have been before God.

## Interpretation

### Page 3

"Chong" is open and passable, so it can hold, let things through, and give rise to use.

Laozi says Dao is like a deep vessel. Because it is not stuffed full, the ten thousand things have room to come and go.

### Page 4

"Deep, as if the ancestor of all things" means Dao is like water with no visible bottom. The ten thousand things seem to come from it and keep growing through it.

It stays quietly in its place, deep down, like well water in the dark, never standing up to announce, "I am the source."

### Page 5

"Blunt sharp points" means softening a keen edge. "Unravel complications" means slowly loosening tangled knots.

"Temper brightness" means making the light gentle. "Join the dust" means entering ordinary life and standing among ordinary people.

Laozi finally says he does not know whose child Dao is. It seems to have been there even before the Lord of Heaven. Laozi says this to remind us: a table or a stone can be held in the hand; Dao cannot. Yet flowers, stones, and people all come out of it.

## Vocabulary Notes

### Page 6

The phrase "emptiness of a vessel" is Legge's image for an open hollow that can hold things and let them move through.

The word "fulness" means being packed so full that no empty space is left. What is too full spills over.

The word "unfathomable" means too deep to see the bottom.

### Page 7

The phrase "Honoured Ancestor" is Legge's way of describing the deep source or root of all things.

The phrase "blunt our sharp points" means softening sharpness, strength that does not wound.

The phrase "unravel the complications" means loosening tangles, like opening a knot in a string.

### Page 8

The phrase "attemper our brightness" means making one's light gentle and balanced.

The phrase "obscurity of others" is Legge's way of describing life among ordinary dust and ordinary people.

The phrase "pure and still" means clear, deep, and quiet, as if always there.

## Main Text

### Page 9

Kongkong Village had an old well. Its stone rim was low, and when you looked down into its dark mouth, you could not see the bottom.

Little Rabbit asked, "If it is empty inside, why does everyone come here every day?"

### Page 10

The old man lowered a wooden bucket, and the rope creaked. The bucket sank to the bottom, paused, and came back up with clear water rocking in the light.

Ahe leaned over the rim and suddenly understood: the well seemed empty, and because it was empty, water could be drawn up.

### Page 11

Little Stone carried over several stones. He wanted to fill the well a little. "Then it will look as if something is inside."

The old man stopped him. "If you fill it, it will no longer be a well. Some uses last only because a little empty space is kept."

### Page 12

The children argued over the water bucket. One pulled left, another pulled right, and the rope tightened into a hard knot.

The old man laid the knot on his knee and opened it loop by loop, his hands unhurried. Ahe watched and saw that the faster one pulls, the tighter the knot gets; when the hands grow steady, the loose end appears.

### Page 13

At sunset, the well water held a soft light, and dust drifted slowly inside it.

Ahe felt that Dao might be like this well: staying quietly in its depth, giving water to the village every day.

## Return to the Original

### Page 14

Looking back at Chapter Four: Dao is open and never stuffed shut, so its use does not run out.

"Deep as a root" means the ten thousand things have an unseen source. To blunt sharpness and untie tangles means true strength first avoids hurting, then slowly unties the tangle.

### Page 15

Ahe wrote: A well stays open, so water comes; hands stay unhurried, so knots loosen. Soft light falls, and even dust is seen.

Dao is like depth with room inside. It lets the world have roads, water, and light.
