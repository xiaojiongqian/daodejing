# 儿童道德经第十二章：其一 眼睛吃太饱，心就饿了

## 第1页

五色令人目盲，五音令人耳聋，五味令人口爽，驰骋畋猎令人心发狂，难得之货令人行妨。

是以圣人为腹不为目，故去彼取此。

## 第2页

第十二章说，眼睛、耳朵、舌头若天天被太强、太多的刺激追着跑，人反而会看不清、听不清、尝不出。颜色、音乐和饭菜本身都不是坏东西。

“五色令人目盲”，是眼睛只顾追热闹，看不见重要的事；“五音令人耳聋”，是声音太满，轻轻提醒和别人话里的难过都被错过。

## 第3页

“五味令人口爽”的“爽”，在古文里是失灵、吃不出味。味道太重，白粥的米香和清水的甘味就容易被错过。

“为腹不为目”，说的是取舍：“腹”是身体和心真正需要的根本；“目”是外面闪闪发亮、让人想追的诱惑。

## 第4页

“五色”指的是各种好看的颜色。

“五音”指的是各种声音和音乐。

“五味”指的是酸、甜、苦、辣、咸等各种味道。

## 第5页

“驰骋畋猎”说的是骑马奔跑、打猎追逐。在这里，可以理解成过度追求刺激。

“难得之货”指的是稀奇、少见，让人特别想拥有的东西。

“行妨”的意思是让人的做法走偏，挡住正路。

## 第6页

空空村一年一次的集市到了。

一早，村口挂起彩布，铜铃叮叮响，糖炒栗子的香味从街头滚到街尾。阿林的脚刚踏进集市，眼睛就忙不过来了。

## 第7页

左边有会变色的纸风车，右边有能发亮的小玻璃珠。

前面有人敲鼓，后面有人吹笛，旁边还有卖糖人的喊：“龙形糖人，只剩三条！”

## 第8页

阿林先买了一串最红的糖葫芦，又尝了一口最甜的蜜糕。

甜还没咽下去，他又被一面会反光的小镜子吸住了。镜子里太阳一闪，像有一条小鱼跳进他的眼睛。

## 第9页

小石头在后面喊：“阿林，老人叫我们去帮忙搬米袋！”

鼓声一响，喊声被盖住。阿林只听见摊主说：“这颗蓝珠子很少见，今天不买，明天就没了。”

## 第10页

他摸摸口袋，里面还有一小包豆种。

那是春天留给菜畦的种子。阿林犹豫了一下，还是用它换了那颗蓝珠子。

## 第11页

傍晚，集市散了。阿林抱着一堆小玩意儿回家，头却胀胀的。晚饭是白米饭、青菜和一碗淡汤，他皱皱鼻子：“一点味道也没有。”

老人夹起一片青菜，慢慢嚼：“你今天的舌头，被糖和蜜吵了一天。现在米香站在门口，它听不见了。”

## 第12页

阿林想拿出蓝珠子，却看见装豆种的小布袋空了。春天能长出一畦豆苗的东西，被他换成了一颗只在晚上闪一闪的珠子。

第二天，他去找摊主，摊位已经空了。珠子躺在手心，还是很亮，他却不想再看了。

## 第13页

老人没有责备他，只递给他一碗热粥。

阿林这一次吃得很慢。米粒软软地散开，一点淡淡的甜从舌头底下出来。他才发现，淡味也是一种味道，只是需要安静一点的舌头。

## 第14页

饭后，他把小玩意儿收进盒子，留下一个纸风车，把蓝珠子放到窗边。他找到小石头，一起把没搬完的米袋挪进仓里。

仓门关上时，阿林听见米袋落地的闷响，也听见老人轻轻咳了一声。昨天被鼓和叫卖盖住的声音，今天都回来了。

## 第15页

集市有热闹，糖有甜，第十二章并没有说它们不好。可感官一直追亮、追响、追重，心就会被牵着跑，错过呼唤，也把豆种换成珠子。

“圣人为腹不为目”，是把真正养人的东西放在前面。眼睛可以看热闹；心安静时，也知道什么该留下，什么该放过。

# Chapter 012-1: When the Eyes Eat Too Much, the Heart Goes Hungry

## Page 1

Colour's five hues from th' eyes their sight will take;
Music's five notes the ears as deaf can make;
The flavours five deprive the mouth of taste;
The chariot course, and the wild hunting waste
Make mad the mind; and objects rare and strange,
Sought for, men's conduct will to evil change.

Therefore the sage seeks to satisfy (the craving of) the belly, and not the (insatiable longing of the) eyes.  He puts from him the latter, and prefers to seek the former.

## Page 2

Chapter Twelve says that too much stimulation can leave the eyes, ears, and tongue less clear. Colors, music, and food are not bad in themselves.

"Five hues take away sight" describes eyes chasing excitement until they miss what matters. "Five notes make the ears deaf" describes sound so full that quiet reminders and the sadness in another person's words go unheard.

## Page 3

The line about flavors means that heavy tastes can make the mouth lose its natural knowing. A tongue trained by strong sweetness and saltiness may miss the fragrance of plain rice and the sweetness of water.

"For the belly, not for the eyes" is about what to take and what to set aside. The belly is what body and heart truly need; the eyes are the shiny things outside that ask to be chased.

## Page 4

The phrase “five hues” means many kinds of colors.

The phrase “five notes” means many kinds of sounds and music.

The phrase “flavours five” means sour, sweet, bitter, spicy, salty, and other tastes.

## Page 5

The phrase “wild hunting” means chasing excitement too fiercely.

The phrase “objects rare and strange” means unusual things that make people want to own them.

The word “conduct” means how a person behaves and chooses.

## Page 6

The yearly market came to Kongkong Village.

Early in the morning, colored cloths hung at the village gate, copper bells rang, and the smell of roasted chestnuts rolled from one end of the street to the other. As soon as Lin stepped in, his eyes had too much work to do.

## Page 7

On the left were paper pinwheels that changed color. On the right were small glass beads that shone.

Ahead, someone beat a drum. Behind him, someone played a flute. Beside him, a candy seller called, "Dragon candy, only three left!"

## Page 8

Lin first bought the reddest candied hawthorn, then tasted the sweetest honey cake.

Before the sweetness had gone down, a tiny shining mirror caught him. Sunlight flashed in it like a fish leaping into his eyes.

## Page 9

Little Stone called from behind, "Lin, the old man asked us to help carry rice sacks!"

The drum covered the call. Lin only heard the seller say, "This blue bead is rare. If you do not buy it today, it will be gone tomorrow."

## Page 10

He touched his pocket. Inside was a small packet of bean seeds.

They were seeds saved for the spring vegetable patch. Lin hesitated, then traded them for the blue bead.

## Page 11

At dusk, the market closed. Lin carried home a heap of small things, but his head felt heavy. Supper was plain rice, greens, and light soup. He wrinkled his nose. "There is no taste."

The old man picked up a leaf of greens and chewed slowly. "Sugar and honey have been shouting at your tongue all day. Now the fragrance of rice is at the door, and your tongue cannot hear it."

## Page 12

Lin reached for the blue bead, then saw that the little seed bag was empty. Something that could have grown a row of bean plants in spring had become a bead that flashed for one evening.

The next day, he went to find the seller, but the stall was gone. The bead still shone in his palm, yet he no longer wanted to look at it.

## Page 13

The old man did not scold him. He only handed him a bowl of warm porridge.

This time Lin ate slowly. The rice grains softened and opened, and a faint sweetness came from under his tongue. He discovered that plain flavor has a taste of its own. It only needs a quieter tongue.

## Page 14

After the meal, Lin put the little market things into a box. He kept one paper pinwheel and placed the blue bead by the window. Then he found Little Stone, and together they carried the remaining rice sacks into the storehouse.

When the door closed, Lin heard the dull thump of the sacks and the old man's soft cough. The sounds buried by drums and shouting yesterday had returned.

## Page 15

The market has its liveliness, and sugar has its sweetness. Chapter Twelve does not call them bad. But senses that always chase brightness, loudness, and heavy flavors can pull the heart away until we miss a call and trade bean seeds for a bead.

"For the belly, not for the eyes" means putting what truly nourishes first. The eyes may enjoy the excitement; a quiet heart knows what to keep and what to let pass.
