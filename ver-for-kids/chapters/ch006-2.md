# 儿童道德经第六章：其二 沉默里的声音

## 典故引入

### 第1页

一九五二年夏天，钢琴家大卫·都铎走上伍德斯托克的舞台，坐在钢琴前。

他按下计时器，分三段坐完四分三十三秒，却没有弹下一个音。

### 第2页

观众先是疑惑，有人不安，也有人觉得自己被开了玩笑。

可是安静一坐下来，风声、雨声、椅子的轻响、人的咳嗽和呼吸，都慢慢浮出来。

### 第3页

演出结束时，许多人仍不能接受它。可后来，这首《四分三十三秒》成了二十世纪最让人争论、也最有影响的作品之一。

它让人重新问：音乐只能是手指弹出的音符吗？

### 第4页

凯奇想让人听见：沉默不是死掉的空白。人弄出来的声音退开时，世界自己的声音就出现了。

这很像“谷神不死”：山谷不发自己的大声音，却让万声经过、显现、回应。

### 第5页

古希腊的阿基米德，曾为一顶王冠想破了头。国王要他看看，王冠里是不是掺了别的金属，又不许把王冠弄坏。

他画图、计算、苦想，越急越卡住。

### 第6页

一天，他去洗澡，身体进水，水从盆边漫出来。他忽然明白：物体占了多少地方，水会替它说出来。

传说他后来就用这个办法，比较王冠和一样重的金子，查出了里面的秘密。

### 第7页

答案在心松开、里面重新有了空处的时候冒出来。硬挤，它不来。

阿基米德已经想了很久，心里才有一个能接住答案的山谷。心一松，答案就来了。

### 第8页

澳大利亚许多原住民传统里，有“歌之径”。在辽阔干燥的土地上，地图画在歌里。

歌里记着山岗、树、沙地和水洼，也记着从这里到那里的顺序。

### 第9页

旅人唱到哪里，脚步就知道往哪里走。唱到水洼，就知道哪里能喝水；唱到山脊，就知道方向要转。

路藏在声音里，绵绵若存。看不见摸不着，却能带人穿过大地。

### 第10页

这样的歌是生命的记忆。它把人、土地、祖先和水源连在一起。

真正的根，不一定长在看得见的地方；有时它藏在记忆、呼吸和声音里。

### 第11页

美国西南部有科罗拉多大峡谷。它不是被一把巨斧劈开的。

科罗拉多河在漫长岁月里流过，日复一日，年复一年，慢慢把岩石磨出深谷。

### 第12页

水看起来柔，时间看起来慢。可柔水不停，慢也会有大力量。

大峡谷的岩层一页一页露出来，像大地打开一本很厚的书。

### 第13页

科罗拉多河只是一直流，一直没停。不停，就是它的力量。

这也是“绵绵”：不急着冒头，却从来不真的断掉。

## 回扣

### 第14页

凯奇的沉默，讲“空能显现”：琴不响，世界的声音反而出现。

阿基米德的放松，讲“静能回应”：心不被逼紧，答案才浮上来。

### 第15页

歌之径讲“根不一定可见”：路在歌里，生命在记忆里延续。

大峡谷讲“绵绵若存”：水细细地流，因不断而开出深谷。

### 第16页

第六章的“谷神”，是空着却能回应、安静却能生养的深处。它不是神仙鬼怪。

“玄牝之门”，是生命不断出来的深深开口。它也不是一扇木门。

### 第17页

所以“用之不勤”，是说用的时候别把源头用干。

读书要认真，也要让心有空；帮助人要真诚，也别把自己累坏。

### 第18页

阿禾写下：空出来，才听得见声音；静下来，才等得到答案；慢下来，水就磨出了深谷。

像山谷留住空，像河水一直流，生命才会绵绵地在。

# Chapter 006-2: Sounds Inside Silence

## Allusion

### Page 1

In the summer of nineteen fifty-two, pianist David Tudor walked onto a stage in Woodstock and sat at the piano.

He started a timer and sat through three movements lasting four minutes and thirty-three seconds, without playing a note.

### Page 2

At first, the audience was puzzled. Some grew uneasy; some felt as if a joke had been played on them.

But once quiet settled in, wind, rain, creaking chairs, coughs, and breathing slowly came forward.

### Page 3

When the performance ended, many people still could not accept it. Yet later, *Four Minutes and Thirty-Three Seconds* became one of the most argued-about and most influential works of the twentieth century.

It made people ask again: must music be only the notes played by fingers?

### Page 4

Cage wanted people to hear this: silence is not dead blankness. When the sounds people make step back, the world's own sounds appear.

This is close to "the valley spirit dies not": the valley does not make a loud sound of its own, yet all sounds pass, appear, and answer there.

### Page 5

In ancient Greece, Archimedes struggled over a king's crown. The king wanted to know whether other metal had been mixed into it, but the crown must not be damaged.

Archimedes drew, calculated, and worried. The harder he pushed, the more stuck he felt.

### Page 6

One day, he stepped into a bath and water spilled over the edge. Suddenly he saw that water could reveal how much space an object took.

The story says he later used this idea to compare the crown with gold of the same weight, and found out its secret.

### Page 7

The answer surfaced when the mind loosened and inner space returned. Force it, and it stays away.

Archimedes had already thought for a long time, so his mind had a valley ready to hold the answer. Once his mind loosened, the answer came.

### Page 8

In many Australian Aboriginal traditions, there are Songlines. Across wide, dry lands, the map is drawn in song.

The songs remember hills, trees, sand, waterholes, and the order from one place to the next.

### Page 9

As travelers sing, their feet know where to go. When the song reaches a waterhole, they know where to drink; when it reaches a ridge, they know where to turn.

The road lives inside sound: long and unbroken. It cannot be seen or touched, yet it can carry people across the land.

### Page 10

Such a song is living memory, joining people, land, ancestors, and water.

A true root does not always grow where we can see it. Sometimes it lives inside memory, breath, and sound.

### Page 11

In the American Southwest lies the Grand Canyon. It was not opened by a giant axe.

The Colorado River flowed through it over vast time, day after day and year after year, slowly shaping rock into a deep valley.

### Page 12

Water looks soft, and time looks slow. But soft water that does not stop can have great power.

Layer after layer of rock appears, like the earth opening a very thick book.

### Page 13

The Colorado River just kept flowing, and never stopped. Not stopping was its strength.

This too is "long and unbroken": not rushing to show itself, yet never truly cut off.

## Return to the Original

### Page 14

Cage's silence teaches that emptiness can reveal: when the piano does not sound, the world's sounds appear.

Archimedes' loosened mind teaches that stillness can respond: when the heart is not forced tight, an answer can rise.

### Page 15

The Songlines teach that roots are not always visible: the road lives in song, and life continues in memory.

The Grand Canyon teaches "long and unbroken": water flows thin and steady, and by continuing it opens depth.

### Page 16

The "valley spirit" in Chapter Six is the depth that is empty yet able to answer, quiet yet able to give life. It is not a god or a ghost.

The "gate of the female mystery" is the deep opening from which life keeps coming. It is not a wooden door either.

### Page 17

So "used gently" means drawing on the source without using it dry.

Study seriously, and also leave the mind space. Help people sincerely, and also do not wear yourself out.

### Page 18

Ahe wrote: Go empty, and you can hear sound; go still, and an answer can arrive; go slow, and water opens a deep valley.

Like a valley keeping space, like a river continuing to flow, life can remain softly present.
