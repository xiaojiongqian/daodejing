# 儿童道德经第十四章：其三 安静里听见的声音

## 第1页

二十世纪有位美国作曲家约翰·凯奇，写过一首很特别的作品，名字叫《四分三十三秒》。

特别的地方在于：演出时，钢琴家坐在钢琴前，很久没有弹出一个音符。

## 第2页

小石头一听就笑了：“不弹，也算一首曲子吗？”

老人把一只空碗放在桌上，用筷子轻轻一碰。清清的一声响过，屋里更静了。“先别说话。”他说。

## 第3页

孩子们坐下来。

起初，安静像一件不合身的衣服，大家都想动一动，咳一声，笑一下。

## 第4页

坐定了，柴火先在灶里轻轻裂开。

接着，窗外的竹叶碰到竹叶；远处有人把水桶放到地上，桶底“咚”地一声。

## 第5页

这些声音刚才也在，只是被说话声、笑声和脚步声盖住了。

小石头压低声音：“原来安静不是空的。”老人说：“安静像一间空屋，自己不出声，却让别的声音进来。”

## 第6页

阿林闭上眼。

他听见自己的呼吸，一进一出；又听见衣袖碰到膝盖的轻响。声音很小，平时他根本不会把它们当成声音。

## 第7页

他想到第十四章。

“听之不闻”，不一定是完全没有声音。也可能是那声音太细、太远，不抢人注意。耳朵静下来，它才出现。

## 第8页

老人说，凯奇那首作品像一个问题。

当我们等不到熟悉的旋律，会不会以为一切都没有发生？当舞台上没有明显的声音，会不会忘了整座厅堂其实正在响？

## 第9页

孩子们在学堂里也试了一次。

大家坐好，不故意制造声音。前几息，总有人想笑。笑意落下去，学堂一点一点打开。

## 第10页

他们听见扫帚掠过地面。

一支笔从桌边滚到书上。阿林的肚子咕噜一声，全班笑了。那笑声短短的，也成了这段安静的一部分。

## 第11页

小石头小声说：“如果一直敲鼓，我就听不见这些。”

老人点头：“有些东西很细。你追它，它就退；你安静，它才来。”

## 第12页

第十四章说“听之不闻名曰希”。

“希”像很稀很细的声音，平时被热闹盖住，静下来才露一点边。这样的安静里有生意，不是死寂。

## 第13页

“视之不见”“搏之不得”也像这样。你看不见安静的形状，抓不住它的身体。可没有它，柴火、竹叶、水桶、呼吸，就没有地方被听见。

这就是“无状之状，无物之象”的一点影子。安静没有形状，作用却落在每一处。它站在舞台边上，也一直在场。

## 第14页

耳朵太忙，就听不见细的声音。

心也一样。安静一点，有些很轻的真实终于走近了，世界一点也没变小。

# Chapter 014-3: Sounds Heard Inside Quiet

## Page 1

In the twentieth century, the American composer John Cage made a very unusual work titled “Four Minutes and Thirty-Three Seconds.”

What made it unusual was this: during the performance, the pianist sat at the piano for a long time without playing a note.

## Page 2

Little Stone laughed. "If he did not play, can it still be a piece of music?"

The old man set an empty bowl on the table and touched it lightly with a chopstick. After the clear sound passed, the room seemed quieter. "Do not speak yet," he said.

## Page 3

The children sat down.

At first, quiet felt like clothing that did not fit. Everyone wanted to move, cough, or laugh.

## Page 4

Once they settled, firewood cracked softly in the stove.

Then bamboo leaves touched bamboo leaves outside. Someone far away set a bucket on the ground with a low thump.

## Page 5

These sounds had already been there, covered by talking, laughing, and footsteps.

Little Stone lowered his voice. "So quiet is not empty." The old man said, "Quiet is like an empty room. It makes no sound of its own, yet it lets other sounds in."

## Page 6

Lin closed his eyes.

He heard his own breath going in and out, and the small sound of his sleeve touching his knee. The sounds were so small that he usually would not have counted them as sounds.

## Page 7

He thought of Chapter Fourteen.

"Listening and not hearing" is not always the absence of sound. Something may be so fine and far away that it asks nothing of your attention. Busy ears never meet it; quiet ears do.

## Page 8

The old man said Cage's work was like a question.

When the familiar melody does not come, do we think nothing has happened? When the stage gives no obvious sound, do we forget that the whole hall is sounding?

## Page 9

The children tried it once in the schoolroom too.

Everyone sat without making sounds on purpose. For the first few breaths, someone always wanted to laugh. The laughter settled, and the room opened little by little.

## Page 10

They heard a broom pass over the floor.

A pencil rolled from the desk edge onto a book. Lin's stomach rumbled. The whole class laughed, and that brief laughter became part of the quiet too.

## Page 11

Little Stone whispered, "If drums kept beating, I would not hear these."

The old man nodded. "Some things are very fine. When you chase them, they move away. When you become quiet, they come."

## Page 12

Chapter Fourteen says, "We listen to it, and we do not hear it."

The inaudible is like a very thin sound, usually drowned out by noise and heard only faintly when things grow quiet. This kind of quiet is alive, far from dead silence.

## Page 13

"We look and do not see" and "we grasp and do not hold" are like this too. You cannot see quiet's shape or grasp its body. Without it, firewood, bamboo leaves, buckets, and breath have no room to be heard.

This is a small shadow of "the form of the formless" and "the semblance of the invisible." Quiet has no shape, yet its effect is everywhere. It stands at the edge of the stage, and it is present all the same.

## Page 14

When ears are too busy, they cannot hear fine sounds.

The heart is the same. When it becomes a little quieter, some very light truths come near, and the world has not grown any smaller.
