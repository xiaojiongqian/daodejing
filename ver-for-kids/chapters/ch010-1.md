# 儿童道德经第十章：其一 先把自己收回来

## 第1页

载营魄抱一，能无离乎？
专气致柔，能婴儿乎？
涤除玄览，能无疵乎？
爱国治民，能无知乎？

天门开阖，能为雌乎？
明白四达，能无为乎？
生之，畜之。
生而不有，为而不恃，长而不宰，是谓玄德。

## 第2页

第十章一连问了许多“能不能”：你能不能先把散开的自己收回来？

“载营魄抱一”，可以先理解成身体在这里，心也在这里。人一急，身体明明在桌边，心却跑去争输赢、想面子、怕出错。抱一，就是别让自己散成一地碎片。

## 第3页

“专气致柔”，是让气息慢下来，身子不僵。像婴儿那样柔，不等于没力气；婴儿哭就哭，笑就笑，心里不藏那么多弯。

“涤除玄览”，像擦心里的镜子。灰尘少一点，看东西就少一点歪；心急时，事情本身未必坏了。

## 第4页

最后几句最深：“生而不有，为而不恃，长而不宰。”

养大它，却不把它当成自己的东西；帮了忙，却不拿帮助压人；看着它长，却不把自己当成它的主人。

## 第5页

“营魄”是古人用来讲人的身心、气血和精神的词。给孩子读时，可以把它理解成“身体和心神”。

“抱一”的意思是抱住一个中心，不让心乱跑。

“致柔”的意思是变得柔和。柔，是身子不僵，遇事不硬顶。

## 第6页

“玄览”指的是很深的观看。这里可以把它想成心里那面照见事情的镜子。

“无为”的意思是不乱插手，也不硬控制。该做的做，不该扯的别扯。

“玄德”指的是很深、不张扬的好品德。它像土里的根，看不见，却养着树。

## 第7页

春天，老人给孩子们一盘小苗，让他们分种到村口。

阿林被大家推出来带队。他端着木盘走在最前面，嘴上不停：“慢一点！别碰叶子！这一排要直！”

## 第8页

他越想负责，心越忙。

脚下有块小石头，他没有看见。木盘一斜，几团湿土滚到边上，小苗东倒西歪，像被惊醒的孩子。

## 第9页

阿林的脸一下红了。他赶紧伸手，把一株小苗往直里扳。他越急，手越重，细细的茎“啪”地折了一点。

孩子们都不说话了。阿林的手停在半空。他本来想照顾它，结果自己的急，把它弄伤了。

## 第10页

老人蹲下来，没有先碰小苗。他说：“先别忙着管它们。你先回来。”

“我不是在这里吗？”阿林低声说。

老人指了指他的脚，又指了指他的胸口：“脚在这里，心跑到‘我负责’和‘不能出错’里去了。心不在，手就会重。”

## 第11页

老人让他坐在石阶上，把木盘放稳。

阿林听见自己的气息，一口进来，一口出去。刚才硬硬的肩膀，慢慢松了一点；攥紧的手，也松了一点。

## 第12页

老人拿来一面蒙着薄灰的旧铜镜。阿林用布轻轻擦，灰退去，天光出来了，他的脸也清楚了。

“心急的时候，看什么都像歪的。”老人说，“镜子先清一点，手才不乱。”

## 第13页

阿林再去看那株小苗。它受了伤，但根还在，需要土、水和一点安静。

他给小苗添土，浇水，又找一根细竹签轻轻扶住。这一次，他把能做的做好，然后把手收回来，不再催它，也不去扳它。

## 第14页

几天后，小苗没有立刻长高，却活了下来。

新叶从旁边慢慢冒出来。有人经过，夸阿林照顾得细。阿林看见叶子向着阳光，心里反而不想把功劳拿出来晃。

## 第15页

阿林想到那天的木盘：身心在一起，手才稳；气息柔下来，镜子擦清，眼睛才看得明。“明白四达，能无为乎”，就是看见问题，也不乱抓。

“生而不有，为而不恃，长而不宰”，就是最深的德。帮它立住，不等于拥有它；照顾它长，也不必当它的主人。

# Chapter 010-1: First Gather Yourself Back

## Page 1

When the intelligent and animal souls are held together in one embrace, they can be kept from separating.  When one gives undivided attention to the (vital) breath, and brings it to the utmost degree of pliancy, he can become as a (tender) babe.  When he has cleansed away the most mysterious sights (of his imagination), he can become without a flaw.

In loving the people and ruling the state, cannot he proceed without any (purpose of) action?  In the opening and shutting of his gates of heaven, cannot he do so as a female bird?  While his intelligence reaches in every direction, cannot he (appear to) be without knowledge?

(The Tao) produces (all things) and nourishes them; it produces them and does not claim them as its own; it does all, and yet does not boast of it; it presides over all, and yet does not control them. This is what is called 'The mysterious Quality' (of the Tao).

## Page 2

Chapter Ten asks many "can you?" questions. The first is this: can you gather your scattered self back?

"Held together in one embrace" can first mean that the body is here, and the heart is here too. When we hurry, the body may be beside the table while the heart runs off to winning, face, or fear of mistakes. To hold one is to keep from scattering into pieces.

## Page 3

"Giving undivided attention to the breath" means slowing down and softening the body. A baby's pliancy is not weakness: babies cry and smile freely, with fewer twists hidden in the heart.

"Cleansed away" is like wiping the heart's mirror. Less dust means less distortion; when the heart is rushed, the matter itself may still be fine.

## Page 4

The last lines go deepest: produce and nourish, yet do not claim, boast, or control.

Help something grow without possessing it. Help someone without pressing them down with your help. Watch life grow without making yourself its ruler.

## Page 5

The phrase “held together in one embrace” means gathered into one, not scattered.

The phrase “undivided attention” means attention that is not split among many worries.

The word “breath” means the air moving in and out. Here it also points to inner steadiness.

## Page 6

The word “pliancy” means softness and flexibility, not stiffness.

The phrase “cleansed away” means wiped clean.

The phrase “mysterious Quality” means a deep, quiet kind of goodness that is hard to see, like roots under the soil.

## Page 7

In spring, the old man gave the children a tray of young shoots to plant by the village gate.

Lin was chosen to lead the group. He carried the wooden tray at the front and kept saying, "Slow down! Don't touch the leaves! This row must be straight!"

## Page 8

The more responsible he wanted to be, the busier his heart became.

There was a small stone at his feet, but he did not see it. The tray tilted, clumps of wet soil rolled to the edge, and the shoots leaned in every direction like startled children.

## Page 9

Lin's face reddened. He hurried to straighten one shoot, and his anxious hand grew heavy. The thin stem snapped a little.

The children fell silent. Lin's hand stopped in midair. He had wanted to care for the shoot, but his own hurry had hurt it.

## Page 10

The old man crouched down without touching the shoot. "Do not rush to manage them," he said. "Come back first."

"Aren't I here?" Lin asked softly.

The old man pointed to his feet and then to his chest. "Your feet are here, but your heart has run into 'I am responsible' and 'I must not make a mistake.' When the heart is absent, the hand grows heavy."

## Page 11

He told Lin to sit on the stone step and set the tray down steadily.

Lin heard his own breathing: one breath entering, one breath leaving. His hard shoulders slowly loosened, and so did his clenched hands.

## Page 12

The old man brought a bronze mirror covered with thin dust. Lin wiped it gently. The dust cleared, daylight returned, and his face became clear too.

"When the heart is rushed, everything looks crooked," said the old man. "Let the mirror clear first, and the hands will not be so wild."

## Page 13

Lin looked again at the hurt shoot. It was injured, but its root was still there. It needed soil, water, and a little quiet.

He added soil, watered it, and placed a thin bamboo stick beside it for support. This time, he did what he could and then drew back his hands. He stopped urging it to grow and stopped pulling at it.

## Page 14

A few days later, the shoot was still short, but it was alive.

A new leaf slowly came out from the side. Someone passing by praised Lin for his care. He watched the leaf turn toward the sun and no longer wanted to wave his credit around.

## Page 15

Lin thought of the wooden tray: body and heart together steadied his hands; soft breath and a clear mirror helped him see. "While intelligence reaches everywhere, can one proceed without action?" means seeing a problem without grabbing wildly.

"Producing and nourishing, yet not claiming or controlling" is the deepest virtue. Helping the shoot stand does not make it yours; caring for its growth does not make you its ruler.
