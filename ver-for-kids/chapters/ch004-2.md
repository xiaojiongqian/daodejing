# 儿童道德经第四章：其二 不刺眼的光和看不见的深处

## 第1页

庄子讲过“空船”的故事。一个人坐船过河，被一条没人的船轻轻撞到，多半只是伸手把它推开。

可如果船上站着一个人，他就容易生气，觉得对方一定是故意撞他。

## 第2页

撞击还是一样轻。可他心里多出一个尖角：“他肯定是故意的。”

第四章说“挫其锐”，先要看的是自己的尖刺是从哪里冒出来的。

## 第3页

唐朝安史之乱过去以后，老将军郭子仪功劳很大，名声也很高。有人在皇帝面前说了他的坏话，皇帝担心他手里的兵太多，忽然收走了他的军队。

立过大功却被怀疑，从前的将士又都听他的话，这正是最容易长出尖刺的时候。

## 第4页

郭子仪没有叫来从前的将士，也没有拍桌子为自己争辩。皇帝让他去修皇陵，他就去了；他还把皇帝的旧诏书和机密文书，一件一件交还回去。

这些东西留在手里，可能被人看成一把刀。他把它们放到明处，疑心就少了可以抓住的影子。

## 第5页

后来，仇家派人挖了他父亲的坟。满朝的人都以为他一定会报复，这一报复，可能又要死很多人。

可是他没有报复。他先说自己：我带兵这么多年，手下的士兵也打扰过百姓，也伤过别人的坟；今天我家遇到这样的事，是我做得还不够好。

## 第6页

别人等着看刀光，他先把报仇的火收了回来。尖刺一收，乱子就找不到继续长大的路。

这也是“解纷”：有时候，就是让怨气找不到地方再添柴。

## 第7页

还有一位谋士叫李泌。他面对叛军的时候，也懂这个道理。叛军来势很凶，像一把刚磨亮的刀。

他看见了这把刀的弱点：许多人来打仗，是为了抢东西；他们虽然凶猛，却熬不了太久。

## 第8页

他的办法是堵住要害的路，守住重要的城，让敌军在范阳和长安之间来回奔跑。

尖刺如果总找不到地方扎进去，就会在奔跑、挨饿和拖延里慢慢变钝。

## 第9页

可皇帝急着打回那两座都城，没有全听他的。眼前的胜仗来得快，深处的乱根却没有除干净。

第四章说“解其纷”，要顺着乱线一直找到暗处的线头。线头如果还留着，过些时候又要缠起来。

## 第10页

再说一位荷兰画家，名叫维米尔。他住在小城代尔夫特，常常画窗边的牛奶、信件和安静的脸。

他的光很轻，像怕惊动桌上的灰尘。

## 第11页

《戴珍珠耳环的少女》里，女孩回过头，暗处有一颗小小的珍珠。几百年后，那点柔光仍让人在画前停下。

这就是“和其光，同其尘”：光走进普通房间，照见普通人，也照见桌上的尘土。

## 第12页

贝多芬是一位作曲家，后来几乎听不见外面的声音。对音乐家来说，掌声、乐队和自己的琴声，都一点点退到听不见的地方。可他心里的声音仍在生长。

晚年《第九交响曲》首演时，他背对观众，听不见掌声。一位歌手轻轻拉他转过身，他才看见，所有人都站了起来。

## 第13页

那一刻，“湛兮似或存”变得很清楚：外面的声音走远了，深处的那个声音还在。

它绕过耳朵，走进了许多人的心里。

## 第14页

空船让“他肯定是故意的”那个尖角落下；郭子仪收起报仇的火，皇帝的疑心也少了可抓的影子。这是“挫其锐”：让力量不先伤人。

李泌先找线头和燃料，让叛军在奔跑、挨饿和拖延里变钝。这是“解纷”：顺着乱线找到深处，别让它越缠越死。

## 第15页

维米尔把光放进普通房间，贝多芬在听不见以后守住深处的声音。他们让阿林看见“和光同尘”“湛兮似存”：深的东西站在低处，也能长久地照人、动人。

第四章说，道留着空处，所以能用；锐劲收柔，才能走进世界。空着，水才来；手不急，结才松。光柔下来，尘土也能亮。

# Chapter 004-2: Gentle Light and the Invisible Depth

## Page 1

Zhuangzi tells the story of an empty boat. A person crossing a river is bumped by a boat with no one inside, and he will probably just push it away.

But if someone stands in that boat, anger easily rises. He thinks the person must have hit him on purpose.

## Page 2

The bump is just as light as before. But a sharp point has grown in his heart: "He must have done it on purpose."

Chapter Four says, "Blunt sharpness." That begins with seeing where our own sharp points come from.

## Page 3

In the Tang dynasty, after the An Lushan Rebellion was over, the old general Guo Ziyi had won great merit and a great name. Someone spoke bad words about him to the emperor. The emperor feared the old general held too many soldiers, and he took the army away.

Being doubted after such service, with old soldiers still loyal to him: this was exactly the moment when a person could grow sharp points.

## Page 4

Guo Ziyi did not call his old soldiers back, and he did not slam the table to argue for himself. The emperor told him to repair the imperial tombs, and he went. He also handed back the old imperial letters and secret papers, one by one.

If those things stayed in his hands, people might see them as blades. He put them out in the open, and suspicion had fewer shadows to grasp.

## Page 5

Later, an enemy had his father's grave dug up. The whole court expected him to take revenge, and revenge could have pulled many people into killing.

But he did not take revenge. He first blamed himself: In all my years of war, my soldiers also troubled people and harmed graves. What has happened to my family today shows that I have not done well enough.

## Page 6

People waited to see blades. He pulled the fire of revenge back first. Once the sharp point softened, the tangle found no road to grow.

This too is "unraveling complications." Sometimes it means giving anger nowhere to add more fuel.

## Page 7

There was also an adviser named Li Bi. He understood this when he faced the rebel armies. The rebels came fiercely, like a newly sharpened blade.

He saw the weakness inside that blade: many of them had come to steal and plunder. They were fierce, but they could not hold out for long.

## Page 8

His plan was to hold the key places and cut the key roads, making the rebels run back and forth between Fanyang and Chang'an.

If a sharp point never finds a place to stab, it slowly grows dull through running, hunger, and delay.

## Page 9

But the emperor wanted the two capital cities back quickly and followed the plan only in part. Victories came fast, while the deep root of the trouble was not pulled out.

Chapter Four's "unraveling complications" means following the tangle until you find the hidden thread. If the thread is left in place, the tangle comes back later.

## Page 10

Now look at a Dutch painter named Vermeer. He lived in the small city of Delft and often painted milk by a window, letters, and quiet faces.

His light is gentle, as if he does not want to startle the dust on the table.

## Page 11

In *Girl with a Pearl Earring*, a girl turns her head, a small pearl bright in the dark. Centuries later, that soft light still makes people stop before the painting.

This is "tempering brightness and joining the dust": light enters an ordinary room and falls on ordinary people, and on the dust on the table.

## Page 12

Beethoven was a composer who later could barely hear the world outside. Applause, orchestras, and even his own piano faded beyond hearing, yet an inner sound kept growing in him.

In his later years, at the first performance of the Ninth Symphony, he stood with his back to the audience and could not hear the applause. A singer gently turned him around, and only then did he see everyone standing.

## Page 13

At that moment, "pure and still, as if present" became clear: the sound outside had moved far away, and the deep sound was still there.

It went around the ears and walked into many hearts.

## Page 14

The empty boat lets the sharp point of "he must have meant it" fall away. Guo Ziyi pulls back the fire of revenge, and the emperor's suspicion finds fewer shadows to grasp. This is blunting sharpness: strength stops short of wounding.

Li Bi looks for the thread and the fuel, letting the rebels grow dull through running, hunger, and delay. This is unraveling complications: follow the tangle to its depth before it becomes a dead knot.

## Page 15

Vermeer places light in ordinary rooms. Beethoven, after losing his hearing, keeps a deeper sound. They show Lin "tempering brightness," "joining the dust," and "being present in hidden depth": deep things stay low, yet they can shine and move people for a long time.

Chapter Four says Dao keeps room inside, so it can be used; it softens its sharpness, so it can enter the world. Water comes where there is room; knots loosen where hands are unhurried. Where light is gentle, even dust can shine.
