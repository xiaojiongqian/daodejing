# 儿童道德经第十五章：其二 雪把路藏起来

## 第1页

关于拿破仑，西方流传着一句很有名的话，大意是：他的字典里没有“不可能”。

这句话听起来像一面亮亮的旗。可第十五章问的，偏偏是另一件事：冬天过河的人，为什么不敢把话说得太满？

## 第2页

拿破仑年轻时很会打仗。一次次胜利，让许多人相信他，也让他越来越相信自己。地图摊开时，山河像线，城池像点，远方好像只要一声命令就会靠近。

失败会让人低头看脚下，连胜却容易让人只看前方。连胜也藏着危险。

## 第3页

一八一二年，他带着大军进入俄国。

地图上的路很细，也很干净。可真正的路有泥，有空村庄，有越拉越长的队伍，还有慢慢压低的天色。

## 第4页

一开始，旗帜还多，马蹄还响。

士兵们相信他们会像从前那样赢。可是走得越深，粮食越难找，马越疲惫，夜里的寒气越来越像一只看不见的手。

## 第5页

俄国的冬天没有大声说话。

它只是一天比一天冷。河面结住，路冻硬，雪落下来，把车辙盖住，也把人的判断一点一点盖住。

## 第6页

如果心里还留着“冬涉川”的那份小心，人会停下来问：

粮够不够？马还能走多久？退路在哪里？现在往前，是为了胜利，还是为了不肯承认自己该停？

## 第7页

可是太满的自信不喜欢这些问题。

它觉得问题扫兴，提醒刺耳，停一步就像输了。它只听见“向前”，听不见雪落在帐篷上的声音。

## 第8页

后来，队伍往回走。

来时的路已经不是来时的路。白雪盖住地面，寒冷钻进衣缝，许多人再也看不见故乡。

## 第9页

老人讲到这里，停了一会儿。

屋外也在下小雪。阿林听见雪落在竹叶上，轻轻一声，又轻轻一声。

## 第10页

小石头问：“那是不是不能说‘我一定行’？”

老人说：“可以有志气。没有志气，船不开，桥不搭，路也没人走。可是志气要带着耳朵，听得见风、水和别人说‘慢一点’，也听得见自己心里那句‘我是不是太满了’。”

## 第11页

阿林想起自己做小木桥。

他觉得两块木板就够，小石头提醒要垫石头，他嫌麻烦。第一场雨来，一头木板陷进泥里，桥歪了，大家只好绕路。

## 第12页

那只是一座小木桥。

可心里的毛病，和雪原上那支大军一样：一旦觉得“我想到了，就一定行”，耳朵就关上一半；一旦嫌提醒麻烦，路就开始在脚下变软。

## 第13页

老人说：“真正有本事的人，不急着显得什么都不怕。”

“他怕错一步会害人，怕看漏一点会误事。这个怕，不把他吓住，只把他的脚步放稳。”

## 第14页

阿林看着窗外的雪。

雪落得很轻，却能把整条路盖住。他懂了：有些危险越是安静，越要小心听。

## 第15页

“豫焉若冬涉川”，冬天的河不会因勇敢变暖，雪路也不会因自信让开。“犹兮若畏四邻”，提醒人看见天气、地势、人心、粮草和退路。

“保此道者不欲盈”，心太满装不下提醒，话太满听不见雪声。字典里的“不可能”可以留着，勇敢仍要带着小心：能进能停，能看地图，也能低头看雪。

# Chapter 015-2: Snow Hides the Road

## Page 1

A famous saying is often connected with Napoleon: in his dictionary, there was no "impossible."

The saying sounds like a bright flag. Chapter Fifteen asks something else: why does a person crossing a winter river avoid speaking with complete certainty?

## Page 2

Napoleon was very gifted in war when young. Victory after victory made many people believe in him, and made him believe more and more in himself. When a map opened, mountains and rivers seemed like lines, cities like dots, and distance as if it could be brought near by one command.

Failure makes people lower their heads and look at their feet. Repeated victory can make them look only ahead, and that carries its own danger.

## Page 3

In the year eighteen twelve, he led a great army into Russia.

On paper, the road was thin and clean. But the real road had mud, empty villages, a line of soldiers stretching longer and longer, and a sky slowly pressing lower.

## Page 4

At first, there were many flags, and hoofbeats were strong.

The soldiers believed they would win as before. But the deeper they went, the harder food was to find, the more tired the horses became, and the night's cold felt more and more like an invisible hand.

## Page 5

The Russian winter did not speak loudly.

It simply grew colder day by day. Rivers froze, roads hardened, and snow fell, covering wheel tracks and slowly covering human judgment too.

## Page 6

If the heart still keeps the caution of crossing a river in winter, it stops and asks:

Is there enough food? How long can the horses walk? Where is the way back? Am I moving forward for victory, or because I refuse to admit I should stop?

## Page 7

Overfull confidence dislikes such questions.

It finds questions tiresome and reminders unpleasant. Even a pause feels like defeat. It hears only "forward" and not snow falling on the tents.

## Page 8

Later, the army turned back.

The road they had taken was no longer the same road. White snow covered the ground, cold entered through seams, and many people never saw home again.

## Page 9

The old man stopped for a while here.

Outside, light snow was falling too. Lin heard it touch bamboo leaves, softly once, then softly again.

## Page 10

Little Stone asked, "Does that mean we should not say, 'I can do it'?"

The old man said, "Ambition is allowed. Without it, boats do not launch, bridges are not built, and roads go untraveled. But ambition needs ears. It must hear wind, water, another person saying 'slow down,' and the voice inside asking, 'Am I too full?'"

## Page 11

Lin remembered a small wooden bridge he had made.

He thought two planks were enough. Little Stone reminded him to set stones underneath, but he found it troublesome. The first rain came, one end sank into mud, the bridge tilted, and everyone had to go around.

## Page 12

That was only a small wooden bridge.

But the same habit of mind was at work in that army on the snowfield. Once we think, "I thought of it, so it must work," the ears half close. Once reminders feel troublesome, the road begins to soften underfoot.

## Page 13

The old man said, "A truly capable person does not hurry to look fearless."

"He fears that one wrong step may harm others, and one missed detail may spoil the matter. That fear does not freeze him; it steadies his feet."

## Page 14

Lin watched the snow outside.

Snow fell lightly, yet it could hide a whole road. He understood: the quieter a danger is, the more carefully one must listen.

## Page 15

"Like those who wade through a stream in winter": a winter river does not grow warm because you are brave, and a snow road does not move aside because you are confident. "Like those afraid of all around them" reminds us to see weather, land, people's hearts, supplies, and the road back.

"Those who preserve this method do not wish to be full": a heart too full has no room for reminders, and words too full cannot hear snow. The "impossible" in the dictionary can stay. Courage still walks with care: move when you can, stop when you must, read the map, and look down at the snow.
