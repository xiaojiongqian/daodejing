# 儿童道德经第十五章：其一 像冬天过河的人

## 第1页

古之善为士者，微妙玄通，深不可识。
夫唯不可识，故强为之容。

豫焉若冬涉川，犹兮若畏四邻，俨兮其若容，涣兮若冰之将释，敦兮其若朴，旷兮其若谷，混兮其若浊。

孰能浊以静之徐清？
孰能安以久动之徐生？

保此道者不欲盈，夫唯不盈，故能蔽不新成。

## 第2页

第十五章说，古时候真正懂道的人，很深，很通，很难用外表看出来。

因为看不清，老子只好勉强用一串比喻描写他们。

## 第3页

他们像冬天过河的人，小心不莽撞；像警觉四周的人，不自大；像到别人家做客的人，庄重不放肆。

他们又像将融的冰，不僵硬；像未雕刻的木头，厚实朴素；像空旷的山谷，能容回声；也像浑水，不装得很清亮。

## 第4页

“孰能浊以静之徐清”，浑水安静下来，泥沙会慢慢沉下去，人心也会慢慢清楚。“孰能安以久动之徐生”，安定不是死住不动，安稳后还要慢慢起作用，像种子发芽。

“不欲盈”是不把自己装满，不急着显得完整、崭新、了不起。正因为不满，反而能长久。

## 第5页

“微妙玄通”指想法细、看得远，又能把事情想通。“豫焉”是小心、不慌。“冬涉川”是冬天过河，水冷冰滑，必须谨慎。

“俨兮”是庄重端正。“敦兮其若朴”是厚实得像未雕刻的木头。“混兮其若浊”是外表像浑水，不故意显得自己多干净。

## 第6页

冬天，空空村的小溪结了一层薄冰。

阿林的纸鸢被风吹到对岸的柳枝上，红尾巴在冷风里一摆一摆。

## 第7页

小石头跺脚：“我跑过去拿！”他刚迈出去，冰面发出细细的“喀”一声，老人立刻伸手拦住他。

“河不像晒谷场。晒谷场跑快了，最多摔一跤；冬天过河，脚下一错，冷水会从鞋口灌进来。”

## 第8页

老人拿一根长竹竿，先探一探冰边。

他没有马上下脚，而是看水色，听冰声，又找石头露出的浅处。孩子们站在岸边，第一次觉得“慢”也有重量。

## 第9页

老人一步一步走。

每一步都不像害怕得不敢动，也不像硬撑着乱冲。他把竹竿点在前面，脚跟着过去，像是在和河商量。

## 第10页

到了对岸，他没有急着摘纸鸢。

他先站稳，再伸手。柳枝弹了一下，雪粒落到他的肩上。他笑了笑，把纸鸢解下来。

## 第11页

回来时，阿林问：“您是不是很怕？”

老人说：“怕一点，正好。完全不怕，脚就会替头脑做主；怕得不能动，也过不了河。”

## 第12页

回到屋里，老人倒了一杯刚打上来的井水。水有点浑，细沙在杯里翻。小石头想用筷子搅：“我帮它快点清。”

老人轻轻按住他的手：“你一搅，它更浑。让它放一会儿。”孩子们看着沙粒慢慢落下，水面一点一点亮起来。

## 第13页

阿林懂了。

冬天过河要慢，浑水变清也要慢。慢下来，心急就不会把路弄坏。

## 第14页

晚上，他把纸鸢放在桌上。

红尾巴有一点破，竹骨也旧了。阿林没有急着做一个全新的，只用细线把破处补好。旧纸鸢第二天照样能飞。

## 第15页

“豫焉若冬涉川”，懂水的人知道何时快、何时慢。“孰能浊以静之徐清”，心里有气、有急、有怕，越搅越浑；停一停，事情会露出底。

“保此道者不欲盈”，像旧纸鸢，破一点可以修，旧东西也能继续飞。真正稳的人心里存着小心，脚下有分寸；慢一点，是让力量找到路。

# Chapter 015-1: Like Someone Crossing a River in Winter

## Page 1

The skilful masters (of the Tao) in old times, with a subtle and exquisite penetration, comprehended its mysteries, and were deep (also) so as to elude men's knowledge.  As they were thus beyond men's knowledge, I will make an effort to describe of what sort they appeared to be.

Shrinking looked they like those who wade through a stream in winter; irresolute like those who are afraid of all around them; grave like a guest (in awe of his host); evanescent like ice that is melting away; unpretentious like wood that has not been fashioned into anything; vacant like a valley, and dull like muddy water.

Who can (make) the muddy water (clear)?  Let it be still, and it will gradually become clear.  Who can secure the condition of rest?  Let movement go on, and the condition of rest will gradually arise.

They who preserve this method of the Tao do not wish to be full (of themselves).  It is through their not being full of themselves that they can afford to seem worn and not appear new and complete.

## Page 2

Chapter Fifteen says that those who truly understood the Dao in ancient times were deep, subtle, and hard to know from the outside.

Because they could not be fully seen, Laozi could only describe them with a string of images.

## Page 3

They were like people crossing a river in winter: careful, not reckless; like people alert to their surroundings: watchful, not arrogant; and like guests in another's home: grave and respectful.

They were also like ice about to melt: not rigid; like uncarved wood: thick and plain; like a valley: open enough to hold echoes; and like muddy water: not pretending to be bright and clear.

## Page 4

"Let muddy water be still and it will gradually become clear": silt sinks when water rests, and the heart slowly grows clear too. The line about rest and movement means that steadiness is not dead stillness. After true rest, action can slowly come alive, like seeds sprouting.

"Not wishing to be full" means not filling oneself up or hurrying to look complete, new, and impressive. Because they are not full, such people can last.

## Page 5

“Subtle and exquisite” means careful, far-seeing thinking that can put things together. “Shrinking” here means cautious. To “wade through a stream in winter” means crossing cold water carefully.

“Grave” means respectful and serious. “Unpretentious like wood that has not been fashioned into anything” describes plain, uncarved wood. “Muddy water” also describes someone who does not show off as pure or bright.

## Page 6

In winter, a thin skin of ice covered the stream in Kongkong Village.

Lin's paper kite had been blown to a willow branch on the other side, its red tail swinging in the cold wind.

## Page 7

Little Stone stamped his foot. "I'll run over and get it!" He had just stepped out when the ice made a thin cracking sound, and the old man reached out to stop him.

"A river is not the drying ground. There, running too fast may only bring a fall. On a winter stream, one wrong step may send cold water into your shoes."

## Page 8

The old man took a long bamboo pole and first tested the ice edge.

He did not step out at once. He looked at the color of the water, listened to the ice, and found the shallow places where stones showed. The children stood on the bank and felt for the first time that slowness had weight.

## Page 9

The old man walked step by step.

He was neither too frightened to move nor bold enough to rush. He placed the pole ahead, then moved his foot, as if discussing the crossing with the river.

## Page 10

When he reached the other side, he did not hurry to pull down the kite.

He first stood firmly, then reached out. The willow branch sprang, and snow grains fell on his shoulder. He smiled and untied the kite.

## Page 11

When he returned, Lin asked, "Were you afraid?"

The old man said, "A little fear is just right. With no fear at all, the feet make decisions for the head. With too much fear, one cannot cross."

## Page 12

Back indoors, the old man poured a cup of newly drawn well water. It was a little muddy, and fine sand turned inside. Little Stone wanted to stir it with chopsticks. "I'll help it clear faster."

The old man gently held his hand. "If you stir it, it becomes muddier. Let it sit." The children watched the sand slowly fall and the water brighten little by little.

## Page 13

Lin understood.

Crossing a winter stream takes time, and muddy water clearing takes time. Slowing down keeps hurry from spoiling the road.

## Page 14

That night, he laid the kite on the table.

The red tail was torn, and the bamboo frame was old. Lin did not hurry to make a completely new kite. He mended the tear with fine thread. The old kite could still fly the next day.

## Page 15

"Like those who wade through a stream in winter": one who understands water knows when to move quickly and when to slow down. "Let muddy water be still and it will gradually become clear": anger, hurry, and fear stir the heart like silt. Pause, and the matter grows clearer.

"Not wishing to be full" is like the old kite. A small tear can be mended, and an old thing can keep flying. A steady person crosses with care in the heart and measure under the feet. Going slower lets strength find the road.
