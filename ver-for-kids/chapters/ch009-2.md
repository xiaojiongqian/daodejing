# 儿童道德经第九章：其二 功成以后，手还抓着什么

## 第1页

有一个古老的比喻，说一位富人田地大丰收，谷子多到旧仓库放不下。

他站在仓门口，看着金黄的粮食，心里想：“我要拆掉旧仓库，盖更大的。把我的粮食、我的财物，全都放进去。”

## 第2页

他以为，仓库够大，就能把安心也锁在里面。可是故事说，就在那一夜，他的生命走到尽头，满仓的粮食一粒也没有跟他走。

收粮和勤劳都是好事。可东西满了，心还不知足，人就会把一生变成守仓库；守住了仓库，却守不住生命。

## 第3页

中国古代的范蠡帮助越王勾践打败吴国，越国上下都知道他立了大功。若留下来，他可以做大官，有车马，天天有人上门。

可他看见，功劳太满，君王和大臣之间就容易危险：能一起吃苦的人，不一定能一起分享功劳和名声。

## 第4页

于是范蠡离开朝廷，坐船走了，越走越远。

他还写信劝一起做官的朋友文种：飞鸟打完了，好弓就被收起来；兔子抓完了，猎狗也会有危险。事情做成后，要懂得退。

## 第5页

文种没有像他那样离开，后来果然遭祸。

这段故事有一点冷。功劳是一朵花，也可能是一根绳子。你抓着它，它也抓着你。

## 第6页

范蠡后来改名叫陶朱公，做生意又变得很富。他三次攒起很多财产，又三次把财产分给亲戚、朋友和邻居。

他能把事情做成，也能在做成以后离开；能把财富聚来，也能在财富变成重担前散开。这才叫知止。

## 第7页

古罗马的辛辛纳图斯正在田里扶犁，敌人围困罗马的消息已经到了门前。人们把很大的权力交给他，请他救急。

一句话可以调动军队，一道命令可以改变许多人的命运。许多人一坐上去，就不愿意下来。

## 第8页

辛辛纳图斯带兵解围，事情很快做成。

然后，他做了更难的一件事：把权力交回去，回到田里，继续扶他的犁。

## 第9页

两千多年后，华盛顿也遇到相似的考验。

他带领军队赢得独立战争。胜利以后，军队听他的话，许多人敬重他。他若想抓住权力，机会就在手边。

## 第10页

可是他主动交还军权。后来做了总统，也在两届之后离开位置，回到自己的农庄。

权力若不肯退，就会从保护大家的工具，慢慢变成压住大家的石头。

## 第11页

还有一个近代故事，像夜里的一声钟。

许多人把泰坦尼克号看成不会沉没的大船。冰山的警告传来，自信满到边上的人嫌提醒扫兴，船上的救生艇也不够。

## 第12页

船撞上冰山，沉进北大西洋，许多人没能回家。

“满”的危险，有时是骄傲让人看不见前面的冰。

## 第13页

“持而盈之，不如其已”，说太满就端不稳；太想拥有，也会被拥有牵住。

“金玉满堂，莫之能守”，提醒人财富越多，越要知足。“富贵而骄，自遗其咎”，则说骄傲会让人听不进劝告，忘了月满会缺。

## 第14页

“功遂身退，天之道”，说到了一个先后。

先要“功遂”：事要做成，责任要尽到。然后才是“身退”：事情已经能自己往前走，自己就别再压在它上面。

## 第15页

退，是把成果从“我的”手里放出来，让大家接着用下去。

会拿起，是本事；会放下，是更深的本事。将满就停，成以后退。亮起来以后，别挡在光前面，事情才能活得更久。

# Chapter 009-2: After Success, What Is the Hand Still Holding?

## Page 1

An old parable tells of a rich man whose fields produced so much grain that his old barns could not hold it.

He stood at the barn door, looking at the golden grain, and thought, "I will tear down the old barns and build bigger ones. I will store all my grain and all my goods there."

## Page 2

He thought that large enough barns could lock peace inside too. But the story says that very night his life ended, and not one grain from the full barns went with him.

Harvesting grain and working hard are good things. Yet when plenty still leaves the heart unsatisfied, life can become an endless watch over barns. The barns remain; life does not.

## Page 3

In ancient China, Fan Li helped King Goujian of Yue defeat Wu, and everyone in Yue knew of his great service. If he stayed, he could hold high office, ride in fine carriages, and receive visitors every day.

But he saw that too much credit could make the space between king and minister dangerous. People who share hardship do not always share success and fame well.

## Page 4

So Fan Li left the court and sailed farther and farther away.

He also wrote to his friend and fellow official Wen Zhong: when the birds are all shot, the good bow is put away; when the hares are all caught, the hunting dog is in danger. When the work is done, one must know how to step back.

## Page 5

Wen Zhong did not leave as Fan Li did, and later disaster came to him.

This part of the story is cold. Credit can be a flower, but it can also become a rope. When you hold it, it may hold you.

## Page 6

Fan Li later changed his name to Tao Zhu Gong and became rich again through trade. Three times he gathered great wealth, and three times he gave it to relatives, friends, and neighbors.

He could finish a great task and then leave. He could also gather wealth and scatter it before it became a burden. This is knowing where to stop.

## Page 7

In ancient Rome, Cincinnatus was guiding a plough when news reached him that enemies had surrounded Rome. The people gave him great power and asked him to save them.

One word from him could move an army; one order could change many lives. Many people, once given such a seat, do not wish to leave it.

## Page 8

Cincinnatus led the army and quickly relieved the danger.

Then he did the harder thing: he returned the power, went back to his field, and took up his plough again.

## Page 9

More than two thousand years later, George Washington met a similar test.

He led an army to win independence. After victory, the soldiers obeyed him and many people honored him. If he had wanted to hold on to power, the chance was there.

## Page 10

But he gave back his military power. Later, after serving two terms as president, he left office and returned to his farm.

Power that refuses to withdraw can slowly change from a tool that protects people into a stone that presses on them.

## Page 11

A more recent story sounds like a bell in the night.

Many people thought the Titanic could not sink. When ice warnings came, confidence made the reminders seem unwelcome, and the ship did not carry enough lifeboats.

## Page 12

The ship struck an iceberg and sank in the North Atlantic. Many people never came home.

Sometimes the danger of being too full is pride that keeps us from seeing the ice ahead.

## Page 13

To carry what is full to the brim is to risk unsteadiness; grasping at possessions also lets them pull us.

"When gold and jade fill the hall, their possessor cannot keep them safe" calls for contentment as wealth grows. "When wealth and honors lead to arrogance, this brings its evil on itself" warns that pride ignores advice and forgets that the full moon must wane.

## Page 14

"When the work is done, withdraw" gives an order to things.

First, the work must be completed and responsibility fulfilled. Then comes stepping back: when the work can move forward on its own, stop pressing yourself down on top of it.

## Page 15

Withdrawing releases the result from the hand of "mine," so others can carry it forward.

Picking something up takes ability; putting it down takes a deeper ability. Stop before fullness spills, and step back after success. Once the light is shining, move out of its way so the work can live longer.
