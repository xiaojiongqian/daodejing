# 儿童道德经第五章：其三 中道的翅膀和安静的善

## 第1页

希腊神话里，代达罗斯用蜡和羽毛做了两副翅膀，带儿子伊卡洛斯逃离迷宫。

起飞前，他叮嘱孩子：要走中间的路。

## 第2页

飞得太低，海水会打湿羽毛；飞得太高，太阳会融化蜡。

飞在中间，翅膀才保得住。这不是胆小，也不是没出息。

## 第3页

伊卡洛斯起初听父亲的话。后来他被飞翔迷住，越飞越高，像要把太阳抓在手里。

蜡化了，羽毛散开，他从高空坠入海中。

## 第4页

“守中”是知道自己的力气该往哪里走。

太兴奋、心里没有中心的时候，自由也会变成坠落。

## 第5页

古印度的悉达多也试过两种极端。他曾是王子，享过宫殿里的舒服日子。

后来他离开宫殿，又把自己逼进很苦很苦的苦行里。

## 第6页

吃得太少，身体虚弱，心也昏沉。享乐没有给他答案，折磨自己也没有给他答案。

他终于明白：琴弦太松没有声音，太紧又会断。

## 第7页

在菩提树下，他放下了追赶，也放下了逃开。

他静静看呼吸，看念头来了又走，让心像风箱一样空着，能动，也能停。

## 第8页

后来，人们把这条路叫作中道。它是放下抓得太紧的东西，让心回到能看清的地方。

它跟两边各让一步的凑合，不一样。

## 第9页

特蕾莎修女在加尔各答照顾那些没人管的人。有人感谢她，也有人怀疑她，可是贫穷和病痛每天都在她眼前。

她没有先问：这个人值不值得？也没有先证明自己多么善良。

## 第10页

她给人洗伤口，喂水，握住临终者的手。这些事很小，却一天一天做了很多年。

她心里没有被“我要显得仁慈”塞满，所以还能看见眼前这个人。这份仁慈没有挂成招牌。

## 第11页

老子说“圣人不仁”，是要提醒我们：别把善良变成偏心、做样子或者喊口号。它并不是叫我们不要善良。

真正的善守得住中心：不被称赞拉高，也不被怀疑压低；该照顾的时候，就照顾。

## 第12页

她有时把路边快死的人抱回临终之家。那人身上有伤，有气味，许多人绕开走。

她先给这个人洗净身体，盖上布，把他当一个人来对待。她不想这是不是一件了不起的大事。

## 第13页

正因为不把善良喊成口号，心里才有空，看见眼前的这一个人。

圣人不仁，是不被“我要证明我善良”牵走；守住中间，才不被赞美和怀疑耗干。

## 第14页

伊卡洛斯飞得太高太急，丢了中心。悉达多尝过享乐，也熬过苦日子，知道两边太过了，都会把人带偏。

特蕾莎的善良不吹自己，守住中间，做眼前该做的事。

## 第15页

第五章里的“中”，像飞行时合适的高度，像琴弦合适的松紧，也像帮助别人时不被名声和脾气拉偏的手。它是活的分寸。

它让力气走在正路上，不显摆，也不容易用光。

## 第16页

当心守住中间，话会少而清，行动会轻而准。

天地像风箱，空处生风。人要是留得住空，偏心就塞不进来，也能在乱糟糟的事情里走出一条路。

# Chapter 005-3: Wings of the Middle Way and Quiet Goodness

## Page 1

In Greek myth, Daedalus made two pairs of wings from wax and feathers. With them, he and his son Icarus could escape the maze.

Before they flew, he warned the boy: take the middle path.

## Page 2

Fly too low, and sea spray will wet the feathers. Fly too high, and the sun will melt the wax.

Flying in the middle is what keeps the wings safe. This is not cowardice, and it is no lack of spirit.

## Page 3

At first, Icarus followed his father. Then flight delighted him, and he rose higher and higher, as if he could seize the sun.

The wax softened, the feathers scattered, and he fell from the sky into the sea.

## Page 4

"Keeping the center" means knowing where your strength should go.

When you are too excited and the center is gone, freedom itself can turn into falling.

## Page 5

In ancient India, Siddhartha also tried both extremes. He had been a prince and had known the easy life of the palace.

Then he left the palace and pushed himself into severe hardship on purpose.

## Page 6

When he ate too little, his body weakened and his mind grew dim. Pleasure did not give the answer. Hurting himself did not give the answer either.

He came to understand: if a string is too loose, it makes no sound; if it is too tight, it breaks.

## Page 7

Under the Bodhi tree, he let go of chasing and let go of running away.

He quietly watched his breath and watched thoughts come and go. His heart became like a bellows: empty, able to move, and able to rest.

## Page 8

Later, people called this the Middle Way. It is letting go of what the hand grips too tightly, so the heart can return to a place where it sees clearly.

It is a different thing from a compromise that takes a little from each side.

## Page 9

Mother Teresa cared for people nobody wanted, in Kolkata. Some thanked her, and some doubted her, but poverty and sickness were before her every day.

She did not first ask, "Is this person worth helping?" She also did not first prove how good she was.

## Page 10

She washed wounds, gave water, and held the hands of people near death. These were small acts, done day after day for many years.

Her heart was not filled with "I must appear kind," so she could still see the person before her. Her kindness never became a signboard.

## Page 11

When Laozi says, "The sage is not benevolent," he warns us not to turn goodness into favorites, showing off, or slogans. He is not telling us to stop being good.

True goodness keeps the center: not lifted by praise, not pressed down by doubt, caring when care is needed.

## Page 12

Sometimes she carried a dying person from the roadside into a home for the dying. The body had wounds and smell, and many people walked around it.

She first washed this person, covered him, and treated him as a human being. She did not ask whether it was a great and important work.

## Page 13

Just because goodness is not shouted as a slogan, the heart has room to see this one person before it.

The sage is "not benevolent" in this sense: not pulled away by "I must prove I am kind." Keeping the middle, the heart is not drained by praise or doubt.

## Page 14

Icarus flew too high and too fast and lost the center. Siddhartha tasted pleasure and endured hard days, and learned that going too far on either side pulls a person off course.

Mother Teresa's goodness did not praise itself; it kept to the middle and did the one thing at hand.

## Page 15

The "center" in Chapter Five is a living measure. It is like the right height in flight, the right tightness in a string, and a helping hand not pulled away by fame or feelings.

It keeps strength on the right path, without showing off and without easily using itself up.

## Page 16

When the heart keeps the center, words become fewer and clearer, and actions become lighter and more exact.

Heaven and earth are like a bellows: wind is born in the hollow. If people can keep some hollow space, favorites cannot block them, and a path can open through a tangle of things.
