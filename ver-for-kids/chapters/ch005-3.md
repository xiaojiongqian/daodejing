# 第005-3章：中道的翅膀和安静的善

## 典故引入

### 第1页

希腊神话里，代达罗斯用蜡和羽毛做了两副翅膀，带儿子伊卡洛斯逃离迷宫。

起飞前，他叮嘱孩子：要走中间的路。

### 第2页

飞得太低，海水会打湿羽毛；飞得太高，太阳会融化蜡。

中间是让翅膀活下来的分寸，跟平庸和没胆量都不沾边。

### 第3页

伊卡洛斯起初听父亲的话。后来他被飞翔迷住，越飞越高，像要把太阳抓在手里。

蜡化了，羽毛散开，他从高空坠入海中。

### 第4页

“守中”是知道力量该走在哪条路上。

兴奋若没有中心，自由也会变成坠落。

### 第5页

古印度的悉达多也走过极端。他曾是王子，享受过宫殿里的安乐。

后来他离开宫殿，又把自己逼进很苦很苦的苦行。

### 第6页

吃得太少，身体虚弱，心也昏沉。享乐没有给他答案，折磨自己也没有给他答案。

他终于明白：琴弦太松没有声音，太紧又会断。

### 第7页

在菩提树下，他放下了追赶，也放下了逃开。

他静静看呼吸，看念头来去，让心像风箱一样空着，能动，也能停。

### 第8页

后来，人们把这条路叫作中道。它是离开执著，让心回到能看清的地方。

它跟两边各取一点的凑合不一样。

### 第9页

特蕾莎修女在加尔各答照顾被遗弃的人。有人感谢她，也有人怀疑她，可贫病每天都在眼前。

她没有先问：这个人值不值得？也没有先证明自己多么善良。

### 第10页

她给人洗伤口，喂水，握住临终者的手。这些事很小，却一天一天做了很多年。

她心里没有被“我要显得仁慈”塞满，所以还能看见眼前这个人。这份仁慈没有挂成招牌。

### 第11页

老子说“圣人不仁”，是要提醒我们：别把慈悲变成偏私、表演或口号。它并不是不要慈悲。

真正的善守得住中心：不被称赞拉高，也不被怀疑压低；该照顾的时候，就照顾。

### 第12页

她有时把路边快死的人抱回临终之家。那人身上有伤，有气味，许多人绕开走。

她先让这个人被洗净、被盖上布、被当作人，不问这是不是一件伟大的事业。

### 第13页

恰恰因为不把善变成口号，心里才有空看见眼前这一位。天地偏爱某一个人，是另一回事。

圣人不仁，是不被“我要证明我仁慈”牵走；守中，所以不被赞美和怀疑耗干。

## 回扣

### 第14页

伊卡洛斯飞得太高太急，丢了中心。悉达多尝过享乐，也熬过苦行，知道两边都会让心偏。

特蕾莎的善不把自己说满，守着中心，做眼前该做的事。

### 第15页

第五章里的“中”，像飞行时合适的高度，像琴弦合适的张力，也像帮助别人时不被名声和情绪拉偏的手。它是活的分寸。

它让力量走在正路上，不炫耀，也不轻易枯竭。

### 第16页

当心守住中间，话会少而清，行动会轻而准。

天地像风箱，空处生风。人若留得住空，偏私就塞不进来，也能在复杂世界里生出路。

# Chapter 005-3: Wings of the Middle Way and Quiet Goodness

## Allusion

### Page 1

In Greek myth, Daedalus made two pairs of wings from wax and feathers. With them, he and his son Icarus could escape the maze.

Before they flew, he warned the boy: take the middle path.

### Page 2

Fly too low, and sea spray will wet the feathers. Fly too high, and the sun will melt the wax.

The middle is the measure that lets the wings survive. It has nothing to do with being dull or cowardly.

### Page 3

At first, Icarus followed his father. Then flight delighted him, and he rose higher and higher, as if he could seize the sun.

The wax softened, the feathers scattered, and he fell from the sky into the sea.

### Page 4

"Keeping the center" is knowing where strength should travel.

When excitement has no center, freedom can turn into falling.

### Page 5

In ancient India, Siddhartha also walked through extremes. He had been a prince and had known palace comfort.

Then he left the palace and pushed himself into severe hardship.

### Page 6

When he ate too little, his body weakened and his mind grew dim. Pleasure did not give the answer. Hurting himself did not give the answer either.

He came to understand: if a string is too loose, it makes no sound; if it is too tight, it breaks.

### Page 7

Under the Bodhi tree, he let go of chasing and let go of running away.

He quietly watched his breath and watched thoughts come and go. His heart became like a bellows: empty, able to move, and able to rest.

### Page 8

Later, people called this the Middle Way. It is leaving attachment, so the heart can return to a place that sees clearly.

It is a different thing from a compromise that takes a little from each side.

### Page 9

Mother Teresa cared for abandoned people in Kolkata. Some thanked her, and some doubted her, but sickness and poverty were before her every day.

She did not first ask, "Is this person worth helping?" She also did not first prove how good she was.

### Page 10

She washed wounds, gave water, and held the hands of people near death. These were small acts, done day after day for many years.

Her heart was not filled with "I must appear kind," so she could still see the person before her. Her kindness never became a signboard.

### Page 11

When Laozi says, "The sage is not benevolent," he warns us not to turn compassion into favoritism, performance, or slogans. He is not rejecting compassion.

True goodness keeps the center: not lifted by praise, not pressed down by doubt, caring when care is needed.

### Page 12

Sometimes she carried a dying person from the roadside into a home for the dying. The body had wounds and smell, and many people walked around it.

She first made sure this person was washed, covered, and treated as human, without asking whether this was a great mission.

### Page 13

Precisely because goodness is not turned into a slogan, there is room in the heart to see this person before us. Heaven and earth favoring one person is another matter.

The sage is "not benevolent" in this sense: not pulled by "I must prove I am kind." Keeping the center, the heart is not drained by praise or doubt.

## Return to the Original

### Page 14

Icarus flew too high and too fast and lost the center. Siddhartha tasted pleasure and endured self-torment, and learned that both pull the heart to one side.

Mother Teresa's goodness did not fill itself with talk; it kept to the center and did the present thing.

### Page 15

The "center" in Chapter Five is a living measure. It is like the right height in flight, the right tension in a string, and a helping hand not pulled away by fame or emotion.

It keeps strength on the right path, without showing off and without wearing out.

### Page 16

When the heart keeps the center, words become fewer and clearer, and actions become lighter and more exact.

Heaven and earth are like a bellows: wind is born in the hollow. If people can keep some hollow space, favoritism cannot block them, and a path can open in a complicated world.
