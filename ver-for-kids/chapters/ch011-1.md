# 儿童道德经第十一章：其一 空出来，才用得上

## 第1页

三十辐共一毂，当其无，有车之用。
埏埴以为器，当其无，有器之用。

凿户牖以为室，当其无，有室之用。
故有之以为利，无之以为用。

## 第2页

第十一章专门夸“空”。车轮有木头、辐条和外圈，中心留着洞，车轴穿过去，车轮才转得起来。

陶土能捏成碗、罐、缸。罐子若是实心的，花纹再好看也装不了水；肚子里空着，才装得下东西。

## 第3页

房子有墙、梁和屋顶，屋里的空处让人住下，门窗让光和风进来。

“有之以为利”，是木头、泥土、墙壁带来便利；“无之以为用”，是空洞、空肚、空屋让这些便利真正派上用场。

## 第4页

“三十辐”指的是古代车轮上的许多辐条，它们从外圈伸向中心。

“毂”指的是车轮中心的部分，里面有孔，车轴从这里穿过。

“当其无”的意思是正因为那里空着。

## 第5页

“埏埴”的意思是揉泥、捏泥，用泥土做器物。

“户牖”指的是门和窗。

这里的“利”指的是能带来方便的形体。

这里的“用”指的是真正发生作用的地方。

## 第6页

秋收前，空空村要修一辆旧木车，好拉稻束、南瓜和一筐筐豆荚。晒谷场边，两个车轮都歪着。

阿林看见车轮中间的黑洞，皱起眉头：“洞就是缺口。缺口不补，车轮一定不结实。”

## 第7页

他找来一块木头，削成圆塞子，塞进车轮中心。

小石头还帮他往缝里抹泥。两个孩子拍着手说：“这下实在了！”

## 第8页

牛被套上车，老人轻轻一喊，牛往前走。绳子绷紧了，车轮却像生了根。

阿林急得去推，车身一晃，空竹筐咚咚滚了一地。小石头摸摸后脑勺：“结实是结实，可是不会转了。”

## 第9页

修车的老木匠把圆塞子拔出来，露出原来的孔。

他把车轴穿进去，车轮轻轻一推，就转了半圈。老木匠说：“车轮中心若不空，车轴从哪里过？”

## 第10页

阿林还有点不服气。下午，他跟陶匠学捏罐子，把泥团拍得厚厚的，还在外面按了几朵花。

陶匠舀水往里倒，水一碰罐口就溅了出来。阿林这才发现，自己捏的是个实心泥疙瘩，外头像罐子，里面却没有肚子。

## 第11页

陶匠把泥重新揉软，手指按进去，慢慢转，慢慢提。

泥壁变薄，肚子空出来。一只不那么花哨的罐子站在桌上，却能稳稳接住半瓢水。

## 第12页

傍晚起风，孩子们把工具搬进新修的小屋。

这间屋子墙很厚，门也做得紧。可大家刚进去，就觉得闷。油灯烟子散不开，眼睛被熏得直眨。

## 第13页

老人拿来凿子，在墙上开了一扇小窗。

风先进来，烟先出去，接着一条晚光落在地上。屋子还是那间屋子，却像醒了过来。

## 第14页

阿林站在门口，想起车轮的洞、陶罐的肚子、小屋的窗。空不是破，也不一定是少；它让车转、让罐盛水、让屋子住人。

车轴穿过空孔，泥壁围住空肚，墙和门窗一起，才有车、罐和屋的用处。“有”和“无”，一直在一块儿做事。

## 第15页

一颗心也要这样。

全塞满了自己的想法，就听不进别人；留一点空，新的风才进得来。

# Chapter 011-1: Empty, So It Can Be Used

## Page 1

The thirty spokes unite in the one nave; but it is on the empty space (for the axle), that the use of the wheel depends.  Clay is fashioned into vessels; but it is on their empty hollowness, that their use depends.

The door and windows are cut out (from the walls) to form an apartment; but it is on the empty space (within), that its use depends.  Therefore, what has a (positive) existence serves for profitable adaptation, and what has not that for (actual) usefulness.

## Page 2

Chapter Eleven praises emptiness. A wheel has wood, spokes, and an outer rim, yet its center holds an opening. The axle passes through it, and the wheel can turn.

Clay can be shaped into bowls, jars, and vats. If a jar is solid inside, even beautiful patterns cannot make it hold water; its empty belly gives it room to hold things.

## Page 3

A house has walls, beams, and a roof. Its open rooms give people space to live, and its doors and windows admit light and air.

"What has existence" means the wood, clay, and walls that help us. "What has not" means the hollow, empty belly, and open room that let those visible things truly work.

## Page 4

The word “spokes” means the rods of a wheel that reach from the rim toward the center.

The word “nave” means the center part of a wheel, where the axle passes through.

The phrase “empty space” means a space left open so something can move, enter, or be held.

## Page 5

The word “vessel” means a bowl, jar, or pot that can hold something.

Here, the word “apartment” means a room or dwelling space.

The phrase “profitable adaptation” means the helpful shape or material of a thing.

The word “usefulness” means the real working power of the thing.

## Page 6

Before the autumn harvest, Kongkong Village had to repair an old wooden cart to carry rice bundles, pumpkins, and baskets of beans. Beside the drying ground, both wheels tilted.

Lin saw the dark hole in the center of a wheel and frowned. "A hole is a gap," he said. "If we do not fill it, the wheel will not be strong."

## Page 7

He found a piece of wood, carved it into a round plug, and pushed it into the center of the wheel.

Little Stone helped smear mud around the cracks. The two children clapped. "Now it is solid!"

## Page 8

The ox was harnessed to the cart, and the old man called softly. The ox stepped forward, the rope tightened, but the wheel stayed rooted to the ground.

Lin hurried over and pushed. The cart shook, and the empty bamboo baskets rolled out with a hollow clatter. Little Stone rubbed his head. "It is solid, but it cannot turn."

## Page 9

The old wheelwright pulled out the round plug and uncovered the original hole.

He slid the axle through it. With one light push, the wheel turned half a circle. "If the wheel's center is not empty," he said, "where can the axle pass?"

## Page 10

Lin was still not fully convinced. That afternoon, he learned to make a jar with the potter. He patted the clay thick and firm and pressed little flowers on the outside.

The potter poured water toward it, and the water splashed off the mouth. Only then did Lin see that he had made a solid lump of clay. It looked like a jar outside, but had no belly inside.

## Page 11

The potter softened the clay again, pressed his fingers into it, turned it slowly, and lifted it slowly.

The wall grew thinner, and the belly opened. A less fancy jar stood on the table, yet it could steadily receive half a ladle of water.

## Page 12

At dusk, the children carried tools into a newly repaired shed.

The walls were thick, and the door shut tight. As soon as everyone went inside, the room felt stuffy. Lamp smoke could not leave, and their eyes began to blink.

## Page 13

The old man brought a chisel and opened a small window in the wall.

Wind entered, smoke went out, and a stripe of evening light fell on the floor. The shed was still the same shed, but it seemed to wake up.

## Page 14

Lin stood at the doorway and thought of the wheel's hole, the jar's belly, and the shed's window. Emptiness is not always damage or lack; it lets the wheel turn, the jar hold water, and the house shelter people.

The axle needs the hole, the clay walls surround the hollow, and walls work with doors and windows. What exists and what is empty work together.

## Page 15

A heart needs room too.

When it is packed full of its own thoughts, it cannot hear others. Leave a little space, and a fresh wind can enter.
