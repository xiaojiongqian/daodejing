# 儿童道德经第十三章：其一 被夸和被笑，都别把心交出去

## 第1页

宠辱若惊，贵大患若身。

何谓宠辱若惊？
宠为下，得之若惊，失之若惊，是谓宠辱若惊。

何谓贵大患若身？
吾所以有大患者，为吾有身，及吾无身，吾有何患？

故贵以身为天下，若可寄天下；
爱以身为天下，若可托天下。

## 第2页

第十三章讲被宠爱和被羞辱。得到宠爱怕失去，失去宠爱怕被看轻，心就被外面的眼光牵来牵去。“宠为下”就是这样一个又低又不稳的位置。

“贵大患若身”，是把大的忧患看得像身体一样真实。有身体，就会饿、会累、会疼、会怕失去；把“我”抓得太紧，也容易被得失惊动。

## 第3页

老子又说，珍惜自身的人，才可以把天下托付给他。

把“我”看得太重，会处处受惊；知道生命珍贵，又不把名声和角色当成自己，才能稳下来，也不会为了掌声和面子伤害自己和别人。

## 第4页

“宠”是被喜欢、被重视；“辱”是被轻看、被羞辱。“若惊”是像受惊一样，心乱了。

“大患”是很大的忧患或危险。“有身”既指身体，也指把“我”抓得太紧。“寄天下”“托天下”，都是把天下托付给一个人照看。

## 第5页

灯会那天，空空村要排小鼓舞。老人把红绸带递给阿林：“你来领第一声鼓，大家听你的鼓点起步。”

小石头拍手：“阿林当领鼓人！”孩子们围过来看红绸带，阿林觉得胸口像塞进一只会跳的小鸟。

## 第6页

练习时，阿林把鼓槌举得很高。

别人慢一点，他就皱眉；别人敲错一下，他就叹气。他嘴上说“别出错”，心里却在想：“大家都在看我。”

## 第7页

晚上，灯笼亮了。阿林看见台下那么多脸，第一声鼓打偏了，“啪”地散开，队伍也跟着乱了一下。

小石头赶紧接上一声，把大家带回来。台下两个小孩笑出声，阿林的脸烧得比灯笼还红。

## 第8页

鼓舞结束后，有人夸小石头反应快，也有人说：“阿林今天怎么慌了？”

这句话说得轻，却像小刺扎进阿林心里。他把红绸带塞进袖子，连饭也不想吃。

## 第9页

老人坐到他旁边：“早上大家夸你时，你也吃不下饭吗？”

阿林想了想：“那时太高兴，忘了饿。”老人说：“夸你，肚子被带走；笑你，肚子也被带走。一条红绸带，真有这么大力气？”

## 第10页

阿林低头看那条红绸带。

它只是一条布。真正把他抬高又摔低的，是他自己把心交给了别人眼里的“领鼓人”。

## 第11页

老人把一碗热饭推到他面前。

“先吃饭。身体饿了，别让面子替它做主。一个连自己这碗饭都照看不好的人，怎么照看一队人的鼓点？”

## 第12页

阿林慢慢吃完饭，又去找小石头。

他说：“谢谢你刚才接住鼓点。”小石头笑了：“你明天也会接住我的。”

## 第13页

第二天，孩子们再练鼓。

阿林还是认真，却不再把每一道目光都当成判决。有人敲错，他先稳住鼓点，再陪他慢慢跟上。

## 第14页

第十三章说“宠辱若惊”。被夸时心飞起来，被笑时心摔下去，都让自己离开脚下。

“得之若惊，失之若惊”，会出现在大人做官、得奖、失势的时候。孩子也会这样：被老师表扬，就怕下次不够好；被朋友笑一下，就觉得自己全坏了。

## 第15页

“贵以身为天下”，先从珍惜身体开始。该吃饭时吃饭，该睡觉时睡觉，该认错时认错，身心稳一点，才不容易被宠辱牵走。

红绸带可以戴在身上，别戴进心里。别人夸你，好好做事；别人笑你，也好好做事。鼓点要稳，心也要稳。

# Chapter 013-1: Do Not Hand Your Heart to Praise or Laughter

## Page 1

Favour and disgrace would seem equally to be feared; honour and great calamity, to be regarded as personal conditions (of the same kind).

What is meant by speaking thus of favour and disgrace?  Disgrace is being in a low position (after the enjoyment of favour).  The getting that (favour) leads to the apprehension (of losing it), and the losing it leads to the fear of (still greater calamity):--this is what is meant by saying that favour and disgrace would seem equally to be feared.

And what is meant by saying that honour and great calamity are to be (similarly) regarded as personal conditions?  What makes me liable to great calamity is my having the body (which I call myself); if I had not the body, what great calamity could come to me?

Therefore he who would administer the kingdom, honouring it as he honours his own person, may be employed to govern it, and he who would administer it with the love which he bears to his own person may be entrusted with it.

## Page 2

Chapter Thirteen speaks of being favored and disgraced. We fear losing favor when it comes, and fear being scorned when it leaves. Outside opinions pull the heart back and forth. "Favor is low" points to this low, unsteady place.

"Great calamity as the body" means treating great trouble as real and close as one's own body. We hunger, tire, hurt, and fear loss. Gripping the idea of "me" too tightly also lets gain and loss startle us.

## Page 3

Laozi also says that one who cherishes his own life may be entrusted with the world.

Taking the self too heavily brings fear at every turn. Knowing life is precious, without mistaking fame and roles for the self, brings steadiness. Such a person will not hurt himself or others for applause or face.

## Page 4

“Favour” means being liked or honored; “disgrace” means being scorned or shamed. Here, “feared” means being startled and made restless.

“Calamity” means great trouble or danger. “Having the body” means having a body and also holding too tightly to “me.” “Entrusted” means being given something important to care for.

## Page 5

On the lantern festival night, Kongkong Village planned a drum dance. The old man handed Lin a red silk band. "You will lead the first beat. Everyone will begin by listening to you."

Little Stone clapped. "Lin is the lead drummer!" The children gathered around the red band, and Lin felt as if a hopping bird had been placed in his chest.

## Page 6

During practice, Lin lifted his drumstick very high.

If someone was slow, he frowned. If someone missed a beat, he sighed. He said, "Do not make mistakes," but inside he thought, "Everyone is watching me."

## Page 7

That night, the lanterns lit. Lin saw all the faces below and struck the first beat off-center. It cracked and scattered, and the line faltered.

Little Stone quickly caught the rhythm and brought everyone back. Two children below laughed. Lin's face burned redder than the lanterns.

## Page 8

After the dance, someone praised Little Stone's quick response. Someone else said, "Why did Lin panic today?"

The words were light, yet they pricked Lin's heart. He stuffed the red band into his sleeve and did not want supper.

## Page 9

The old man sat beside him. "This morning, when everyone praised you, did you lose your appetite too?"

Lin thought. "I was so happy I forgot I was hungry." The old man said, "Praise made you forget your belly, and laughter took away your appetite. Does a strip of red silk really have that much power?"

## Page 10

Lin looked down at the red band.

It was only cloth. What had lifted and dropped him was his own heart, handed to the "lead drummer" in other people's eyes.

## Page 11

The old man pushed a bowl of warm rice toward him.

"Eat first. Your body is hungry. Do not let face make its decisions. If you cannot care for this bowl of rice, how can you care for a line of drumbeats?"

## Page 12

Lin slowly finished the rice and went to find Little Stone.

"Thank you for catching the beat," he said. Little Stone smiled. "Tomorrow you will catch mine."

## Page 13

The next day, the children practiced again.

Lin was still serious, but he no longer treated every glance as a judgment. When someone missed a beat, he steadied the rhythm first, then helped the child follow it.

## Page 14

Chapter Thirteen says that favor and disgrace can both startle. Praise made Lin's heart fly, and laughter made it fall. Both carried him away from the ground beneath his feet.

"Getting it is frightening, losing it is frightening" plays out when adults gain office or awards, or lose power. Children know it too. A teacher's praise can make us fear failing next time. A friend's laughter can make us feel entirely bad.

## Page 15

"Honoring the body as the world" begins with cherishing one's own body. Eat when it is time to eat, sleep when it is time to sleep, and admit a mistake when it is time. A steadier body and heart are harder for favor and disgrace to drag away.

The red band may be worn on the body, but keep it out of the heart. If others praise you, do the work well. If others laugh at you, still do the work well. Keep the drumbeat steady, and the heart steady too.
