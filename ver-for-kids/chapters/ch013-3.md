# 儿童道德经第十三章：其三 脚落到地上

## 第1页

尼泊尔加德满都谷地有一种传统：人们会从小女孩中选出“库玛丽”，尊她为活女神。

这是别处的文化，讲起它要带着尊重。我们借这个故事看一件事：当一个人被放到很高的位置，怎样不让心被位置带走？

## 第2页

被选中的女孩还很小。

她穿红衣，坐在宫殿的窗前。节日到来时，街上挤满人，花落在台阶上，鼓声一阵一阵涌上来。

## 第3页

人们抬头看她。

有人合掌，有人低头，有人把愿望轻轻说出来。一个孩子被这么多人仰望，很容易觉得自己真的住在云上。

## 第4页

云上的日子，也有安静得发空的时候。

她不能跑出巷子，把鞋踩进雨后的泥水，也不能大笑到肚子疼。她坐得很直，连表情也要小心。

## 第5页

有一天，远处传来学校的铃声，从窗缝里钻进来。

她看见一群孩子背着书包经过，有人边走边啃饼，有人把铅笔夹在耳朵后面。

## 第6页

这些平常的事，在她眼前亮了起来。

她低头看自己的脚。那双脚被照顾得很好，却很少自己踩在石板路上。

## 第7页

库玛丽的身份不会持续一生。

到了一定时候，她要从高座上下来。红衣会收起，窗前会有下一位女孩，人们的目光也会转向新的座位。

## 第8页

那一天，她第一次以普通学生的身份走进学校。

门口没有人跪拜。老师叫她的名字，同学递给她一张纸：“这里可以坐。”

## 第9页

她伸手接纸，手心微微出汗。

鼓声和花都留在节日里，身边也没人提醒她坐得像神。她听见自己的呼吸，也听见鞋底轻轻碰到地面。

## 第10页

如果她把“女神”当成全部的自己，退位就像从高处掉下来。

若她知道那是一段尊贵而临时的角色，走下来虽然会痛，她依然还是自己。

## 第11页

下课时，操场上有人跑。

她先站着看。后来，一个女孩回头喊：“来呀！”她犹豫了一下，迈出第一步。

## 第12页

脚踩到土地，灰尘轻轻起来。

她跑得不快，裙角也有点乱，却笑了起来。这笑不用向谁解释，也不必像节日里那样端正。

## 第13页

老人讲到这里，小石头低声问：“从那么高的地方下来，会不会很难过？”

“会。被人仰望，心里会亮；没人仰望，心里会空。所以得宠会惊，失宠也会惊。”阿林想起红绸带，他只当一晚领鼓人，心就上上下下；她从小被当作神女，回到自己更不容易。

## 第14页

老人说：“坐在高处时，别忘自己有脚；走下来时，也别以为只剩下空。”高座会换人，脚下的地面一直在。

阿林低头看自己的脚。它们沾过泥，踢过石子，也因跑太快摔疼过。它们很普通，却能带他回家。

## 第15页

老子知道心会惊。“得之若惊，失之若惊”，因为人常把临时的角色当成自己，而角色会来，也会走。

“贵以身为天下”看重真实的生命，比称号更根本。吃饭、写字、走路、奔跑、呼吸，这些平常事把人接住。脚落到地上，心也落到地上；被看见时不飞走，不被看见时也不散掉。

# Chapter 013-3: Feet Touching the Ground

## Page 1

In Nepal's Kathmandu Valley, there is a tradition of choosing a young girl as Kumari, honoring her as a living goddess.

This is another culture, and we should speak of it with respect. The story lets us ask: when someone is placed very high, how can she keep her heart from being carried away by that position?

## Page 2

The chosen girl is still very young.

She wears red clothes and sits at a palace window. During festivals, the street fills with people, flowers fall on the steps, and drums rise in waves.

## Page 3

People look up at her.

Some join their hands, some bow their heads, and some whisper wishes. When so many people look up to a child, she may easily feel that she truly lives among clouds.

## Page 4

Life among clouds can also feel quiet and empty.

She cannot run through the alleys or splash through puddles. She cannot even laugh until her stomach hurts. She sits very straight, and even her face must be careful.

## Page 5

One day, a distant school bell slipped through the window.

She saw children passing with schoolbags. One ate a flatbread while walking; another held a pencil behind one ear.

## Page 6

These ordinary things began to shine before her.

She looked down at her feet. They had been cared for carefully, yet they had seldom stepped by themselves on the stone road.

## Page 7

The role of Kumari does not last a whole life.

At a certain time, she must come down from the high seat. The red clothes are put away, another girl sits at the window, and people's eyes turn toward the new seat.

## Page 8

On that day, she enters school as an ordinary student for the first time.

No one kneels at the door. The teacher calls her name. A classmate hands her a sheet of paper. "You can sit here."

## Page 9

She reaches for the paper, and her palm is damp.

The drums and flowers belong to the festival now, and no one reminds her to sit like a goddess. She hears her own breath and the soft touch of her shoes on the floor.

## Page 10

If she has taken "goddess" as her whole self, stepping down feels like falling from a height.

If she knows it was a noble and temporary role, stepping down may hurt, yet she is still herself.

## Page 11

At recess, children run on the playground.

At first she watches. Then a girl turns back and calls, "Come!" She hesitates, then takes her first step.

## Page 12

Her feet touch the earth, and dust rises softly.

She does not run fast, and her skirt is a little messy, but she laughs. This laugh needs no explanation and does not have to be solemn like a festival.

## Page 13

When the old man reached this part, Little Stone asked softly, "Would it hurt to come down from so high?"

"It would. Being looked up to can make the heart shine; being looked up to no longer can leave it empty. So gaining favor startles, and losing favor startles too." Lin remembered his red band. He had been lead drummer for only one night, and his heart had gone up and down. For someone treated as a goddess since childhood, returning to herself would be harder still.

## Page 14

The old man said, "When you sit high, remember that you have feet. When you come down, do not believe you are left with nothing." The high seat would pass to another girl. The ground remained beneath her feet.

Lin looked down at his own feet. They had been muddy, had kicked stones, and had hurt after running too fast. They were ordinary, but they could bring him home.

## Page 15

Laozi knew the heart could be startled. "Getting it is frightening, losing it is frightening," because we often mistake a temporary role for the self. Roles come, and roles go.

"Honoring the body as the world" honors real life, more basic than any title. Eating, writing, walking, running, and breathing hold a person steady. Let the feet and heart touch the ground. Being seen need not carry us away, and being unseen need not make us fall apart.
