# 儿童道德经第八章：其一 水往低处去

## 第1页

上善若水。
水善利万物而不争，处众人之所恶，故几于道。

居善地，心善渊，与善仁，言善信，正善治，事善能，动善时。
夫唯不争，故无尤。

## 第2页

第八章说，最高的善，像水。

水好在哪里？它能让许多生命继续活下去，却不把功劳挂在自己身上。

## 第3页

河上公说，水在天上是雾露，在地上是泉源。草木靠水生长，人也靠水而活。

可是水常去低处、湿处、浑处。大家嫌那里脏、嫌那里不好看，水却不嫌。

## 第4页

这不是水“没出息”。低处能接住水，水也能接住低处的需要。

所以“处众人之所恶”，是不把好看、显眼、被夸放在最前面。它也不是故意去找苦吃。

## 第5页

“居善地”，是待在合适的位置；“心善渊”，是心里有深处，不被一点响动搅乱。

“与善仁”，是给出去，像水浇灌东西；“言善信”，是说话像清水照影，不把真相照歪。

## 第6页

“正善治”，是把乱处慢慢洗平；“事善能”，是方杯里成方，圆碗里成圆，事情需要怎样就怎样做。

“动善时”，是该流时流，该停时停。冬天结冰，春天化开，水不和时节硬拧。

## 第7页

“上善”是很高的善，让生命真正受益。“几于道”是很接近道，却仍留着一点距离。

“善利万物”是帮助许多生命。“处众人之所恶”是待在别人嫌低、嫌湿、嫌脏的地方。

“善渊”是像深水潭一样安静、有深度。“无尤”是少有人埋怨；不争功、不抢位，麻烦也少。

## 第8页

雨停以后，空空村后山多了一股水。

它从石缝里钻出来，先绕过一块青石，又往坡下的泥洼里去。泥洼低低的，积着落叶，还有一点不好闻的烂草味。

## 第9页

小石头捂住鼻子：“它为什么偏要去那里？那边又湿又脏。”

阿林看见村口新修的石阶，心里一亮：“不如把水引过去。水从石阶旁边流，一定很漂亮。”

## 第10页

孩子们搬来小石子，排出一条直直的水道。

水被拦住，先停了一会儿。后面的水继续来，前面的水没地方走，就在石子后面慢慢涨高。

## 第11页

“再堵紧一点！”小石头喊。他们塞住缝隙，水被挤进直道，没走几步就漫出来，冲散了刚摆好的泥炉子。

阿林拿树枝去拨：“这边，这边！”水却钻进草根，绕到石头背后。

## 第12页

老人走过来问：“你们想让水好看，还是想让水有用？”高处的石阶干干净净，花已喝足雨；坡下泥洼里的菜苗却蔫着叶子。

他拨开烂草，露出细根：“低处不是没人要的地方，只是大家常常不愿意低头看。”

## 第13页

阿林把堵住的石子一颗一颗移开，又顺着坡势挖了一条浅浅的小沟。

水先浑了一阵，带着泥沙往下走。等沙沉住，清水就贴着菜苗根边慢慢流。

## 第14页

小石头蹲下来，看见小虫从湿土里爬出来，菜叶一点一点挺起。“它不是怕高，它是知道哪里渴。”

傍晚，水流到村口，洗过孩子们踩脏的木屐，又流进菜畦旁的小沟。没人给它挂“今天辛苦了”的牌子，晚饭桌上却多了一把水灵灵的青菜。

## 第15页

第八章说“水善利万物而不争”。水没有留在石阶旁装点风景，顺着地势来到菜苗身边。

它看见需要，把自己放低，也把生命扶起来。

# Chapter 008-1: Water Goes Low

## Page 1

The highest excellence is like (that of) water.  The excellence of water appears in its benefiting all things, and in its occupying, without striving (to the contrary), the low place which all men dislike.  Hence (its way) is near to (that of) the Tao.

The excellence of a residence is in (the suitability of) the place; that of the mind is in abysmal stillness; that of associations is in their being with the virtuous; that of government is in its securing good order; that of (the conduct of) affairs is in its ability; and that of (the initiation of) any movement is in its timeliness.

And when (one with the highest excellence) does not wrangle (about his low position), no one finds fault with him.

## Page 2

Chapter Eight says that the highest goodness is like water.

What makes water so good? It lets many lives continue, without claiming the credit for itself.

## Page 3

Heshang Gong says water becomes mist and dew in the sky, and springs on the earth. Plants live by water, and people live by water too.

Yet water often goes to low, wet, muddy places. People may dislike those places because they are dirty or plain, but water does not.

## Page 4

Water going low is not a failure. Low places receive water, and water meets the needs there.

So occupying the place others dislike means not placing beauty, attention, and praise first. It is not about looking for hardship either.

## Page 5

"A good residence" means being in the fitting place. "A good mind" is deep, like a pool not disturbed by every noise.

"Good giving" nourishes like water. "Good words" are trustworthy, like clear water that does not bend the truth of a reflection.

## Page 6

"Good rule" slowly washes disorder into order. "Good work" can become square in a square cup and round in a round bowl, doing what the matter needs.

"Good movement" moves at the right time. Water freezes in winter and melts in spring. It does not argue with the season.

## Page 7

The phrase “highest excellence” means very high goodness. It truly helps life, whether or not anyone is watching.

The phrase “benefiting all things” means helping many living things, not only the one we happen to like. The phrase “low place” means a place others may avoid, but where help can be received.

The phrase “abysmal stillness” means deep stillness, like a quiet pool. The word “timeliness” means doing something at the fitting time. The word “wrangle” means to argue and fight over position or credit.

## Page 8

After the rain, a thread of water appeared behind Kongkong Village.

It slipped out of a crack in the stone, curved around a green rock, and headed toward a muddy hollow. The hollow was low, full of fallen leaves and a sour smell of rotting grass.

## Page 9

Little Stone covered his nose. "Why does it have to go there? That place is wet and dirty."

Lin saw the new stone steps at the village gate and had an idea. "Let's guide the water there. It will look beautiful flowing beside the steps."

## Page 10

The children carried small stones and laid out a straight channel.

The stones stopped the flow for a moment. More water came from behind, and with nowhere to go, it slowly rose behind them.

## Page 11

"Block it tighter!" Little Stone called. They closed the gaps, and the water was squeezed into the straight channel. After a few steps, it spilled over the side and washed apart the small mud stove they had just made.

Lin poked at the stream with a branch. "This way!" But the water slipped into grass roots and curved behind a stone.

## Page 12

The old man came over and asked, "Do you want the water to be pretty, or to be useful?" The high stone steps were clean, and the flowers had drunk enough rain. Down in the hollow, the vegetable shoots hung their leaves.

He lifted away the rotting grass and showed the thin roots. "A low place is not useless. People just rarely look down and notice it."

## Page 13

Lin moved the blocking stones away one by one, then dug a shallow ditch along the slope.

The water ran muddy for a while, carrying silt downward. As the sand settled, clear water flowed beside the roots of the shoots.

## Page 14

Little Stone crouched and saw an insect crawl out of the damp soil and the vegetable leaves slowly lift. "It wasn't afraid of being high," he said. "It knew where the thirst was."

By evening, the water reached the village gate, washed the children's muddy wooden clogs, and flowed into the ditch beside the vegetable beds. No one hung a sign saying, "You worked hard today," but a bunch of fresh green vegetables appeared on the supper table.

## Page 15

Chapter Eight says water benefits all things and does not strive. The stream did not fight for the beautiful place beside the steps; it followed the slope to the shoots.

It saw their need, lowered itself, and helped life stand up.
